**Specific Instructions on Substantive Issues**

Please address each of the following carefully:

**i. Definition of Confidential Information — Exceptions/Carve-Outs**

- Corrigan's proposed carve-out for information "generally available within the data analytics industry" — **reject**. This is far too broad and would effectively gut the protections for a significant portion of the information we expect to receive.
- Corrigan's proposed carve-out for information independently developed "without reference to" Confidential Information — **accept**. This is standard.
- Our original exception for information "publicly available through no fault of Receiving Party" — **retain as drafted**.

**ii. Definition of Representatives**

- Corrigan proposes adding affiliates and their respective personnel to the definition of Representatives. Per the playbook, this is acceptable **only if** we add breach-responsibility language — i.e., the Receiving Party remains responsible for any breach by its affiliates and their personnel as if such breach were a breach by the Receiving Party itself. Accept with that condition; draft the necessary addition.

**iii. Permitted Purpose**

- Our original language: "Evaluating a potential strategic transaction between the parties."
- Corrigan's proposed language: "evaluating a potential acquisition by Velantis of the Data Analytics Division of Corrigan."
- Note that Corrigan DAD is an unincorporated division, not a separate legal entity. Please evaluate whether Corrigan's proposed language creates any structural or interpretive issues. If so, propose alternative language that accurately reflects the contemplated transaction (acquisition of a division/assets) without being overly specific or creating unintended implications. Use your judgment here, but flag for me if you see a meaningful concern.

**iv. Confidentiality Term**

- Corrigan proposes **3 years**. Per the playbook, this is a hard line: the confidentiality term **must not exceed 2 years** from the date of the agreement. Reject the 3-year proposal and revert to 2 years.

**v. Residuals Clause**

- **Flag for careful attention.** The residuals clause is a **must-have** per the playbook and must be retained.
- I understand Corrigan's concerns — customer lists, proprietary algorithms, employee compensation data — and we should acknowledge those concerns constructively.
- Acceptable compromise: propose a qualified version under which the residuals clause does not apply to (a) trade secrets or (b) information specifically marked "Highly Confidential — No Residuals" by the Disclosing Party. This gives Corrigan a meaningful protective mechanism while preserving the clause. Draft accordingly.

**vi. Standard of Care**

- Corrigan proposes "the highest degree of care" — **reject**. This is above market and not something we can accept.
- Revert to our original standard: "reasonable care, and in no event less than the same degree of care the Receiving Party uses to protect its own confidential information of a similar nature."

**vii. Compelled Disclosure — Cost Allocation**

- Corrigan proposes that the Disclosing Party bear all costs of seeking a protective order — **reject**.
- Our counter-position: each party bears its own costs; the Receiving Party uses commercially reasonable efforts to cooperate with any effort by the Disclosing Party to obtain a protective order or other appropriate remedy.

**viii. Return/Destruction of Materials**

- Corrigan proposes a **5 business day** window for return/destruction — **reject** as impracticable given the volume of materials anticipated.
- Revert to our original **10 business day** window.
- Additionally, please add a bona fide document-retention carve-out for electronically archived copies maintained on automatic backup systems or in accordance with legal or regulatory retention requirements. Such copies remain subject to the NDA's confidentiality obligations for the full duration of the confidentiality term.

**ix. Non-Solicitation**

- Corrigan proposes extending the non-solicitation period from our original **18 months** to **24 months** — **reject**. Retain 18 months.
- Corrigan's proposed **liquidated damages of $150,000 per breach** — **rejected**. Our counter-remedy is equitable relief (injunction). The liquidated damages provision is unnecessary and would create complications. Remove it entirely and ensure the agreement provides for injunctive and other equitable relief as the remedy for breach of the non-solicitation.

**x. Standstill — Flag for Careful Attention**

- Corrigan proposes a **12-month standstill with a don't-ask-don't-waive (DADW) provision**.
- **Primary position: reject the standstill entirely.** A standstill is inappropriate here — this is a consensual, buy-side-initiated process. We are the party approaching Corrigan about a potential acquisition, and a standstill serves no legitimate purpose in that context.
- **Fallback** (authorized only if Corrigan insists and it becomes a genuine deal-blocker): we would accept a narrowed **6-month standstill** with a **fall-away provision** triggered upon a third-party bid for Corrigan or the DAD, and **no DADW**. Do not offer the fallback in the first instance. Reject outright, and hold the fallback in reserve for further negotiation if needed. If you think we need to discuss strategy on this point, call me before drafting.

**xi. Governing Law and Dispute Resolution**

- Governing law **must remain Delaware**. This is non-negotiable.
- Dispute resolution: exclusive jurisdiction in the **Delaware Court of Chancery** (or, if the Court of Chancery declines jurisdiction, the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware).
- **Reject** Corrigan's proposal for binding arbitration under JAMS rules in Richmond, Virginia. Our rationale: injunctive relief — which is the primary remedy contemplated by the agreement — is far better served by court jurisdiction, and Delaware Court of Chancery practice is well-suited to these disputes. We are not willing to arbitrate.

**xii. Bond / Injunctive Relief**

- Corrigan's proposed **$500,000 bond requirement** before seeking injunctive relief — **rejected**. A bond requirement of this nature is not market for M&A NDAs and is inconsistent with Delaware Court of Chancery practice.
- Our position: injunctive relief must be available **without the requirement of posting a bond or other security**. Draft accordingly.

**xiii. No Obligation to Negotiate / Transaction Disclaimer**

- Corrigan proposes: "Neither party shall have any obligation to continue discussions or negotiate in good faith."
- Replace with our preferred language: "Nothing herein shall obligate either party to consummate any transaction." Corrigan's formulation could be read to negate any implied duty that might otherwise arise; our language is cleaner and more narrowly addresses the point.
