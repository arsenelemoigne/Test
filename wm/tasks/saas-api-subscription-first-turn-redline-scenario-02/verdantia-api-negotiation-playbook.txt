Section 1 — Purpose and Scope
This Playbook governs all negotiations of API and platform licensing agreements involving the Verdantia Nexus API product line. It provides the authorized negotiating range for Verdantia's in-house legal team, including Priya Chandrasekaran, VP & Associate General Counsel, and outside counsel at Linden & Foss LLP, specifically Marcus Trevino, Partner, when engaging with counterparties and their advisors in connection with the licensing of the Verdantia Nexus API. The positions, strategies, and parameters set forth herein reflect the collective judgment of Verdantia's Legal, Finance, Engineering, and Platform Partnerships teams and have been approved by the General Counsel.
This Playbook applies to all new enterprise API licensing transactions and all material amendments to existing agreements involving the Verdantia Nexus API. For purposes of this Playbook, a "material amendment" includes any modification that affects pricing, SLA commitments, intellectual property rights, liability exposure, or term and termination provisions. This Playbook cross-references and is intended to be used in conjunction with Verdantia's Standard Form Library, which includes the API License Agreement template (Form VER-STD-API-2024), the Standard SaaS Order Form, the Data Processing Addendum (DPA), and the Professional Services Addendum. Negotiators should have current versions of each of these documents readily accessible throughout the negotiation process.
All negotiating positions set forth in this Playbook are organized according to a four-tier framework for each clause category:
(a) Preferred/Starting Position — The position Verdantia leads with in its initial draft and the first round of negotiations. This represents the optimal commercial and legal outcome for Verdantia and should be presented with confidence as Verdantia's standard market position.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground Position — The position to which Verdantia may move in the course of negotiation in exchange for reciprocal concessions from the counterparty. Movement to this position should be deliberate, documented, and, where possible, contingent on receiving value from the counterparty on other terms.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor Position — The absolute minimum position that Verdantia will accept on the applicable term. Movement below the floor is not authorized under this Playbook and requires escalation as described in Section 22.
(d) Rationale and Business Context — The commercial, technical, or legal reasoning underlying the positions set forth above. This section is provided to equip negotiators with persuasive justifications during discussions with counterparties and their counsel.
Nothing in this Playbook constitutes a pre-authorization to concede below a stated floor position. Any deviation from the Walk-Away/Floor position on any clause category, regardless of deal size or strategic importance, requires escalation in accordance with the procedures set forth in Section 22 of this Playbook. Negotiators who make concessions below stated floors without proper escalation and written approval are acting outside the scope of their authority, and such concessions may not be binding on Verdantia.
This Playbook is a living document. It is reviewed and updated on a semi-annual basis by the Office of the General Counsel in consultation with the Finance, Engineering, and Platform Partnerships teams. Version 3.2 reflects updates as of May 2025, incorporating lessons learned from recent transactions and updated infrastructure cost data from Engineering.
Section 2 — Payment Terms
2.1 — Invoice Timing and Net Terms
Payment terms are among the first commercial provisions to be discussed in any enterprise API licensing negotiation. Verdantia's positions on invoice timing and net payment terms are designed to ensure predictable quarterly cash inflows consistent with the Company's revenue recognition model and treasury forecasting requirements.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Net 30 from invoice date, invoiced quarterly in advance. Under this structure, Verdantia issues invoices on the first business day of each calendar quarter for the upcoming quarter's license fees. For a deal at an Annual License Fee of $1,260,000, the quarterly invoice amount is $315,000, payable within 30 calendar days of the invoice date. This structure provides Verdantia with cash collection well within the quarter, supporting accurate forecasting and minimizing days sales outstanding (DSO).
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Net 35 from invoice date, with a written acknowledgment of receipt of invoice within 5 business days of transmission. The acknowledgment requirement is included to foreclose the common counterparty tactic of claiming delayed or non-receipt of invoices as a basis for extending the effective payment period. In practice, the invoice acknowledgment mechanism shortens the dispute cycle and aligns both parties on the payment timeline from the date of acknowledged receipt.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: Net 45 from invoice date. Do not concede beyond Net 45 under any circumstances. Any counterparty request for Net 60 or longer must be rejected at the negotiating table. If the counterparty cites internal procurement constraints requiring payment terms longer than Net 45, the negotiator should offer to adjust the invoice issuance date (e.g., issuing invoices 15 days before the start of each quarter) rather than extending the net terms beyond 45 days.
(d) Rationale: Verdantia's revenue recognition model and treasury forecasting depend on predictable quarterly cash inflows. Net 30 is the prevailing standard in the SaaS and API industry for enterprise-tier transactions, and Verdantia's Finance team has built its cash flow projections and working capital models on the assumption of Net 30 collections. Net 45 remains within Finance's tolerance but introduces a material DSO impact at scale—across a portfolio of enterprise licensees, the incremental 15 days of float can represent several million dollars of delayed cash inflow in any given quarter. Extending beyond Net 45 would require Verdantia to adjust its working capital facility, which carries a cost that is not reflected in the standard license fee structure.
2.2 — Payment Currency and Method
All payments under the Agreement shall be made in United States Dollars (USD) by wire transfer to Verdantia's designated bank account, as specified in the applicable invoice. Automated Clearing House (ACH) transfers are acceptable for domestic licensees domiciled and operating within the United States. For any transaction with a Total Contract Value (TCV) exceeding $1,000,000, payment by check is not acceptable due to processing delays, reconciliation complexity, and the risk of lost or misdirected instruments. International licensees must remit payment by wire transfer and shall bear all bank fees, intermediary charges, and currency conversion costs associated with the transfer. Verdantia will not absorb foreign exchange risk or accept payment in currencies other than USD. The invoice will specify wire instructions, including the receiving bank name, ABA routing number (for domestic wires), SWIFT code (for international wires), account number, and any required reference information.
Section 3 — Late-Payment Interest
Timely payment is essential to Verdantia's financial planning and operational stability. The late-payment interest provision serves as a meaningful commercial incentive for licensees to adhere to the agreed payment schedule. The following positions reflect a calibrated approach to interest rates that balances enforceability, commercial reasonableness, and deterrence.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Interest accrues on all overdue amounts at a rate of 1.5% per month, compounding monthly, from the date payment was due until the date payment is received in full. This rate (approximately 19.6% annualized with compounding) is consistent with market norms for enterprise software and API licensing and provides a strong incentive for the licensee to prioritize timely payment.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Interest accrues on overdue amounts at a rate of 1.25% per month, calculated as simple interest (not compounding), together with a statutory-maximum savings clause. The savings clause provides that, notwithstanding the stated rate, interest shall not exceed the maximum rate permitted by applicable law. The inclusion of the savings clause is a standard enforceability measure and should not be characterized to the counterparty as a substantive concession—it is a prudent drafting practice that protects both parties.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: Interest at a rate of 1.0% per month, or the maximum rate permitted by applicable law, whichever is lesser. Do not concede below 1.0% per month under any circumstances. The statutory-cap qualifier must always be included in the final agreement language to avoid enforceability challenges in jurisdictions with usury statutes or other rate limitations. If the counterparty objects to the interest rate on the basis that its internal policy or applicable law prohibits interest above a certain threshold, the negotiator should request documentation of the specific legal restriction and, if verified, apply the statutory-cap qualifier rather than reducing the stated contractual rate.
(d) Rationale: Late-payment interest must remain a meaningful incentive for timely payment. At 1.0% per month, the annualized rate of approximately 12% still materially exceeds Verdantia's weighted average cost of capital (currently approximately 6.8%) and serves the intended deterrent purpose. Dropping below this level renders the provision effectively decorative—an interest rate that does not exceed the licensee's own cost of capital provides no financial incentive to prioritize Verdantia's invoice over other payables. Additionally, a below-market interest rate signals to the counterparty that Verdantia does not treat payment timeliness as a serious obligation, which can undermine the broader commercial relationship.
Section 4 — Overage Pricing
4.1 — Per-Call Overage Rate
Overage pricing is one of the most commercially significant provisions in any Verdantia Nexus API licensing transaction. The overage rate directly determines Verdantia's unit economics on high-consumption accounts and is a key input to the Company's long-term revenue model. Negotiators should approach this provision with a clear understanding of Verdantia's marginal infrastructure costs and the contribution margin required to sustain the Verdantia Nexus API platform.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: $0.004 per API call above the Base Tier of 5,000,000 calls per calendar month. The Base Tier is included in the Annual License Fee; only calls in excess of the Base Tier are subject to overage billing. At this rate, a licensee consuming 7,500,000 calls in a given month (2,500,000 overage calls) would incur an overage charge of $10,000 for that month. The $0.004 rate reflects Verdantia's full cost of service delivery, including infrastructure, monitoring, support, and a contribution margin consistent with the Company's overall gross margin targets.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: $0.003 per API call, applied as a flat rate to all usage above the Base Tier. Alternatively, Verdantia may offer a volume step-down structure as follows: $0.003 per API call for usage from 5,000,001 to 10,000,000 calls per month, with a step-down to $0.0025 per API call for all usage above 10,000,000 calls per month. Volume step-down structures are acceptable because they incentivize higher consumption, which in turn drives network effects, increases the licensee's switching costs, and generates valuable Derived Data for Verdantia's machine-learning pipeline. The step-down model should be positioned to the counterparty as a reward for growth and a recognition of the economies of scale that Verdantia achieves at higher throughput levels.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: $0.003 per API call at any tier. Do not concede below $0.003 per call at any volume threshold. Any step-down below $0.0025 per call at any volume threshold requires the written sign-off of both the VP of Engineering and the Chief Financial Officer prior to being offered or accepted. Negotiators do not have authority to approve sub-$0.0025 step-downs independently.
(d) Rationale: The $0.003 per call rate reflects Verdantia's marginal infrastructure cost (including compute, storage, bandwidth, and monitoring) plus a 40% contribution margin. This contribution margin is the minimum necessary to fund ongoing platform development, capacity expansion, and the engineering team's operational support for high-throughput accounts. Below the $0.003 threshold, overages become margin-negative on accounts with sustained high-volume consumption, meaning Verdantia would effectively be subsidizing the licensee's usage growth. The step-down model is an acceptable compromise because the marginal cost per call decreases slightly at very high volumes due to infrastructure amortization, but this effect is fully captured at the $0.0025 level—there is no further cost benefit below that figure.
4.2 — Overage Measurement and Reporting
Overages are calculated on a calendar-month basis (first day of the month through the last day of the month, UTC) using Verdantia's proprietary metering infrastructure. Verdantia's metering system records each API call at the point of ingress to the Verdantia Nexus API gateway, and the call count used for billing purposes is the count recorded by Verdantia's metering system. The licensee will have access to a real-time usage dashboard within the Verdantia Nexus API management portal, providing visibility into cumulative monthly call counts, daily and hourly usage trends, and projected overage charges based on current consumption rates.
Any disputes regarding metering accuracy must be raised by the licensee in writing within 15 calendar days of the date of the invoice reflecting the disputed overage charges. Disputes not raised within this period are deemed waived. Upon receipt of a timely dispute, Verdantia will review its metering logs and provide the licensee with a detailed usage report. If the parties are unable to resolve the dispute within 30 days, the matter will be escalated in accordance with the dispute resolution provisions of the Agreement. Negotiators should resist counterparty attempts to substitute a third-party metering tool or to use the licensee's own call logs as the authoritative measurement source; Verdantia's metering infrastructure is the single source of truth for billing purposes.
Section 5 — Burst Allowance
The burst allowance defines the threshold above the contracted Base Tier at which Verdantia begins throttling API calls or applying overage billing. The burst allowance is designed to accommodate legitimate, short-term spikes in the licensee's API consumption—such as those resulting from seasonal traffic patterns, product launches, or marketing campaigns—without triggering immediate throttling or overage charges. However, the burst allowance must be bounded to protect the shared infrastructure on which the Verdantia Nexus API operates.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: The burst allowance is set at 150% of the contracted Base Tier. For a licensee with a Base Tier of 5,000,000 calls per month, the burst allowance permits up to 7,500,000 calls per month before throttling or overage billing is triggered. Calls within the burst allowance but above the Base Tier are included in the license fee; no overage charges apply within the burst band. This structure provides the licensee with a meaningful buffer (2,500,000 additional calls) to accommodate usage spikes without financial penalty, while preserving Verdantia's ability to manage shared infrastructure capacity.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: The burst allowance may be increased to 175% of the contracted Base Tier, i.e., up to 8,750,000 calls per month for a 5,000,000-call Base Tier. This represents an additional 1,250,000 calls of headroom beyond the preferred position. The 175% burst allowance should be offered as a concession in exchange for reciprocal movement from the counterparty on another provision—it should not be conceded unilaterally.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: 175% of the contracted Base Tier is the maximum concession on burst allowance. Do not agree to 200% or higher under any circumstances. A burst allowance of 200% or more effectively doubles the licensee's contracted capacity at no additional cost and exposes Verdantia to uncompensated capacity reservation. At that level, the burst band ceases to function as a temporary spike accommodation and instead becomes a de facto expansion of the Base Tier without a corresponding increase in fees. Additionally, burst consumption at 200% or above by any single tenant risks service degradation for other tenants on the shared Verdantia Nexus API infrastructure, potentially triggering SLA credit obligations across Verdantia's entire customer portfolio.
(d) Rationale: The Verdantia Nexus API architecture provisions shared infrastructure capacity across all tenants based on their contracted Base Tiers plus a reserve buffer. The reserve buffer is sized to accommodate burst consumption at up to 175% of contracted tiers across the fleet. Burst headroom above 175% would require Verdantia to provision dedicated capacity buffers for the requesting licensee, which is not economically justified without supplemental fees. Engineering has modeled the infrastructure impact and confirmed that a 175% fleet-wide burst ceiling is the maximum sustainable threshold under current capacity planning assumptions. Any request for burst allowances exceeding 175% should be redirected to a discussion about upgrading the licensee's Base Tier to a higher volume commitment, which would be priced accordingly.
Section 6 — Uptime SLA
6.1 — Availability Commitment
The uptime Service Level Agreement (SLA) is one of the most heavily negotiated provisions in enterprise API licensing transactions and one of the most commercially significant terms in this Playbook. The uptime commitment directly affects Verdantia's SLA credit exposure, its infrastructure investment requirements, and its competitive positioning in the market. Negotiators must approach this provision with a thorough understanding of both the technical capabilities and the limitations of the Verdantia Nexus API platform.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Verdantia commits to 99.9% monthly uptime for the Verdantia Nexus API, excluding scheduled maintenance windows. The 99.9% target permits approximately 43.2 minutes of unplanned downtime per calendar month. Uptime is measured on a calendar-month basis (first day through last day, UTC). Scheduled maintenance windows, as defined in Section 8 of this Playbook, are excluded from the uptime calculation. This uptime commitment is consistent with prevailing market standards for enterprise-grade API platforms and reflects the actual performance capabilities of Verdantia's current multi-region infrastructure.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Hold at 99.9%. The 99.9% uptime target is not a concession point—it is the floor on the percentage figure itself. If the counterparty pushes for 99.95% (approximately 21.6 minutes of permitted downtime per month) or higher, the negotiator should not concede on the uptime percentage. Instead, the negotiator should offer an enhanced Priority 1 (P1) incident response commitment as an alternative that addresses the counterparty's underlying concern about response urgency during outages. Specifically, Verdantia will guarantee an initial response within 30 minutes of detection or report of a P1 incident and will provide status updates to the licensee's designated technical contact every 15 minutes during the pendency of any P1 outage. This enhanced response commitment should be documented in the Agreement as a separate SLA obligation alongside, but distinct from, the uptime percentage commitment. This approach preserves the 99.9% target while delivering material operational value to the licensee—in practice, the licensee's primary concern during an outage is not the contractual uptime percentage but the speed and quality of Verdantia's response.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: 99.9% monthly uptime. Do not agree to 99.95% or higher. This is a firm floor with no exceptions. Verdantia's current multi-region architecture—operating across AWS us-east-1, us-west-2, and eu-west-1—cannot reliably sustain a 99.95% commitment across all API endpoints without a dedicated infrastructure investment that has not been scoped, budgeted, or approved. Committing to 99.95% would expose Verdantia to SLA credit liability for downtime events that are within normal operational parameters for the current architecture. If the counterparty makes 99.95% a deal-breaking requirement, escalate to Priya Chandrasekaran for a business-case analysis of the incremental infrastructure investment that would be required to support the higher commitment, including the cost of additional redundancy, failover mechanisms, and 24/7 site reliability engineering coverage.
(d) Rationale: The gap between 99.9% and 99.95% may appear trivial on paper—the difference between approximately 43 minutes and approximately 22 minutes of permitted downtime per month—but the infrastructure and operational cost to move from one level to the other is substantial and non-linear. Achieving 99.95% would require, at a minimum, additional active-active redundancy across all three regions, automated failover with sub-second switchover times, and a dedicated site reliability engineering team providing 24/7 coverage with no single points of failure in the on-call rotation. Engineering estimates the annualized incremental cost of sustaining 99.95% at approximately $2.4 million across infrastructure and personnel, which is not reflected in the standard Annual License Fee. The enhanced P1 incident response alternative is a proven negotiation strategy that gives the licensee material operational value—faster response, more frequent communication, and a contractual commitment to urgency—without exposing Verdantia to SLA credit liability for an uptime target it cannot consistently meet across its entire fleet.
6.2 — Uptime Measurement Methodology
Uptime is measured by Verdantia's designated third-party monitoring tool (currently Datadog Synthetics) at 60-second intervals across all primary API endpoints listed in the Verdantia Nexus API Service Description. An endpoint is considered "available" if it returns a valid HTTP response (2xx or 3xx status code) within the defined latency threshold (currently 5,000 milliseconds) during each monitoring interval. Monthly uptime is calculated as follows:
Monthly Uptime Percentage = ((Total Minutes in Month − Downtime Minutes) / Total Minutes in Month) × 100
The following categories of events are excluded from the downtime calculation and do not count against the uptime commitment:
(i) Scheduled maintenance windows conducted in accordance with Section 8 of the Agreement;
(ii) Downtime caused by the licensee's applications, equipment, network connectivity, or client-side errors (HTTP 4xx status codes);
(iii) Force majeure events as defined in the Agreement;
(iv) Downtime resulting from the licensee's failure to implement required updates, patches, or configuration changes as specified by Verdantia in the Documentation; and
(v) DNS propagation delays or internet routing issues outside of Verdantia's network perimeter.
Negotiators should resist counterparty attempts to add custom measurement endpoints, substitute the licensee's own monitoring tools as the authoritative source of uptime data, or include licensee-selected third-party monitoring services as a co-determinative measurement source. Verdantia's third-party monitoring tool is the single authoritative source for uptime measurement, and this must be clearly stated in the Agreement.
Section 7 — SLA Credits
SLA credits are the financial mechanism through which Verdantia provides recourse to licensees for uptime shortfalls. The credit structure must be calibrated to provide a meaningful incentive for Verdantia to maintain high availability without creating a revenue exposure that is disproportionate to the contract value or that transforms the SLA credit into a de facto liquidated-damages mechanism.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Credits are calculated at a rate of 5% of the applicable Monthly Fee for each 0.1% of uptime shortfall below 99.9% in a given calendar month, subject to an aggregate cap of 30% of the applicable Monthly Fee for that month. For illustrative purposes, at a Monthly Fee of $105,000 (derived from an Annual License Fee of $1,260,000): each 0.1% of uptime shortfall generates a credit of $5,250; the maximum credit in any given month is $31,500. Under this structure, a month in which Verdantia delivers 99.5% uptime (0.4% shortfall) would result in a credit of $21,000 (4 × $5,250), well within the 30% cap.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Credits are calculated at a rate of 7.5% of the applicable Monthly Fee for each 0.1% of uptime shortfall below 99.9%, subject to an aggregate cap of 50% of the applicable Monthly Fee for that month. At a Monthly Fee of $105,000: each 0.1% of uptime shortfall generates a credit of $7,875; the maximum credit in any given month is $52,500. This represents a meaningful increase in credit exposure over the preferred position and should be offered only in exchange for reciprocal movement from the counterparty on another provision of comparable value.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: 7.5% per 0.1% shortfall and a 50% monthly cap represent the absolute floor. Do not concede above 7.5% per increment, and do not concede above a 50% monthly cap. Any request from the counterparty for credits calculated at 10% or more per increment, or for a 100%-of-monthly-fee credit cap, or for a "full refund" structure in which the licensee receives a complete refund of the Monthly Fee for any month in which the uptime target is missed, must be firmly rejected. Such structures convert SLA credits from an incentive mechanism into a punitive damages provision and are fundamentally inconsistent with Verdantia's commercial model.
(d) Rationale: SLA credits are designed to function as a service-level incentive—a financial commitment by Verdantia to maintain high availability—not as a liquidated-damages mechanism or a revenue clawback tool. A 50% monthly cap ensures that Verdantia retains revenue sufficient to cover the fixed infrastructure costs that continue to be incurred during and after any period of reduced availability. During an outage event, Verdantia's engineering team is fully mobilized to restore service; the infrastructure costs do not decrease during downtime—they increase, as additional resources are deployed for incident response and remediation. Credits beyond 50% of the Monthly Fee would create a perverse incentive structure in which a licensee could benefit financially from outages, potentially leading to strategic delay in reporting restored service or to aggressive uptime-dispute posturing. The 50% cap also ensures that Verdantia's overall revenue model remains viable even in a worst-case-scenario month, which is important for revenue predictability and investor relations.
The following structural requirements apply to SLA credits in all transactions and are non-negotiable:
(i) Credits are the sole and exclusive remedy for uptime shortfalls below the SLA target. This must be stated expressly in the Agreement using language substantially similar to: "The service credits described in this Section constitute the Licensee's sole and exclusive remedy, and Verdantia's entire liability, for any failure to meet the uptime commitment set forth herein."
(ii) Credits are applied as offsets against the next quarterly invoice following the month in which the shortfall occurred. Credits are not paid in cash, are not refundable, and do not carry over beyond the immediately following invoice period. If the Agreement terminates before the credit is applied, the credit is forfeited.
(iii) Credits must be claimed by the licensee within 30 calendar days of the end of the month in which the shortfall occurred. The licensee must submit a written credit request identifying the affected month, the claimed uptime shortfall, and supporting data. Credits not claimed within this period are deemed waived.
Section 8 — Scheduled Maintenance Windows
Scheduled maintenance is essential to the ongoing reliability, security, and performance of the Verdantia Nexus API. The maintenance window provision defines the amount of planned downtime Verdantia may take without affecting its uptime SLA commitment and establishes the notice requirements for scheduled maintenance events.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Verdantia reserves the right to perform scheduled maintenance for up to 4 hours per calendar month. Verdantia will provide the licensee's designated technical contact with a minimum of 72 hours' (3 calendar days') prior written notice before each scheduled maintenance event, specifying the date, time, expected duration, and scope of the maintenance activity. Scheduled maintenance will be performed during Verdantia's standard maintenance window (Saturday 02:00–06:00 Eastern Time) whenever commercially practicable, though Verdantia reserves the right to schedule maintenance outside this window when operationally necessary.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Verdantia reserves the right to perform scheduled maintenance for up to 3 hours per calendar month. Verdantia will provide the licensee's designated technical contact with a minimum of 96 hours' (4 business days') prior written notice before each scheduled maintenance event. Maintenance will be scheduled during Verdantia's standard maintenance window (Saturday 02:00–06:00 Eastern Time) whenever commercially practicable. This position reduces the monthly maintenance allowance by one hour while extending the notice period to give the licensee additional lead time to prepare for potential service interruptions.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: 3 hours per month is the minimum maintenance window. Do not agree to 2 hours or fewer of monthly scheduled maintenance under any circumstances. Verdantia's Engineering team has confirmed that the Verdantia Nexus API requires an average of 2.4 hours of planned downtime per month across the current fleet for activities including patching, schema migrations, certificate rotations, infrastructure upgrades, and capacity rebalancing. A 3-hour window provides a necessary buffer above the average to accommodate months in which more extensive maintenance is required (e.g., major infrastructure upgrades or database migrations). A 2-hour window would force Verdantia to defer maintenance activities, creating a maintenance backlog that would degrade long-term system reliability, increase the risk of unplanned outages, and ultimately harm the licensee's service quality.
Notice periods beyond 5 business days are operationally unworkable for planned maintenance and must be rejected. However, negotiators must ensure that the Agreement includes a carve-out for critical security maintenance. Specifically, the Agreement should provide that Verdantia may perform emergency security maintenance with 24 hours' prior written notice (or, in cases of imminent security threat, concurrent notice) when Verdantia reasonably determines that immediate maintenance is necessary to address a critical security vulnerability, a zero-day exploit, or a regulatory compliance requirement. Emergency security maintenance is subject to the same monthly hour cap but is exempt from the standard notice period.
(d) Rationale: Engineering has confirmed that the Verdantia Nexus API requires an average of 2.4 hours of planned downtime per month across the current fleet. This figure is based on a trailing 18-month analysis of maintenance events across all production environments. The 3-hour window provides a 25% buffer above the observed average, which is the minimum necessary to accommodate variance—some months require virtually no planned downtime, while others (particularly those involving major version upgrades, database schema changes, or infrastructure migrations) may require the full 3 hours or close to it. Reducing the window to 2 hours would leave no margin for error and would create operational pressure to rush maintenance activities, increasing the risk of human error and unplanned outages. The 96-hour notice period at the acceptable middle-ground position is a reasonable accommodation that balances the licensee's planning needs with Verdantia's operational flexibility.
Section 9 — Intellectual Property: Derived Data
9.1 — Ownership of Derived Data
Derived Data ownership is one of the most strategically important provisions in any Verdantia Nexus API licensing transaction and is designated as a highest-priority negotiation item (see Section 23). Derived Data—defined as data generated by the Verdantia Nexus API through the processing of licensee Input Data, including but not limited to analytics, aggregations, indices, model outputs, inference results, and enriched data sets—is the foundation of Verdantia's competitive advantage. Verdantia's machine-learning models, product improvement cycles, and cross-customer benchmarking capabilities all depend on Verdantia's ability to retain ownership of and unrestricted access to Derived Data across its entire customer portfolio. Negotiators must approach this provision with the understanding that Derived Data ownership is a non-negotiable core interest, not a commercial concession point.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Verdantia owns all Derived Data generated through the licensee's use of the Verdantia Nexus API, including all intellectual property rights therein. The licensee receives a term-limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable license to use Derived Data generated from its own Input Data for the licensee's internal business purposes during the term of the Agreement. This license terminates automatically upon expiration or termination of the Agreement, and all Derived Data rights revert fully to Verdantia. This position gives the licensee full operational utility of the Derived Data during the Agreement term while preserving Verdantia's ownership and long-term strategic interests.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Verdantia retains ownership of all Derived Data, including all intellectual property rights therein. The licensee receives a perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use Derived Data generated from its own Input Data for the licensee's internal business purposes. This license survives termination or expiration of the Agreement, ensuring that the licensee is not left without access to analytics and outputs generated from its own data after the relationship ends. Verdantia retains the unrestricted right to use aggregated and de-identified Derived Data—including Derived Data generated from the licensee's Input Data—for product improvement, benchmarking, model training, new feature development, and any other purpose, without restriction, during and after the term of the Agreement. This position represents a meaningful concession from the preferred position—the perpetual, irrevocable license provides the licensee with permanent utility—and should be offered only in exchange for reciprocal movement on other terms.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: Never assign ownership of Derived Data to the licensee. This is a hard floor with no exceptions, regardless of deal size, strategic importance, or counterparty pressure. Ownership of Derived Data is foundational to Verdantia's machine-learning pipeline and to the value proposition of the Verdantia Nexus API across the entire customer base. If the counterparty insists on ownership of Derived Data—whether framed as an outright assignment, a joint ownership structure, a work-for-hire argument, or any other construct that would vest the licensee with ownership rights—the negotiator must escalate to Priya Chandrasekaran immediately. Do not engage in further negotiation on this point without guidance from the VP & Associate General Counsel.
(d) Rationale: Derived Data powers Verdantia's cross-customer model training and continuous improvement loop. The Verdantia Nexus API's machine-learning models are trained on aggregated, de-identified Derived Data from across the customer fleet, and the quality and breadth of this training data are directly proportional to the API's performance, accuracy, and value to all licensees. Assigning Derived Data ownership to any single licensee would fragment the data asset and create irreconcilable conflicts across the customer portfolio—if one licensee owns its Derived Data, every licensee will demand the same, and Verdantia's ability to train models on cross-customer data would be fundamentally impaired. The perpetual license offer at the acceptable middle-ground position gives the licensee permanent utility of the Derived Data generated from its own Input Data—which is the licensee's real commercial interest—without stripping Verdantia of the underlying ownership that is essential to its platform strategy.
9.2 — Aggregated and De-Identified Data Rights
Verdantia's right to aggregate and de-identify licensee data for use in product improvement, model training, benchmarking, and new feature development must be expressly preserved in the Agreement. This right is not implied and should be stated in clear, affirmative terms. The Agreement should provide that Verdantia may collect, aggregate, and de-identify data derived from the licensee's use of the Verdantia Nexus API—including usage patterns, performance metrics, and Derived Data—for the purpose of improving the Verdantia Nexus API and related products and services.
Aggregated data must be irreversibly de-identified in compliance with all applicable privacy laws, including but not limited to the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (to the extent applicable), and any other data protection regulations governing the licensee's Input Data. De-identification must be performed using methods that render the data incapable of being re-identified to the licensee or to any individual data subject, consistent with industry best practices and applicable regulatory guidance.
This right survives termination or expiration of the Agreement. The survival of the aggregation and de-identification right is essential because Verdantia's models are trained on historical data and cannot retroactively be untrained upon termination of any single licensee relationship.
Section 10 — Intellectual Property: Feedback
Feedback ownership is a non-negotiable position for Verdantia and is designated as a highest-priority negotiation item (see Section 23). This provision governs the ownership of ideas, suggestions, enhancement requests, bug reports, feature requests, and other feedback ("Feedback") that the licensee may provide to Verdantia regarding the Verdantia Nexus API during the term of the Agreement.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Any Feedback provided by the licensee regarding the Verdantia Nexus API—including but not limited to suggestions, enhancement requests, ideas, feature proposals, workflow recommendations, bug reports, usability observations, and performance observations—shall become the sole and exclusive property of Verdantia upon submission or communication to Verdantia, with a full, irrevocable assignment of all rights, title, and interest, including all intellectual property rights, worldwide, in and to such Feedback. The licensee agrees to execute any documents reasonably requested by Verdantia to perfect or confirm Verdantia's ownership of Feedback. The licensee waives any moral rights in Feedback to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Same as the Preferred/Starting Position. There is no middle ground on Feedback ownership. This is the only provision in this Playbook where the Preferred, Acceptable, and Floor positions are identical.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: Verdantia must retain sole and exclusive ownership of all Feedback. Do not agree to any of the following alternative structures, regardless of how they are framed by the counterparty or its counsel:
(i) A mutual license structure in which the licensee retains ownership and grants Verdantia a license to use the Feedback;
(ii) A co-ownership arrangement in which both parties share ownership rights in the Feedback;
(iii) A reservation of rights by the licensee in any Feedback submitted to Verdantia;
(iv) A "license back" structure in which Verdantia owns the Feedback but the licensee retains a license to use it independently;
(v) Any construct that conditions Verdantia's use of Feedback on attribution, compensation, or further consent from the licensee.
This is a non-negotiable position, and the negotiator should communicate it as such clearly and early in the negotiation process.
(d) Rationale: Feedback is incorporated directly into the Verdantia Nexus API product roadmap. Enhancement requests, feature suggestions, and usability observations from licensees are among the most valuable inputs to Verdantia's product development cycle. Shared ownership, a mutual-license construct, or any reservation of rights by the licensee would create blocking rights that impair Verdantia's ability to implement product improvements derived from any single licensee's input. For example, if a licensee retained co-ownership of a feature suggestion and later objected to the manner in which Verdantia implemented it, the licensee could assert a claim that impedes a product release benefiting the entire customer base. Accepting anything less than full, unencumbered ownership risks creating an intellectual property cloud over future product releases and feature updates. The industry-standard approach for API and SaaS platforms is full assignment of Feedback, and counterparties that resist this position are typically seeking to preserve negotiating leverage rather than to protect a genuine commercial interest.
Section 11 — Indemnification
11.1 — IP Indemnity (Verdantia as Indemnitor)
The intellectual property indemnity is a standard provision in enterprise API licensing agreements and reflects Verdantia's confidence in the non-infringing nature of the Verdantia Nexus API. However, the scope of this indemnity must be carefully circumscribed to ensure that Verdantia's exposure is limited to infringement arising from the licensee's authorized use of the API as contemplated by the Agreement and Documentation, and does not extend to infringement caused by the licensee's unauthorized modifications, integrations, or use outside the scope of the license grant.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Verdantia will defend, indemnify, and hold harmless the licensee and its officers, directors, employees, and agents from and against any third-party claim, action, suit, or proceeding alleging that the licensee's authorized use of the Verdantia Nexus API, in accordance with the Agreement and Documentation, directly infringes a valid United States patent, registered copyright, or trade secret of a third party. This indemnity is subject to the following conditions: (i) the licensee must promptly notify Verdantia of the claim in writing; (ii) the licensee must grant Verdantia sole control of the defense and any related settlement negotiations; and (iii) the licensee must provide reasonable cooperation at Verdantia's expense. The "authorized use" qualifier is essential to the scope of this indemnity and must appear in the final agreement language. It limits the indemnity to use that is in accordance with the license grant, the Documentation, and any applicable use restrictions, and excludes any claim arising from unauthorized modifications, combinations with third-party software or hardware not approved by Verdantia, or use of the API in a manner not contemplated by the Agreement.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: In addition to the defense and indemnification obligation, accept a cure-or-terminate approach that provides Verdantia with flexibility in responding to infringement claims. Specifically, upon notice of an infringement claim, Verdantia may, at its sole option and expense: (i) procure the right for the licensee to continue using the Verdantia Nexus API as contemplated by the Agreement; (ii) modify the API (or the affected portion thereof) to make it non-infringing while maintaining substantially equivalent functionality; or (iii) if neither (i) nor (ii) is commercially practicable, terminate the affected portion of the Agreement and refund to the licensee any pre-paid fees attributable to the unused portion of the then-current term for the affected services. This cure-or-terminate structure is well-established in the SaaS and API licensing market and provides the licensee with a clear remediation path while giving Verdantia the operational flexibility to choose the most efficient resolution.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: Never remove the "authorized use" qualifier from the IP indemnity. This qualifier is the primary scope limitation on Verdantia's indemnification exposure and is a non-negotiable element of this provision. Without the authorized-use qualifier, Verdantia would be exposed to indemnification liability for the licensee's unauthorized modifications to the API or its outputs, the licensee's integration of the API with infringing third-party systems, the licensee's use of the API in combination with other software or hardware in a manner that creates an infringing combination, and the licensee's use of the API outside the scope of the license grant (e.g., in territories, for purposes, or in volumes not authorized by the Agreement). The authorized-use qualifier ensures that Verdantia's indemnity obligation is coextensive with the scope of the license Verdantia has granted—no broader and no narrower.
(d) Rationale: The authorized-use qualifier is Verdantia's primary defense against tail-risk exposure from licensee-caused infringement scenarios. In the absence of this qualifier, Verdantia would effectively become an insurer of the licensee's entire technology stack—any third-party infringement claim touching the Verdantia Nexus API, regardless of whether the infringement was caused by the API itself or by the licensee's modifications, integrations, or unauthorized use, would trigger Verdantia's indemnity obligation. This is an unacceptable risk allocation that no reasonable licensor would accept. The authorized-use qualifier aligns the indemnity with the scope of the license grant: Verdantia stands behind the product it has built and the use it has authorized, but does not assume responsibility for what the licensee does with the product beyond the authorized scope.
11.2 — Data-Breach Indemnity
Data-breach indemnification is an increasingly common negotiation topic in enterprise API licensing, driven by the heightened regulatory and litigation environment surrounding data security. Verdantia's position on data-breach indemnification is designed to provide meaningful recourse to the licensee in the event of a data breach caused by Verdantia's failure to meet its security obligations, while ensuring that the trigger for the indemnity is tied to specific, objective, and controllable commitments rather than subjective standards.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: No specific data-breach indemnity beyond the general representations and warranties set forth in the Agreement. Remedies for data breaches are governed by the general liability cap (see Section 12.1) and the Data Processing Addendum, which contains detailed security obligations, breach notification requirements, and cooperation commitments. This position reflects the view that data-breach liability is most appropriately addressed through the DPA and the general liability framework, rather than through a standalone indemnity that creates a separate and potentially uncapped exposure.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Accept a data-breach indemnity, but limit the trigger exclusively to Verdantia's breach of its security obligations under the Agreement. Specifically, the data-breach indemnity should apply only where the data breach results directly from Verdantia's failure to implement or maintain the security measures required under the Security section of the Agreement (or, if applicable, the security-related provisions of the DPA). The indemnity should not be triggered by a mere occurrence of a data breach, regardless of cause. Do not accept a negligence-based trigger (which would introduce a subjective standard and expose Verdantia to hindsight bias in breach-related litigation) or a strict-liability trigger (which would make Verdantia liable for breaches caused by factors entirely outside its control, such as zero-day exploits in third-party software, state-sponsored cyberattacks, or licensee-caused vulnerabilities).
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: A data-breach indemnity triggered by Verdantia's breach of its security obligations under the Agreement is the floor. Do not agree to any of the following trigger standards:
(i) A general negligence standard (e.g., "negligent handling of licensee data");
(ii) A strict liability standard (e.g., "any unauthorized access to or disclosure of licensee data");
(iii) A trigger based on any data compromise regardless of cause or fault (e.g., "any data breach affecting licensee data processed by Verdantia").
Each of these alternative triggers expands Verdantia's exposure beyond breaches that Verdantia can prevent through adherence to its security commitments, and each introduces unacceptable risk.
(d) Rationale: A security-obligation trigger ties the data-breach indemnity to specific, scoped commitments that Verdantia controls and can insure against. Verdantia's security obligations under the Agreement are defined, measurable, and auditable—they specify the technical and organizational measures Verdantia must implement, and compliance with those measures can be verified through SOC 2 Type II audits (see Section 15). A negligence trigger, by contrast, introduces subjective standards ("reasonable care," "industry standard security practices") that may be assessed unfavorably in hindsight after a breach event, particularly by a jury or arbitral panel evaluating conduct after the fact. The security-obligation trigger gives the licensee meaningful protection—if Verdantia fails to do what it committed to do, the indemnity applies—while insulating Verdantia from liability for breaches that occur despite full compliance with its security commitments.
11.3 — Mutual Law-Violation Indemnity
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground / Floor: Accept a mutual indemnity under which each party agrees to defend, indemnify, and hold harmless the other party from and against third-party claims arising from the indemnifying party's violation of applicable laws in the performance of its obligations under the Agreement. This is a standard mutual provision and should not be a point of significant negotiation. The key requirement is that the obligation must be truly mutual—it applies equally to both parties, and neither party's compliance risk is treated as greater than the other's. Verdantia should ensure that the language does not create an asymmetric obligation (e.g., by defining "applicable laws" more broadly for Verdantia than for the licensee, or by imposing a higher standard of compliance on Verdantia). The mutual law-violation indemnity is subject to the general liability cap set forth in Section 12.1.
Section 12 — Liability Caps
12.1 — General Liability Cap
The general liability cap is one of the most important risk-allocation provisions in the Agreement and directly determines Verdantia's maximum financial exposure under the contract. The cap must be set at a level that is commercially reasonable, insurable, and proportionate to the contract value.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Each party's aggregate liability under the Agreement for all claims arising during the 12-month period preceding the event giving rise to the claim is capped at an amount equal to 1× the Annual License Fee paid or payable by the licensee during that 12-month period. At an Annual License Fee of $1,260,000, this results in a liability cap of $1,260,000. The cap applies to all claims arising under or in connection with the Agreement, whether in contract, tort, strict liability, or any other legal theory, and covers all damages, losses, costs, and expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees to the extent recoverable under the Agreement). The general liability cap does not apply to carve-out obligations (see Sections 12.2 and 12.3), which are subject to their own caps or exclusions.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Each party's aggregate liability is capped at 1.5× the Annual License Fee paid or payable during the 12-month period preceding the claim. At an Annual License Fee of $1,260,000, this results in a cap of $1,890,000. The increase from 1× to 1.5× should be characterized to the counterparty as a significant concession that reflects Verdantia's confidence in its service delivery and its commitment to the licensee's business relationship, and should be offered only in exchange for reciprocal movement on other terms.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: 1.5× annual fees ($1,890,000 at current pricing). Do not exceed this multiple under any circumstances. Any request from the counterparty for a cap of 2× or higher must be rejected. If the counterparty frames the request as a "market standard" or "non-negotiable," the negotiator should note that 1× to 1.5× is the prevailing range in enterprise API and SaaS licensing and that caps above 1.5× are uncommon outside of highly regulated industries or government contracts. If the counterparty persists, escalate to Priya Chandrasekaran.
(d) Rationale: A cap of 1.5× the Annual License Fee balances the counterparty's need for meaningful recovery in the event of a significant breach with Verdantia's need to bound its financial exposure at a level that is proportionate to the revenue generated by the contract. At the 1.5× level, the cap remains fully insurable under Verdantia's current errors and omissions (E&O) professional liability policy, which provides coverage for contractual liability claims arising from Verdantia's services. A cap above 1.5× would exceed the per-occurrence retention threshold under the current E&O policy, potentially requiring Verdantia to procure additional coverage or accept uninsured exposure, neither of which is acceptable for a standard enterprise API licensing transaction.
12.2 — Data-Breach Super Cap
Data-breach claims represent a category of heightened risk that warrants separate treatment within the liability framework. The "super cap" is a mechanism that provides enhanced recovery for data-breach claims while preserving the principle of capped liability.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Data-breach liability is subject to the general liability cap set forth in Section 12.1. No separate or elevated cap for data-breach claims.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Establish a separate "super cap" for data-breach claims at 3× the Annual License Fee, i.e., $3,780,000 at an Annual License Fee of $1,260,000. The super cap applies to all claims arising from or related to a data breach (as defined in the Agreement or the DPA), including but not limited to the data-breach indemnity obligation set forth in Section 11.2. The super cap is used in lieu of uncapped liability for data breaches and provides the licensee with a materially higher recovery ceiling for data-breach claims than is available under the general cap, while keeping Verdantia's exposure bounded and insurable. The super cap is the licensee's exclusive remedy for data-breach claims and supersedes the general liability cap for such claims.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: $3,780,000 (3× Annual License Fee at current pricing). Do not agree to uncapped data-breach liability under any circumstances. Uncapped exposure for data-breach claims is uninsurable and creates existential risk that is disproportionate to the contract value. Even Verdantia's $5,000,000 cyber-liability policy, which is among the more robust policies in the enterprise API space, has finite limits. A commitment to uncapped liability would mean that a single data-breach event could expose Verdantia to damages exceeding the total value of the contract, the cyber-liability policy limit, and potentially the Company's net assets. This is not a commercially reasonable risk allocation.
(d) Rationale: The super cap acknowledges the heightened stakes of data-breach claims—which may involve regulatory fines, notification costs, credit monitoring expenses, and third-party lawsuits—while keeping Verdantia's exposure bounded and insurable under Verdantia's current $5,000,000 cyber-liability policy (underwritten by Meridian National Insurance Company). At the 3× level ($3,780,000), the super cap falls within the policy limit with sufficient margin to cover defense costs and related expenses. A super cap above 3× would approach or exceed the policy limit, creating a gap between Verdantia's contractual commitment and its available insurance coverage.
12.3 — Consequential Damages
The consequential-damages waiver is a standard risk-allocation provision in enterprise technology agreements and is critical to ensuring that each party's exposure is limited to direct, foreseeable damages that are proportionate to the contract value.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Mutual exclusion of all indirect, consequential, incidental, special, exemplary, and punitive damages, regardless of the legal theory upon which such damages are based and regardless of whether the party against which damages are sought has been advised of the possibility of such damages. This is a broad, mutual waiver that protects both parties equally and is the prevailing market standard in enterprise API and SaaS licensing.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Maintain the mutual consequential-damages waiver but accept a carve-out from the waiver for the IP indemnity obligation set forth in Section 11.1. Under this carve-out, consequential damages may be recoverable by the indemnified party in connection with a third-party IP infringement claim covered by the IP indemnity, subject to the general liability cap. Do not extend the consequential-damages carve-out to data-breach claims. Data-breach consequential damages must remain subject to the super cap set forth in Section 12.2, which already provides enhanced recovery for data-breach claims. A consequential-damages carve-out for data breaches, in combination with the super cap, would create a potentially unlimited exposure for certain categories of data-breach damages (e.g., lost profits, reputational harm, business interruption) that are inherently speculative and difficult to quantify.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: The consequential-damages carve-out is limited to the IP indemnity obligation only. Do not accept a carve-out for data breaches, confidentiality breaches, or any other category of claims. If the counterparty insists on a data-breach consequential-damages carve-out, the negotiator should counter by pointing to the super cap (3× annual fees, $3,780,000) as the appropriate mechanism for addressing the licensee's enhanced-recovery concerns for data-breach claims. The super cap provides materially higher recovery than the general liability cap and is specifically designed to address the heightened stakes of data-breach claims—it serves the same commercial purpose as a consequential-damages carve-out but in a more predictable and bounded manner.
Section 13 — Term, Termination, and Early-Termination Fee
13.1 — Initial Term and Renewal
The standard initial term for Verdantia Nexus API licensing agreements is 3 years, commencing on the Effective Date specified in the applicable Order Form. The 3-year initial term provides both parties with a stable planning horizon and allows sufficient time for the licensee to complete integration, realize the full value of the Verdantia Nexus API, and develop the data assets and workflows that depend on the API's ongoing availability.
Following the expiration of the initial 3-year term, the Agreement will automatically renew for successive 1-year periods unless either party provides the other party with at least 90 calendar days' prior written notice of non-renewal before the end of the then-current term (whether the initial term or any renewal term). Auto-renewal is the default position, and the negotiator should resist counterparty attempts to require affirmative opt-in for each renewal period, as this creates unnecessary administrative burden and introduces uncertainty into Verdantia's revenue forecasting. The 90-day notice requirement for non-renewal is designed to provide both parties with sufficient lead time to plan for the end of the relationship, including data migration, transition planning, and reallocation of internal resources.
13.2 — Termination for Convenience
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Either party may terminate the Agreement for convenience upon 180 calendar days' prior written notice to the other party, subject to an early-termination fee equal to 50% of the remaining fees payable by the licensee during the then-current annual period (i.e., the balance of the annual commitment that has not yet been invoiced or paid as of the effective date of termination). For example, if the licensee terminates for convenience 6 months into a contract year with an Annual License Fee of $1,260,000 and has already paid $630,000 (two quarterly invoices), the early-termination fee would be 50% of the remaining $630,000, or $315,000. The early-termination fee is payable within 30 days of the effective date of termination.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Either party may terminate for convenience upon 120 calendar days' prior written notice. The early-termination fee is reduced to 25% of the remaining fees payable during the then-current annual period. Using the same example: 50% of the annual fee remaining ($630,000) × 25% = $157,500 early-termination fee. This represents a significant reduction in both the notice period and the termination fee and should be offered only in exchange for meaningful reciprocal concessions from the counterparty.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: Minimum 120 calendar days' notice; minimum 25% early-termination fee. Do not agree to a notice period shorter than 120 days under any circumstances. A notice period of 90 days or less is insufficient for Verdantia to adjust its capacity planning, deallocate dedicated infrastructure resources, reallocate engineering support personnel, and update its revenue forecasts. Do not waive the early-termination fee entirely. A zero-fee termination-for-convenience clause would allow licensees to exit the Agreement immediately after completing integration and extracting initial value from the Verdantia Nexus API without compensating Verdantia for the substantial ramp-up investment that Verdantia incurs during the onboarding period, including dedicated infrastructure provisioning, custom configuration, integration support, and the sales-cycle investment required to close the transaction.
(d) Rationale: The 120-day minimum notice period provides sufficient lead time for Verdantia to adjust capacity planning and re-allocate engineering resources that may have been dedicated to the licensee's account. Verdantia's infrastructure provisioning for enterprise API licensees involves long-lead-time commitments (e.g., reserved cloud instances, database capacity, and monitoring resources) that cannot be instantaneously deallocated or redirected. The 25% early-termination fee covers a portion—though not all—of the sunk costs of onboarding, dedicated infrastructure provisioning, integration support, and the fully burdened cost of the sales cycle that generated the deal. The fee is intentionally calibrated to be non-punitive while still providing a meaningful financial disincentive against opportunistic early termination.
13.3 — Termination for Cause / Cure Period
Either party may terminate the Agreement for cause if the other party commits a material breach of the Agreement and fails to cure such breach within the applicable cure period following receipt of written notice specifying the breach in reasonable detail.
Verdantia's preferred cure period is 30 calendar days from the date of receipt of written notice of the material breach. This period applies equally to both parties—Verdantia does not seek an asymmetric cure period. The 30-day cure period is sufficient for most categories of breach, including payment defaults, SLA non-compliance, use-restriction violations, and confidentiality breaches, and is consistent with prevailing market standards in enterprise technology agreements.
Verdantia is prepared to extend the cure period to 45 calendar days if the counterparty provides a strong justification for the extension, such as the complexity of the breach, the need for third-party coordination to effect a cure, or regulatory constraints that may delay remediation. Any extension beyond 45 days should be resisted, as it effectively permits the breaching party to continue in breach for an extended period without consequence and may undermine the non-breaching party's ability to mitigate damages.
The cure period applies to both parties equally. Negotiators should ensure that the Agreement does not contain an asymmetric cure period (e.g., 30 days for the licensee but 60 days for Verdantia, or vice versa), as such provisions create an appearance of unfairness that can complicate enforcement.
Section 14 — Post-Termination Transition
14.1 — Read-Only Access Period
The post-termination transition period addresses the licensee's legitimate need to extract and migrate data from the Verdantia Nexus API following termination or expiration of the Agreement. Verdantia recognizes that abrupt cessation of API access on the termination date may create operational disruption for the licensee and is prepared to offer a limited period of read-only access to facilitate an orderly transition. However, this access must be bounded in both duration and scope to prevent post-termination use of the API as a substitute for a paid license.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: No free post-termination access to the Verdantia Nexus API. All access ceases on the effective date of termination or expiration. The licensee is expected to complete any data migration and transition activities during the notice period preceding termination (120–180 days under Section 13.2) or the cure period preceding a for-cause termination (30–45 days under Section 13.3). This position maximizes the commercial incentive for the licensee to maintain the Agreement and ensures a clean break on the termination date.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Verdantia will provide up to 60 calendar days of free read-only access to the Verdantia Nexus API following the effective date of termination or expiration, solely for purposes of data migration and transition. During this period, the licensee may retrieve its Input Data and any Derived Data to which it is entitled under a surviving license (see Section 9.1), but may not submit new API calls for processing, create new data, or use the API for any operational or production purpose. All other terms of the Agreement (including confidentiality, use restrictions, and IP provisions) remain in effect during the read-only access period. This position provides the licensee with a reasonable window to complete data extraction while maintaining Verdantia's commercial incentives.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: 60 calendar days of free read-only access is the maximum concession. Do not agree to 90 days or more of free post-termination access under any circumstances. If the licensee requires access beyond the 60-day period, such access will be provided at Verdantia's then-current standard API rates under a short-term access agreement. The negotiator should frame this as a fair and flexible approach: the licensee receives 60 days at no cost to complete a standard migration, and if additional time is needed due to the licensee's specific circumstances, Verdantia is willing to provide extended access on a paid basis.
(d) Rationale: Sixty calendar days is sufficient for a licensee to extract and migrate its data from the Verdantia Nexus API, based on Verdantia's experience across its enterprise customer portfolio. Verdantia's API supports bulk data export functionality that enables efficient data extraction, and the 60-day window provides ample time for even the most complex migrations. Providing extended free access beyond 60 days undermines the commercial model by allowing the licensee to continue deriving value from the API without paying for it, and creates a perverse incentive for the licensee to delay transition activities in order to maximize the period of free access.
14.2 — Data Return and Deletion
Upon termination or expiration of the Agreement (and, if applicable, upon expiration of the read-only access period described in Section 14.1), Verdantia will, at the licensee's election, either (i) return to the licensee all Input Data in Verdantia's possession or control, in a standard, machine-readable format, or (ii) securely delete all Input Data in Verdantia's possession or control using commercially reasonable methods consistent with NIST SP 800-88 guidelines for media sanitization. Verdantia will complete the return or deletion within 30 calendar days following termination (or following the expiration of the read-only access period, if applicable). Upon the licensee's written request, Verdantia will provide a written certification of deletion signed by an authorized officer.
This is a mutual position—the licensee is similarly obligated to return or delete any Verdantia Confidential Information in its possession following termination—and should not be a point of contention in negotiations. Negotiators should ensure that the data return and deletion provision is consistent with the data handling obligations set forth in the DPA and that any retention exceptions (e.g., data retained in backup systems or as required by applicable law) are clearly documented.
Section 15 — Audit Rights
Audit rights enable Verdantia to verify the licensee's compliance with the terms of the Agreement, including use restrictions, volume limitations, and data handling obligations. Verdantia's audit rights are also an important component of the Company's SOC 2 Type II compliance program and its cyber-liability insurance requirements.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Verdantia may audit the licensee's use of the Verdantia Nexus API once per calendar year upon not less than 30 calendar days' prior written notice. The audit may be conducted by Verdantia's internal audit team or by a third-party auditor designated by Verdantia. The audit will be conducted during the licensee's regular business hours and in a manner designed to minimize disruption to the licensee's operations. The licensee will provide reasonable access to relevant systems, records, and personnel. The audit is conducted at the licensee's expense (including the licensee's internal costs of participation and any costs of providing access).
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Verdantia may audit the licensee's use of the Verdantia Nexus API once per calendar year, plus one additional audit following a documented security incident affecting the licensee's use of the API or Verdantia's provision of the services. Notice of not less than 20 business days prior to the commencement of the audit. Each party bears its own costs of the audit—Verdantia bears the cost of its audit team or third-party auditor, and the licensee bears its own internal costs of participation and access.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: Once per calendar year plus one post-incident audit; 20 business days' notice; each party bears its own costs. Do not agree to any of the following:
(i) More than two audits per calendar year (one annual plus one post-incident). Additional audits beyond this frequency are burdensome and unnecessary under normal operating conditions.
(ii) Fewer than 20 business days' notice. A shorter notice period may not provide sufficient time for the licensee to prepare or for Verdantia to coordinate its audit team, and it raises the risk that the audit will be conducted in a chaotic or adversarial manner.
(iii) A cost allocation under which Verdantia pays the licensee's costs of audit participation. This allocation would create a financial disincentive for Verdantia to exercise its audit right and would effectively penalize Verdantia for conducting compliance verification.
(d) Rationale: Annual audits are sufficient for compliance verification under normal circumstances and are consistent with industry norms for enterprise technology agreements. The post-incident audit right is critical for two independent reasons. First, it is a requirement of Verdantia's SOC 2 Type II program, which is audited annually by Clairemont & Sadler CPAs LLP and which requires Verdantia to demonstrate the ability to investigate and verify customer-side security events that may affect the integrity of Verdantia's platform. Second, Verdantia's cyber-liability insurance policy requires Verdantia to maintain contractual audit rights that enable post-incident investigation. Failure to maintain these rights could jeopardize coverage under the policy. The each-party-bears-own-cost allocation is the fairest approach and avoids gamesmanship—if one party bore the other's costs, there would be an incentive for the cost-bearing party to avoid exercising the audit right (if it paid the other's costs) or to make the audit as expensive as possible (if the other party was paying).
Section 16 — Sub-Processors
The sub-processor provision governs Verdantia's ability to engage third-party service providers (sub-processors) in connection with the processing of licensee data. This provision is closely related to the DPA and must be consistent with Verdantia's obligations under applicable data protection laws, including the GDPR (for EU data subjects) and the CCPA (for California consumers).
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Verdantia retains the right to engage sub-processors under a general authorization model. The licensee grants general authorization for Verdantia to engage sub-processors, subject to the following conditions: (i) Verdantia will maintain an up-to-date list of its sub-processors, available to the licensee upon request; (ii) Verdantia will provide the licensee with not less than 30 calendar days' prior written notice before engaging a new sub-processor or making a material change to an existing sub-processor relationship; and (iii) Verdantia will ensure that each sub-processor is bound by data protection obligations no less protective than those imposed on Verdantia under the Agreement and DPA.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: General authorization with enhanced notice and objection rights. Verdantia will provide the licensee with not less than 45 calendar days' prior written notice before engaging a new sub-processor. The licensee will have a 20-business-day objection window, commencing on the date of Verdantia's notice, during which the licensee may object to the engagement of the proposed sub-processor on reasonable data-security grounds. If the licensee objects, the parties will discuss the objection in good faith and attempt to reach a mutually acceptable resolution. If no resolution is reached within 15 business days following the licensee's objection, the licensee's sole remedy is termination of the affected processing activity (i.e., the specific data processing function for which the sub-processor was proposed), not termination of the entire Agreement. The licensee may not object on commercial or competitive grounds—only on reasonable data-security grounds supported by a written explanation of the specific security concern.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: General authorization model. Do not agree to a specific prior written consent model under any circumstances. A consent-based model would give the licensee a unilateral veto over Verdantia's operational infrastructure decisions, including vendor selections, cloud provider migrations, and technology stack changes. This veto power would create unacceptable delays in vendor transitions, inhibit Verdantia's ability to respond to security incidents that require rapid vendor changes, and effectively freeze Verdantia's current vendor stack. The general authorization model with notice and objection rights gives the licensee a meaningful voice in sub-processor decisions without granting a blocking right that could impair Verdantia's operations.
(d) Rationale: Verdantia's infrastructure relies on a dynamic sub-processor ecosystem that includes cloud infrastructure providers, monitoring and observability platforms, content delivery networks, security tools, and specialized data processing services. The composition of this ecosystem evolves over time as Verdantia identifies opportunities to improve performance, reduce costs, enhance security, or comply with new regulatory requirements. A consent-based model would effectively freeze the current vendor stack and prevent Verdantia from implementing cost or performance improvements, responding to vendor-initiated changes (e.g., a sub-processor's end-of-life announcement), or complying with new security mandates that require adoption of updated tools or services. The extended notice period (45 days) and objection right (20 business days) give the licensee a meaningful voice in sub-processor decisions and ample time to evaluate the security implications of a proposed sub-processor change, without granting a blocking right that would subordinate Verdantia's operational flexibility to the licensee's approval.
Section 17 — Governing Law and Dispute Resolution
17.1 — Governing Law
The choice of governing law is a foundational provision that affects the interpretation of virtually every other term in the Agreement. Verdantia's strong preference is for Delaware law, which provides the most developed, predictable, and business-friendly body of commercial law for the Company's contract structures.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: The Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict-of-laws principles. All disputes arising under or in connection with the Agreement shall be subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware, sitting in Wilmington, Delaware, or, if the Court of Chancery declines jurisdiction, the Superior Court of the State of Delaware, sitting in New Castle County. Each party irrevocably consents to the personal jurisdiction and venue of such courts and waives any objection based on inconvenient forum. The Court of Chancery is the preferred forum because it is a court of equity with deep expertise in commercial and technology disputes, it does not use juries (eliminating jury-trial risk), and it provides expedited proceedings for injunctive relief and other equitable remedies.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Delaware law governs. Disputes are resolved by binding arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association (AAA) under its Commercial Arbitration Rules, seated in Wilmington, Delaware. For disputes involving amounts in controversy exceeding $500,000, the dispute is heard by a panel of three arbitrators, each of whom must have at least 10 years of experience in technology or commercial law. For disputes involving amounts in controversy at or below $500,000, the dispute is heard by a single arbitrator meeting the same qualification requirements. The arbitration proceedings shall be confidential, and the arbitrator(s) shall issue a reasoned award. Judgment on the award may be entered in any court of competent jurisdiction. The arbitration alternative may be offered to counterparties that have internal policies prohibiting litigation or that prefer the confidentiality and finality of arbitration.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: Verdantia strongly prefers Delaware governing law and will not concede to New York governing law. While New York is a well-developed commercial law jurisdiction, it introduces unnecessary variability into the interpretation of Verdantia's standard contract provisions, many of which have been specifically drafted with Delaware law principles in mind. If arbitration is offered as an alternative to litigation, the arbitration must be seated in Wilmington, Delaware—not in New York, not in the counterparty's home jurisdiction, and not in any other location. The Wilmington seat preserves Verdantia's home-forum advantage, ensures consistency with the Company's corporate governance documents (Verdantia is a Delaware C-Corporation with its registered agent in Wilmington), and provides logistical convenience for Verdantia's legal team.
(d) Rationale: Verdantia is a Delaware C-Corporation, and Delaware law is the most developed and predictable body of commercial law for the Company's contract structures. Delaware's courts—particularly the Court of Chancery—have a deep bench of judges with extensive experience in technology, licensing, and commercial disputes, and Delaware case law provides well-established guidance on the interpretation of common contract provisions, including limitation-of-liability clauses, indemnification obligations, and intellectual property assignments. New York law, while generally well-developed, introduces unnecessary variability—for example, New York courts have applied different interpretive standards to limitation-of-liability clauses and consequential-damages waivers than Delaware courts, which could affect the enforceability of provisions that Verdantia has carefully drafted under Delaware law assumptions. Wilmington-seated arbitration preserves forum convenience while offering the counterparty the procedural neutrality, confidentiality, and finality that many enterprise licensees value in the arbitration process.
17.2 — Injunctive Relief Carve-Out
Notwithstanding any arbitration agreement set forth in the Agreement, both parties retain the right to seek injunctive relief, temporary restraining orders, or other equitable relief in any court of competent jurisdiction to prevent irreparable harm, including but not limited to breaches of confidentiality obligations, intellectual property infringement, and unauthorized use or disclosure of trade secrets. The parties acknowledge that a breach of such obligations would cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages would be an inadequate remedy and that the non-breaching party shall be entitled to seek equitable relief without the requirement of posting a bond or other security, to the extent permitted by applicable law.
This carve-out is a standard provision in commercial agreements that include arbitration clauses and is not a concession. It is necessary to ensure that either party can obtain emergency relief in a court of competent jurisdiction without waiting for the appointment of an arbitral tribunal, which may take weeks or months under the AAA's Commercial Arbitration Rules. The negotiator should resist counterparty attempts to narrow this carve-out (e.g., by limiting it to a specific court or by requiring the posting of a bond).
Section 18 — Assignment
The assignment provision governs each party's ability to transfer its rights and obligations under the Agreement to a third party. This provision is important for protecting Verdantia's commercial interests, competitive position, and relationship with the specific licensee with which it contracted.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Neither party may assign or transfer the Agreement, or any of its rights or obligations hereunder, without the prior written consent of the other party, which consent shall not be unreasonably withheld, conditioned, or delayed. Any attempted assignment without consent is void and of no effect. This position gives each party a consent right over all assignments, ensuring that neither party is forced into a contractual relationship with an entity it has not evaluated and approved.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Neither party may assign the Agreement without the prior written consent of the other party, except that either party may assign the Agreement without the other party's consent in connection with a merger, acquisition, reorganization, or sale of all or substantially all of the assigning party's assets, provided that:
(i) The assignee is not a direct competitor of Verdantia (as determined by Verdantia in its reasonable discretion, based on an objective assessment of the assignee's product and service offerings as of the date of the proposed assignment); and
(ii) The assignee expressly assumes all obligations of the assigning party under the Agreement in writing, pursuant to an assumption agreement in a form reasonably acceptable to Verdantia.
These two conditions are cumulative—both must be satisfied for the assignment to be effective without consent.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: The competitor exclusion and the obligation-assumption requirements are non-negotiable conditions for any assignment carve-out. Do not waive either condition under any circumstances. If the counterparty requests removal of the competitor exclusion, the negotiator should explain that an unrestricted M&A assignment right could result in a Verdantia competitor gaining access to the Verdantia Nexus API under favorable commercial terms negotiated by the original licensee, and simultaneously gaining visibility into Verdantia's pricing, SLA structure, overage economics, and technical architecture through the Agreement itself and the operational relationship. If the counterparty requests removal of the obligation-assumption requirement, the negotiator should explain that without a written assumption, the assignee would have no privity of contract with Verdantia and Verdantia would have no recourse against the assignee for post-assignment breaches.
(d) Rationale: The competitor exclusion prevents a scenario in which a Verdantia competitor acquires one of Verdantia's licensees and thereby obtains (i) access to the Verdantia Nexus API under the licensee's existing commercial terms, which may be more favorable than anything the competitor could negotiate directly, and (ii) detailed insight into Verdantia's pricing model, SLA commitments, technical architecture, and operational practices through the Agreement and the ongoing service relationship. This is not a theoretical risk—the enterprise API market is characterized by active M&A activity, and competitors routinely acquire companies in adjacent market segments. The obligation-assumption requirement is a standard protective measure that ensures the assignee is bound by the same obligations as the original licensee, providing Verdantia with contractual recourse in the event of a post-assignment breach.
Section 19 — Insurance
The insurance provision requires each party to maintain adequate insurance coverage throughout the term of the Agreement, providing a financial backstop for the indemnification and liability obligations set forth in the Agreement.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Each party shall maintain, at its own expense, commercially reasonable insurance coverage throughout the term of the Agreement, including at a minimum: (i) commercial general liability insurance with coverage limits of not less than $2,000,000 per occurrence and $4,000,000 in the aggregate; (ii) professional liability/errors and omissions (E&O) insurance with coverage limits of not less than $5,000,000 per claim and in the aggregate; and (iii) cyber-liability insurance (including coverage for data breach response costs, regulatory defense and penalties, and third-party liability) with coverage limits of not less than $5,000,000 per occurrence and in the aggregate. Each party shall provide certificates of insurance evidencing the required coverage upon the other party's written request.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Agree to specify minimum coverage levels for cyber-liability insurance at $5,000,000 per occurrence and in the aggregate, consistent with Verdantia's current policy limits. This level of specificity may be requested by counterparties that want assurance that Verdantia has adequate insurance to support the data-breach super cap and data-breach indemnity obligations. Verdantia's current cyber-liability policy, underwritten by Meridian National Insurance Company, provides coverage at this level, and a commitment to maintain $5,000,000 in cyber-liability coverage is consistent with Verdantia's current program.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: $5,000,000 is the maximum cyber-liability insurance commitment that Verdantia can make under this Playbook. Verdantia's current cyber-liability policy limit is $5,000,000 per occurrence and in the aggregate, and the Company cannot commit to a higher figure without procuring additional coverage, which has not been budgeted and would require underwriting review, premium negotiation, and board approval. Reject any counterparty requirement for $10,000,000 or above in cyber-liability coverage. If the counterparty insists on a higher coverage requirement, the negotiator should escalate to Priya Chandrasekaran for evaluation of the cost and availability of an endorsement to the existing policy or an excess cyber-liability layer. Do not commit to a higher coverage level without confirmed coverage from the insurance carrier and budget approval from the CFO.
(d) Rationale: Verdantia's current cyber-liability policy is underwritten at $5,000,000 per occurrence and in the aggregate by Meridian National Insurance Company. This coverage level is consistent with industry norms for enterprise API and SaaS companies of Verdantia's size and risk profile, and it exceeds the data-breach super cap of $3,780,000, providing a margin for defense costs and related expenses. Committing to a higher threshold—such as the $10,000,000 level commonly requested by large financial-services licensees—would require Verdantia to bind a new policy or an excess layer, which involves premium cost (estimated at $75,000–$120,000 annually for an additional $5,000,000 of coverage, based on the most recent broker indication), underwriting timeline (6–8 weeks), and potential coverage gaps during the underwriting period. Until such coverage is confirmed and budgeted, Verdantia cannot contractually commit to it.
Section 20 — Most-Favored Customer (MFC) Clauses
MFC clauses—sometimes called "most-favored nation" (MFN) clauses, pricing-parity clauses, or "best pricing" commitments—are provisions that require the licensor to offer the licensee pricing that is equal to or better than the pricing offered to any other customer. These clauses are categorically unacceptable in Verdantia's API licensing agreements.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: No MFC, MFN, or pricing-parity clause of any kind in the Agreement. Verdantia's pricing is confidential and varies by customer based on a range of factors, including API call volume commitments, term length, integration complexity, support tier, strategic relationship value, and other commercial considerations. The licensee's pricing reflects the specific terms negotiated for that licensee and does not create any entitlement to pricing parity with any other Verdantia customer.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Reject any MFC clause. As an alternative to an MFC clause, Verdantia will offer to provide the licensee with an annual benchmarking report comparing the licensee's pricing tier to anonymized market benchmarks for comparable API licensing arrangements (matched for call volume, term length, and feature parity). The benchmarking report may be prepared by Verdantia's Finance team or by a mutually agreed third-party industry analyst. The benchmarking report is provided for informational purposes only and does not create any obligation on Verdantia's part to adjust the licensee's pricing. This alternative gives the licensee visibility into market pricing without creating an enforceable price-matching obligation.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: MFC clauses are rejected in all cases. No exceptions, regardless of deal size, strategic importance, or counterparty leverage. This is a categorical prohibition, not a negotiation position. The negotiator should communicate this position clearly and early, without ambiguity, and should not entertain extended discussion of MFC clause structures, scope limitations, or "narrow" MFC formulations. Any MFC clause—even one that is limited to a specific product tier, geography, or customer segment—creates cascading repricing risk across Verdantia's entire customer portfolio, particularly given the range of deal structures, volume commitments, and customized pricing arrangements in Verdantia's enterprise book of business.
(d) Rationale: Verdantia's pricing model depends on the ability to tailor commercial terms to each licensee's specific circumstances, including its volume commitment, term length, strategic value, integration complexity, and support requirements. No two enterprise deals are identical, and Verdantia's pricing reflects the unique combination of factors in each transaction. An MFC clause would retroactively reprice every existing and future deal whenever a single transaction closes at a lower effective rate—even if that lower rate reflects a dramatically different deal structure (e.g., a higher volume commitment, a longer term, or a lower support tier). The cascading repricing effect of an MFC clause would be virtually impossible to manage across a portfolio of dozens of enterprise licensees, each with different terms and renewal dates. The benchmarking-report alternative gives the licensee the transparency it needs to evaluate its pricing in context without creating an enforceable price-matching obligation that would destabilize Verdantia's entire pricing architecture.
Section 21 — Force Majeure
The force majeure provision excuses each party's non-performance of its obligations under the Agreement (other than payment obligations, as discussed below) during the continuance of a force majeure event—defined as any event beyond the affected party's reasonable control, including but not limited to acts of God, natural disasters, pandemics, epidemics, war, terrorism, government actions or orders, labor disputes, power outages, internet or telecommunications failures, cyberattacks (to the extent not caused by the affected party's failure to maintain commercially reasonable security measures), and similar events.
(a) Preferred/Starting Position: Standard mutual force majeure clause excusing performance (other than payment obligations) during the continuance of a force majeure event, subject to the following conditions: (i) the affected party must provide prompt written notice of the force majeure event to the other party, including the nature of the event, its expected duration, and the steps being taken to mitigate its impact; (ii) the affected party must use commercially reasonable efforts to resume performance as soon as practicable; and (iii) if the force majeure event continues for more than 90 consecutive days, either party may terminate the Agreement upon 30 days' written notice. Payment obligations are expressly excluded from the force majeure defense—the licensee's obligation to pay fees is not excused or suspended by a force majeure event affecting either party.
(b) Acceptable Middle-Ground: Accept a carve-out providing that force majeure does not excuse the licensee's obligation to pay fees that accrued prior to the commencement of the force majeure event. Fees that accrued before the force majeure event represent compensation for services already delivered and remain payable regardless of the subsequent occurrence of a force majeure event. However, fees accruing during an extended force majeure event (one exceeding 30 consecutive days) may be suspended on a pro-rata basis for the period during which Verdantia is unable to deliver the Verdantia Nexus API service due to the force majeure event. The pro-rata suspension applies only to the period of actual service unavailability, not to the entire duration of the force majeure event if Verdantia is able to restore partial or full service during the event.
(c) Walk-Away/Floor: The fee-payment carve-out described in the acceptable middle-ground position (pre-accrued fees remain payable) is acceptable and should be conceded if the counterparty requests it—this is a reasonable position that Verdantia can live with. Do not agree to any provision under which force majeure excuses all payment obligations retroactively (i.e., a provision that would excuse payment of fees that were already due and payable before the force majeure event commenced). Pre-accrued fees represent services already rendered, and retroactive excusal of these fees would result in unjust enrichment of the licensee at Verdantia's expense.
(d) Rationale: Verdantia incurs infrastructure costs regardless of the occurrence of a force majeure event. Cloud infrastructure reservations, personnel costs, monitoring services, and other fixed operational expenses continue to accrue during a force majeure event. Pre-accrued fees represent services that Verdantia has already delivered to the licensee, and the licensee has already received the benefit of those services. The pro-rata suspension for extended outages (exceeding 30 consecutive days) is a fair concession that prevents the licensee from paying for a service it cannot access over an extended period, while preserving Verdantia's right to be compensated for services already delivered and for periods during which the service remains available.

Section 23 — General Negotiation Principles
The following principles govern the overall approach to API licensing negotiations and should be followed by all negotiators in every transaction:
Lead with the Preferred/Starting Position. All terms in the initial draft sent to the counterparty should reflect the Preferred/Starting Position set forth in this Playbook. The initial draft is Verdantia's opening position and should be presented as Verdantia's standard market terms. Do not preemptively concede to the Acceptable Middle-Ground position in the initial draft, even if the negotiator anticipates that the counterparty will request movement. Starting at the preferred position preserves negotiating room and allows Verdantia to trade concessions strategically.
Trade concessions; do not give them unilaterally. Every concession Verdantia makes should be made in exchange for a reciprocal concession from the counterparty. Where possible, package concessions across multiple clause categories to demonstrate movement across the deal while preserving floor positions on high-priority items. For example, a concession on the burst allowance (from 150% to 175%) might be traded for the counterparty's acceptance of Verdantia's preferred position on the maintenance window (4 hours instead of 3 hours) or on the SLA credit cap (30% instead of 50%). The goal is to create a sense of mutual give-and-take that advances the negotiation toward closure without eroding Verdantia's position on critical terms.
Prioritize the highest-priority positions. The following positions are designated as the highest-priority items in this Playbook and should be the last items conceded, if they are conceded at all:
(i) Uptime SLA (99.9%) — Section 6. Do not concede to 99.95% or higher.
(ii) Derived Data ownership — Section 9. Do not assign ownership to the licensee under any circumstances.
(iii) Feedback ownership — Section 10. Verdantia retains sole and exclusive ownership. No middle ground.
(iv) Authorized-use qualifier on IP indemnity — Section 11.1. Non-negotiable.
(v) MFC clause rejection — Section 20. Rejected categorically in all transactions.
These five positions represent Verdantia's core strategic interests and competitive advantages in the API licensing market. Concessions on these items would have systemic implications across Verdantia's entire customer portfolio and product strategy, far exceeding the value of any single transaction.
Document all concessions. Every concession made during negotiations—whether at or within the Acceptable Middle-Ground range—must be documented in the Negotiation Tracker, which is maintained on Verdantia's Legal SharePoint site at: Legal > Active Deals > [Matter Reference]. The Negotiation Tracker should include the date of the concession, the clause category, the position conceded from and the position conceded to, the reciprocal concession obtained from the counterparty (if any), and the name of the person who approved the concession. Completed Negotiation Trackers are reviewed following signature of the Agreement and are used as inputs to the semi-annual Playbook review process to ensure that the Playbook remains aligned with market dynamics and Verdantia's evolving commercial position.
