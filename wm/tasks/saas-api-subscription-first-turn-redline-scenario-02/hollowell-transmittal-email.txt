I hope this message finds you well. I am writing on behalf of our client, Hollowell Industries Group LLC ("Hollowell"), in connection with the proposed API Access and Use Agreement for the Verdantia Nexus API (Hollowell Matter Ref. HIG-DT-2025-312).

Please find attached Hollowell's first markup of the agreement, redlined against Verdantia's initial draft circulated on June 6, 2025. The markup contains 34 proposed changes. Derek Calloway (Senior Corporate Counsel, Hollowell) and Rachel Fernsby (VP, Digital Transformation, Hollowell) have each reviewed and approved the markup from both the legal and business side. I have copied Marcus Trevino at Linden & Foss for his convenience.

I wanted to provide a roadmap of Hollowell's principal positions across the markup to facilitate your review. Our changes are organized thematically below.

**1. Derived Data and Intellectual Property**

Ownership of Derived Data generated from Hollowell's Input Data is a critical requirement for Hollowell. The markup proposes that Hollowell retain sole ownership of such Derived Data, rather than Verdantia owning it with a term-limited internal-use license back to Hollowell, as contemplated in the current draft. Separately, with respect to feedback and suggestions, we have proposed a mutual license framework in place of the automatic assignment of feedback as Verdantia IP. We believe this structure more appropriately reflects each party's contributions and incentives.

**2. Service Levels and Uptime**

Given that the Verdantia Nexus API will be integrated into Hollowell's internal supply chain management platform (Hollowell SCM) as a production-grade dependency, Hollowell requires commercially reasonable SLA commitments that reflect that level of reliance. Specifically:

- *Uptime:* Hollowell's markup proposes 99.95% uptime (approximately 21.6 minutes of maximum unscheduled downtime per month), compared to the draft's 99.9%.
- *SLA Credits:* We propose credits of 10% of the monthly fee ($105,000) per 0.1% shortfall below the committed uptime threshold, with credits capped at 100% of the monthly fee (i.e., up to $105,000 per month). The current draft provides for 5% per 0.1% shortfall, capped at 30%.
- *Scheduled Maintenance:* Hollowell's markup proposes a maximum of 2 hours per month, with 5 business days' advance written notice, in place of the draft's 4-hour window with 72 hours' notice.

**3. Financial and Payment Terms**

- *Payment Terms:* Hollowell proposes Net 45 from invoice date, in place of the draft's Net 30. The quarterly-in-advance invoicing cadence at $315,000 per quarter remains unchanged.
- *Late-Payment Interest:* We propose the lesser of 1.0% per month or the applicable statutory maximum, compared to the draft's 1.5% per month.
- *Overage Rate:* Hollowell proposes $0.002 per API call above the base tier of 5,000,000 calls per month, compared to the draft rate of $0.004 per call.
- *Burst Allowance:* We have requested a 200% burst allowance, in place of the 150% allowance in the current draft.
- *Most-Favored-Customer Clause:* Hollowell has inserted a most-favored-customer pricing parity clause to ensure that the pricing and economic terms remain competitive relative to similarly situated customers.

**4. Liability and Indemnification**

Hollowell's positions in this section are consistent with its enterprise procurement standards for agreements of this nature and scope:

- *General Liability Cap:* Hollowell proposes a cap of 2× the annual license fee ($2,520,000), compared to the draft's 1× cap ($1,260,000).
- *Data Breach Liability:* Hollowell's markup proposes that liability arising from data breaches be uncapped.
- *Consequential Damages:* We have requested a carve-out from the mutual consequential damages waiver for claims arising from intellectual property infringement and data breaches.
- *Data-Breach Indemnity:* The markup adds a data-breach indemnity obligation on Verdantia's part, triggered by Verdantia's negligence.
- *Cyber-Liability Insurance:* Hollowell requires Verdantia to maintain a minimum $10,000,000 cyber-liability insurance policy for the duration of the agreement.

**5. Termination and Post-Termination**

Hollowell seeks flexibility on termination provisions to align with its internal budget and procurement cycles:

- *Termination for Convenience:* Hollowell proposes 90 days' prior written notice with no early-termination fee, in place of the draft's 180-day notice period and early-termination fee equal to 50% of remaining fees in the then-current annual period.
- *Cure Period for Material Breach:* Hollowell proposes 45 days to cure, compared to the draft's 30 days.
- *Post-Termination Transition:* Hollowell requests 90 days of read-only API access at no additional charge following expiration or termination, in order to support an orderly transition.
- *Data Return:* We note that both parties appear to be aligned on a 30-day data return period, and we have not proposed changes on this point.

**6. Governing Law and Dispute Resolution**

Hollowell's markup proposes New York governing law and binding arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association, seated in Manhattan. This is consistent with Hollowell's standard dispute resolution framework and replaces the draft's choice of Delaware law and exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery.

**7. Assignment**

Hollowell's markup adds a right for either party to assign the agreement without consent in connection with a merger, acquisition, or sale of all or substantially all of the assigning party's assets.

**8. Subcontractors**

The markup requires Verdantia to obtain specific prior written consent before engaging any subcontractors in connection with performance under the agreement. This replaces the general authorization with 30 days' notice contained in the current draft.

**9. Audit Rights**

Hollowell proposes the right to conduct up to two audits per calendar year, upon 15 days' prior written notice, with audit costs borne by Verdantia. The current draft provides for one audit per year with 30 days' notice at Hollowell's expense.
