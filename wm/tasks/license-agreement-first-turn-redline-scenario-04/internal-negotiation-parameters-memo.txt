1. Background and Purpose
On March 14, 2025, Veridian transmitted to Halcyon Industrial Holdings, LLC ("Halcyon" or "Licensee") our standard-form enterprise license agreement for the Veridian OnePlatform suite, designated as DRAFT – Veridian Template – v1.0 (03/14/2025) (the "Template Agreement" or "v1.0"). The Template Agreement was delivered substantially on Veridian's standard commercial terms, reflecting the pricing and legal framework that has been used — with customary deal-specific adjustments — across all of Veridian's Tier 1 enterprise deployments over the past three years.
On June 9, 2025, Veridian received Halcyon's first markup of the Template Agreement, designated as DRAFT – HIH First Markup – v2.0 (06/09/2025) (the "Halcyon Markup" or "v2.0"). The Halcyon Markup was prepared by Halcyon's outside counsel, Prewitt, Nakamura & Oakes LLP, with Catherine Prewitt serving as lead partner on the engagement. The Halcyon Markup is an extensive redline that introduces a number of significant modifications to the Template Agreement's risk allocation, commercial structure, and intellectual property framework.
In a separate conversation between Derek Yun and Halcyon's Chief Information Officer, Brian Kasprzak, Mr. Kasprzak communicated that Halcyon considers this transaction strategically important to its enterprise technology modernization initiative and that Halcyon is committed to reaching a mutually acceptable agreement. However, Mr. Kasprzak also emphasized that the positions reflected in the Halcyon Markup were directed by Halcyon's board of directors and outside counsel, and that Halcyon's negotiating team will need substantive engagement on each issue before agreeing to withdraw or modify any of the marked-up provisions. Veridian should therefore anticipate a thorough and potentially multi-round negotiation process.
The purpose of this memorandum is to provide Maren Solberg with comprehensive internal guidance on Veridian's acceptable, preferred, and firm positions on each substantive issue raised in the Halcyon Markup. This memorandum is organized issue-by-issue to support Maren's preparation of the counter-turn redline and accompanying cover note. Each section below identifies the relevant provision of the Halcyon Markup, summarizes the change proposed by Halcyon, and sets forth Veridian's response with clear authorization levels.

2. Governing Law and Dispute Resolution
2.1 Governing Law
Halcyon Markup Reference: Section 13.1 — Halcyon's counsel has replaced "State of Delaware" with "State of Texas" as the governing law jurisdiction.
Veridian Position — Firm Preference for Delaware, with New York as Acceptable Compromise:
Veridian has a strong preference for retaining Delaware as the governing law of the Agreement. Delaware is Veridian's state of incorporation, the jurisdiction under which all of Veridian's template agreements are drafted and analyzed, and the state whose case law most directly informs Veridian's standard commercial terms. Veridian's legal team has extensive experience litigating and resolving disputes under Delaware law, and the Agreement's provisions — particularly those relating to limitation of liability, indemnification, and intellectual property — have been drafted with Delaware's statutory and common-law framework in mind. Changing the governing law to an unfamiliar jurisdiction introduces unnecessary interpretive risk across multiple provisions of the Agreement.
If Halcyon refuses to accept Delaware governing law, Veridian is prepared to offer New York as a neutral compromise jurisdiction. New York is a widely accepted governing-law choice for complex commercial agreements and software licensing transactions. New York courts and practitioners have developed a substantial and well-reasoned body of case law addressing licensing disputes, limitation-of-liability provisions, and intellectual property issues. The selection of New York law would be commercially reasonable for both parties and would avoid any perception that the Agreement favors one party's home jurisdiction over the other.
Texas governing law is not acceptable. Veridian's legal team has not reviewed the Agreement's provisions under Texas law, and a number of the Agreement's clauses — including the limitation-of-liability framework, the consequential-damages waiver, and the audit provisions — may be interpreted differently or may require material revision under Texas statutory and common-law principles. Accepting Texas governing law would necessitate a comprehensive legal review of the entire Agreement, which is neither practical nor warranted given the availability of Delaware and New York as established alternatives.
Instruction: Redline Section 13.1 to restore Delaware as the governing law. In the cover note, indicate that Veridian is open to discussing New York as an alternative if Halcyon has concerns about the selection of Delaware, but that Texas is not a jurisdiction Veridian can accept for this Agreement.
2.2 Dispute Resolution
Halcyon Markup Reference: Section 13.2 — Halcyon's counsel has replaced the exclusive Delaware court jurisdiction clause with a mandatory arbitration provision requiring (i) binding arbitration under the American Arbitration Association ("AAA") Commercial Arbitration Rules, (ii) a three-arbitrator panel, and (iii) Houston, Texas as the seat of arbitration.
Veridian Position — Firm Rejection of Mandatory Arbitration for IP Disputes; Tiered Resolution Mechanism Acceptable:
Veridian will not accept mandatory arbitration as the sole dispute-resolution mechanism for the Agreement. In particular, Veridian's position is firm and non-negotiable that court jurisdiction must be preserved for any claims involving Licensor Technology, intellectual property ownership, or injunctive or other equitable relief. Intellectual property disputes — including claims for misappropriation of Veridian's source code, unauthorized use of Licensor Technology outside the scope of the license grant, or infringement of Veridian's proprietary rights — require access to judicial remedies, including preliminary and permanent injunctive relief, that cannot be effectively or efficiently obtained through arbitration. Arbitral tribunals lack the institutional mechanisms to issue enforceable injunctive orders with the speed and procedural safeguards that a court of competent jurisdiction provides.
Veridian is, however, open to binding arbitration under the AAA Commercial Arbitration Rules for a defined category of disputes: specifically, payment disputes where the amount in controversy is less than five hundred thousand dollars ($500,000). Arbitration of smaller-dollar payment disputes can be a cost-effective and efficient alternative to full-scale litigation, and Veridian recognizes that Halcyon may prefer the confidentiality and procedural flexibility of an arbitral forum for such matters.
Accordingly, Veridian's counter-proposal is a tiered dispute-resolution clause structured as follows:
(i) Good-Faith Negotiation. The parties shall first attempt in good faith to resolve any dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement through direct negotiation between designated senior representatives of each party. The negotiation period shall be thirty (30) days following written notice from the disputing party identifying the dispute and the relief sought.
(ii) Binding Arbitration for Qualifying Payment Disputes. If the dispute involves exclusively a payment obligation under Article 4 or Schedule A and the total amount in controversy is less than five hundred thousand dollars ($500,000), and if the dispute is not resolved through the negotiation process described in clause (i), either party may submit the dispute to binding arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association under its then-current Commercial Arbitration Rules. The arbitration shall be conducted by a single arbitrator (not a three-arbitrator panel) selected in accordance with AAA procedures. The seat of arbitration shall be the city and state agreed upon for the governing-law jurisdiction (i.e., Wilmington, Delaware, or New York, New York, as applicable under Section 13.1).
(iii) Exclusive Court Jurisdiction for All Other Disputes. For all disputes not subject to arbitration under clause (ii) — including, without limitation, disputes involving intellectual property, Licensor Technology, license scope, equitable relief, confidentiality, or any dispute where the amount in controversy equals or exceeds five hundred thousand dollars ($500,000) — the parties shall submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in the jurisdiction selected under Section 13.1. Each party irrevocably waives any objection to venue and any claim of inconvenient forum.
With respect to the arbitrator panel, if arbitration applies for qualifying payment disputes, Veridian's strong preference is for a single arbitrator rather than a three-arbitrator panel. A three-arbitrator panel substantially increases the cost and duration of the arbitral process, which is contrary to the efficiency rationale for routing smaller payment disputes to arbitration in the first instance. A single arbitrator is appropriate for disputes in the sub-$500,000 range.
Instruction: Redline Section 13.2 to replace the Halcyon-drafted mandatory arbitration clause with the tiered dispute-resolution clause described above. Delete all references to Houston, Texas as the seat of arbitration and to a three-arbitrator panel. Retain express language preserving each party's right to seek provisional or injunctive relief from a court of competent jurisdiction without first submitting to the negotiation or arbitration process.
3. License Grant, Affiliate Sublicensing, and User Model
3.1 Affiliate Sublicensing
Halcyon Markup Reference: New Section 2.1(a) — Halcyon's counsel has added a new provision granting Licensee the right to sublicense access to the Platform to its "Affiliates" at no additional fee and without any requirement that Affiliates execute a joinder or acknowledgment of the Agreement's terms.
Veridian Position — Preferred: Sublicensing Acceptable Under Three Conditions:
Veridian recognizes that Halcyon operates through a network of subsidiary and affiliated entities and that extending Platform access to those entities is a legitimate business requirement. Accordingly, Veridian is prepared to accept Affiliate sublicensing, but only under all three of the following conditions, each of which is integral to Veridian's risk allocation and pricing framework:
(a) Definition of "Affiliate." The term "Affiliate" must be limited to entities in which Halcyon Industrial Holdings, LLC holds more than fifty percent (>50%) direct or indirect ownership of the outstanding voting interests. This is consistent with the existing definition in Section 1.1 of the Template Agreement. Halcyon's markup has modified the ownership threshold to "fifty percent (50%) or more," which would bring in entities where Halcyon holds exactly 50% of voting interests — i.e., joint ventures and parity co-investments where Halcyon does not exercise sole controlling influence. The ">50%" threshold must be restored. An entity in which Halcyon holds a non-controlling 50% interest is not an "Affiliate" for purposes of the license grant, and extending Platform access to such entities without Veridian's separate written consent is not acceptable.
(b) Written Joinder Agreement. Each Affiliate must execute a written joinder agreement in a form reasonably acceptable to Veridian before being permitted to access the Platform. The joinder agreement will bind the Affiliate to all terms and conditions of the Agreement, including without limitation the use restrictions, confidentiality obligations, data provisions, and intellectual property protections. Veridian's form joinder will be attached as an exhibit to the Agreement. This requirement ensures that Veridian has a direct contractual relationship with each entity accessing its Platform and can enforce the Agreement's terms against Affiliates directly, rather than relying solely on Halcyon's obligation to procure Affiliate compliance.
(c) Affiliate Users Count Toward Named User Cap. All Authorized Users at Affiliates must count toward the Named User Cap agreed upon in Schedule A. Affiliate access does not expand the total user entitlement under the Agreement. If Halcyon and its Affiliates collectively require more than the Named User Cap, additional Named Users must be purchased under the terms of Schedule A, Section A.6.
Reject: The "at no additional fee" language inserted by Halcyon must be deleted. Affiliate usage is not provided as a free add-on above and beyond the negotiated license fee. Affiliate usage is included in — and limited by — the Named User Cap and the corresponding annual license fee set forth in Schedule A. The fee structure proposed in Section 4 below reflects a per-user economic model that accounts for the total population of Named Users across Halcyon and its Affiliates.
Instruction: Restore the sublicense restriction in Section 2.3 of the Template Agreement, but add a cross-reference to the new Affiliate sublicense provision in Section 2.1(a), making clear that the general prohibition on sublicensing is subject to the Affiliate sublicense right as specifically set forth therein. Delete the "at no additional fee" and "without joinder" language. Restore the ">50%" ownership threshold in the Section 1.1 definition of "Affiliate."
3.2 User Count and Fee Adjustment

This is a fundamental and non-negotiable position. The Named User licensing model is the foundation of Veridian's pricing architecture, infrastructure capacity planning, and resource allocation methodology for all OnePlatform enterprise deployments. Each Named User represents a discrete allocation of compute resources, storage capacity, and support bandwidth within Veridian's cloud infrastructure. Permitting "unlimited Authorized Users" would eliminate Veridian's ability to forecast and provision the infrastructure required to deliver committed performance levels, would undermine the per-user economics upon which the annual license fee is calculated, and would fundamentally alter the commercial bargain reflected in the Template Agreement.
Halcyon's redefined "Authorized User" concept — which would encompass any employee, contractor, or agent of Licensee or any Affiliate, without any requirement for individual credentialing, identification, or registration — is similarly rejected. The Named User model requires that each user be a specific, identified individual who is assigned a unique set of login credentials. Credential sharing is prohibited under the Agreement. This credentialing requirement is not merely an administrative formality; it is essential for security, audit integrity, usage tracking, and compliance verification.
Preferred Counter-Position on User Count: Veridian recognizes that Halcyon's user population may exceed the 1,500 Named Users proposed in v1.0. Accordingly, Veridian is willing to increase the Named User Cap from 1,500 to 2,500 Named Users. This represents a significant increase — approximately 67% above the original cap — and should provide Halcyon with substantial flexibility to deploy the Platform across its organization and qualifying Affiliates.
Adjusted Year 1 Annual License Fee: At the 2,500 Named User level, the Year 1 annual license fee would be $3,168,000.00, reflecting per-user economics consistent with the original rate structure in v1.0. This fee has been calculated by Veridian's finance team and validated against the cost model for infrastructure provisioning at the 2,500-user tier. The quarterly installment under the advance-payment schedule (see Section 4.2 below) would be $792,000.00.
Instruction: Restore Section 1.14 ("Named User" — defined as a specific, identified individual who is assigned unique login credentials by Licensee's administrator; credential sharing is prohibited). Restore Section 1.15 ("Named User Cap" — the maximum number of Named Users authorized to access the Platform at any time during the applicable Term, as set forth in Schedule A; revised to 2,500). Revise Schedule A, Sections A.2 and A.3 to reflect the 2,500 Named User Cap and the adjusted Year 1 annual license fee of $3,168,000.00. Restore Schedule A, Section A.6 ("Additional Named Users") with per-user pricing to be specified in the Schedule. Delete Halcyon's redefined "Authorized User" concept and all references to "unlimited" users throughout the Agreement.

4. Fees and Payment
4.1 Fee Escalator
Halcyon Markup Reference: Section 4.2 / Schedule A — Halcyon's counsel has proposed an annual fee escalator equal to the lesser of CPI-U or 2%.
Veridian Position — Preferred Counter-Position: CPI-U with 3% Floor and 5% Cap:
Veridian proposes an annual fee escalator tied to the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), U.S. City Average, All Items, as published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, using the 12-month trailing percentage change for the period ending three (3) calendar months before the applicable anniversary date of the Agreement. The escalator would be subject to a floor of three percent (3%) per annum and a cap of five percent (5%) per annum.
The rationale for this structure is as follows:
•  3% Floor. Veridian's internal cost projections for infrastructure, personnel, and platform maintenance reflect annual cost increases that consistently exceed 3%. The floor ensures that Veridian's revenue from this account grows at a rate that at least partially offsets these cost increases, protecting Veridian's margin and its ability to sustain investment in the Platform. Veridian's board-approved revenue growth commitments also assume a minimum annual escalator of 3% across the enterprise customer base.
•  5% Cap. The cap provides Halcyon with meaningful upside protection against inflationary spikes. In the event of an extraordinary inflationary period in which CPI-U exceeds 5%, Halcyon's annual fee increase would be limited to 5%, providing budget predictability and limiting Halcyon's exposure to macroeconomic volatility beyond its control.
•  CPI-U Linkage. The use of CPI-U as the reference index acknowledges Halcyon's desire for an objective, third-party benchmark rather than a purely discretionary escalator. The CPI-U is widely used in commercial contracts, is publicly available, and is transparent to both parties.
A floor of 3% per annum represents Veridian's minimum acceptable concession on this issue. Veridian will not agree to any escalator formula that permits the annual increase to fall below 3%, regardless of the CPI-U reading in any given period.
Reject: The "lesser of CPI-U or 2%" formulation proposed by Halcyon is rejected. This formulation effectively caps the annual fee increase at 2% regardless of the actual rate of inflation, because the "lesser of" structure means that whenever CPI-U exceeds 2%, the 2% cap controls, and whenever CPI-U is below 2%, the lower CPI-U rate controls. Under current economic projections, this would result in annual fee increases well below Veridian's cost-of-service increase rate, eroding the economic value of the Agreement over the five-year term.
Instruction: Redline Section 4.2 and Schedule A to replace the Halcyon-proposed escalator with the CPI-U formula described above, including the 3% floor and 5% cap. Include the specific CPI-U reference (Bureau of Labor Statistics, U.S. City Average, All Items, 12-month trailing change) and the calculation methodology (measured as of the period ending three months before the applicable anniversary date).
4.2 Payment Terms
Halcyon Markup Reference: Section 4.3 / Schedule A — Halcyon's counsel has proposed semi-annual billing in arrears with net 60-day payment terms.
Veridian Position — Firm: Quarterly-in-Advance Invoicing Is Non-Negotiable:
Veridian's position on the invoicing and payment cadence is firm and non-negotiable: annual license fees must be invoiced and paid quarterly in advance. Veridian's revenue recognition policies, cash flow planning, credit-risk management framework, and financial reporting to investors and lenders all depend on the advance-payment model. Payment in arrears introduces collection risk, revenue-recognition timing complications, and cash flow variability that are inconsistent with Veridian's financial operations and treasury management practices. A semi-annual billing cycle further compounds these issues by concentrating payment obligations into two large installments rather than four more manageable ones, increasing the potential impact of any single late or missed payment.
Acceptable Compromise on Payment Window: Veridian recognizes that Halcyon, as a large industrial conglomerate, may have internal accounts-payable cycles that make a strict 30-day payment window challenging. Accordingly, Veridian is willing to extend the payment window from net thirty (30) days to net forty-five (45) days from the date of each invoice. This represents a meaningful accommodation that provides Halcyon with an additional fifteen days to process each quarterly payment while preserving the advance-payment structure.
Reject: Semi-annual billing cycle — rejected. Payment in arrears — rejected. Net 60-day payment terms — rejected. The combination of semi-annual billing, arrears payment, and net 60-day terms proposed by Halcyon would result in Veridian providing up to eight months of service before receiving the corresponding payment. This is commercially unacceptable and inconsistent with industry norms for enterprise SaaS agreements.
Instruction: Retain the original Section 4.3 structure (quarterly invoicing, payment in advance). Change "net thirty (30) days from the date of invoice" to "net forty-five (45) days from the date of invoice." Retain the quarterly installment format. At the adjusted Year 1 annual license fee of $3,168,000.00, each quarterly installment would be $792,000.00. Revise Schedule A accordingly. The first quarterly installment should be due on or before the forty-fifth (45th) day following the Effective Date, with subsequent installments due on or before the forty-fifth (45th) day following the first day of each subsequent calendar quarter during the Term.
5. Liability and Damages
5.1 Aggregate Liability Cap
Halcyon Markup Reference: Section 11.1 — Halcyon's counsel has proposed increasing the aggregate liability cap from twelve (12) months' trailing fees to two times (2×) the total fees paid and payable over the full initial Term.
Veridian Position — Preferred Counter-Position: 18-Month Trailing-Fee Cap:
Veridian is willing to increase the aggregate liability cap beyond the 12-month trailing-fee standard in the Template Agreement. Specifically, Veridian proposes that the aggregate liability cap for each party be equal to the total fees actually paid by Licensee in the eighteen (18)-month period immediately preceding the event giving rise to the claim (or, if fewer than eighteen months have elapsed since the Effective Date, the total fees actually paid during such shorter period, annualized to reflect an 18-month equivalent). This represents a 50% increase over the original cap and provides Halcyon with materially greater protection while remaining within the range of industry-standard liability caps for enterprise SaaS agreements of this scale and value.
Firm Position — Rejection of 2× Full-Term Cap: Halcyon's proposed cap of "two times (2×) the total fees paid and payable over the full initial Term" is commercially unacceptable. At the adjusted Year 1 annual license fee of $3,168,000.00, and assuming standard escalator-driven increases over the five-year initial term, total fees over the initial term would exceed approximately $17 million. A 2× multiplier on that figure would result in an aggregate liability cap in excess of $34 million — a level of exposure that is grossly disproportionate to the value of the Agreement in any single-incident scenario, far exceeds industry norms for SaaS agreements, and would create an uninsurable risk for Veridian. Moreover, the "paid and payable" formulation includes fees that have not yet been collected, meaning the cap would be at its maximum from the Effective Date, before Veridian has received any meaningful portion of the total contract value.
Instruction: Redline Section 11.1 to reflect the 18-month trailing-fee cap. Retain the all-caps formatting required by applicable law for limitation-of-liability provisions. The cap should apply on an aggregate, per-party basis to all claims arising under or in connection with the Agreement, subject only to the exceptions listed in Section 11.3 (as modified per Section 5.3 below).
5.2 Consequential Damages Waiver Carve-Outs
Halcyon Markup Reference: Section 11.2 — Halcyon's counsel has added four carve-outs to the mutual consequential-damages waiver, pursuant to which the waiver would not apply to: (a) breaches of Article 5 (Confidentiality), (b) Licensor's IP indemnity obligations under Section 10.1, (c) Licensor's breach of data-security obligations under Section 6.4, and (d) Licensee's lost profits arising from Platform downtime exceeding SLA thresholds.
Veridian Position — Accept Two Carve-Outs, Reject Two:
Veridian is prepared to accept two of the four proposed carve-outs and to reject the remaining two. The analysis is as follows:
(a) Breaches of Article 5 (Confidentiality) — ACCEPT. Veridian is willing to carve out breaches of confidentiality from the consequential-damages waiver, provided that the carve-out applies mutually (i.e., to breaches by either party). A breach of confidentiality can result in competitive harm, loss of proprietary advantage, and reputational damage that are inherently consequential in nature. Excluding such claims from the consequential-damages waiver is consistent with market practice and is appropriate given the sensitivity of the information exchanged under the Agreement, including Veridian's source code, architecture documentation, and pricing data.
(b) Licensor's IP Indemnity Obligations under Section 10.1 — REJECT. The IP indemnity is already excepted from the aggregate liability cap under Section 11.3 of the Template Agreement. Adding a consequential-damages carve-out on top of the cap exception would create a compounding of exposure: Veridian would be liable for uncapped consequential damages in connection with IP indemnity claims. This level of exposure is excessive, commercially unreasonable, and not consistent with Veridian's risk allocation framework. The IP indemnity is further governed by the sole-remedy/mitigation clause in Section 10.2 (see Section 6.2 below), which provides a structured and balanced remedial framework. Layering unlimited consequential damages onto this framework would render the mitigation hierarchy meaningless. This carve-out is rejected.
(c) Licensor's Breach of Data-Security Obligations under Section 6.4 — ACCEPT. Veridian is willing to carve out Licensor's breach of its data-security obligations from the consequential-damages waiver. Data-security breaches can result in regulatory fines, notification costs, credit-monitoring expenses, and other damages that are consequential in nature but are a foreseeable and legitimate concern for an enterprise customer entrusting its data to Veridian's infrastructure. This carve-out is consistent with Veridian's position in a number of recent enterprise agreements and reflects the market trend toward greater vendor accountability for data security.
(d) Licensee's Lost Profits from Platform Downtime Exceeding SLA Thresholds — REJECT. This carve-out is rejected in its entirety. The SLA credit mechanism set forth in Schedule C (as counter-proposed in Section 8 below) is designed to be the sole and exclusive remedy for any failure to meet the uptime commitment. Permitting Licensee to pursue lost-profits claims for downtime — in addition to receiving SLA credits — would create uncapped, unquantifiable exposure for Veridian and would fundamentally undermine the predictability and finality of the SLA framework. Lost-profits claims arising from downtime are inherently speculative, highly variable depending on Halcyon's business operations on any given day, and virtually impossible for Veridian to forecast or insure against. The SLA credit mechanism provides a fair, pre-agreed remedy that adequately compensates Licensee for service interruptions. This is a critical position that Veridian must maintain.
Instruction: Redline Section 11.2 to include carve-outs (a) (Confidentiality — mutual) and (c) (Data Security — Licensor only) as exceptions to the consequential-damages waiver. Delete carve-outs (b) (IP Indemnity) and (d) (Lost Profits from Downtime) in their entirety. Include an explanatory comment in the redline or cover note for each rejected carve-out, briefly stating the commercial rationale for rejection.
5.3 Exceptions to Aggregate Cap
Halcyon Markup Reference: Section 11.3 — Halcyon's counsel has added two new exceptions to the aggregate liability cap: (d) Licensor's IP indemnity obligations under Section 10.1, and (e) Licensor's data-security obligations under Section 6.4.
Veridian Position — Accept Data Security Exception, Reject Uncapped IP Indemnity:
Veridian is prepared to accept the addition of Licensor's data-security obligations (Section 6.4) to the list of exceptions to the aggregate liability cap. This is consistent with Veridian's acceptance of the data-security carve-out from the consequential-damages waiver (see Section 5.2(c) above) and reflects Veridian's confidence in its data-security infrastructure and practices. Adding Section 6.4 to the cap exceptions ensures alignment between the consequential-damages framework and the aggregate-cap framework with respect to data-security obligations.
However, Veridian rejects the proposed exception for IP indemnity obligations under Section 10.1, to the extent that Halcyon's intent is to make the IP indemnity entirely uncapped — i.e., not subject to any monetary limitation whatsoever. The existing Section 11.3 of the Template Agreement already excepts indemnification obligations generally from the aggregate cap, but the IP indemnity remains subject to the sole-remedy/mitigation framework set forth in Section 10.2 (see Section 6 below). That framework provides a structured remedial path — modify, replace, procure a license, or terminate and refund — that effectively caps Veridian's exposure by channeling the remedy through operational solutions rather than open-ended monetary damages. Removing the IP indemnity from the aggregate cap while simultaneously eliminating the sole-remedy clause (as Halcyon has proposed in its deletion of Section 10.2) would create a scenario in which Veridian faces truly unlimited, unconstrained liability for IP claims — a position that is commercially untenable and uninsurable.
Instruction: Accept the addition of Section 11.3(e) (data-security obligations under Section 6.4). Reject Section 11.3(d) to the extent it would make the IP indemnity uncapped beyond the existing structure. In the redline, retain the existing general exception for indemnification obligations but add a cross-reference to the sole-remedy/mitigation clause in Section 10.2 to confirm that the IP indemnity, while excepted from the aggregate cap, remains subject to the remedial framework of Section 10.2.
6. IP Indemnity
6.1 Scope of IP Indemnity
Halcyon Markup Reference: Section 10.1 — Halcyon's counsel has expanded the scope of the IP indemnity to include (i) third-party claims of trade-secret misappropriation (in addition to patent and copyright infringement) and (ii) a continuing post-termination defense obligation requiring Veridian to defend, indemnify, and hold Halcyon harmless against IP claims that arise or are asserted after expiration or termination of the Agreement, without temporal limitation.
Veridian Position on Trade-Secret Misappropriation — Acceptable:

No two enterprise agreements are directly comparable on a simple per-user basis, and any attempt to reduce complex, multi-variable pricing to a single "effective per-user fee" metric would be misleading and commercially distortive.
(b) Confidentiality Conflicts. The MFC provision would require Veridian to disclose the pricing terms of its other customer agreements to Halcyon — either directly (to demonstrate compliance) or indirectly (through the fee-adjustment mechanism). Such disclosure would breach the confidentiality obligations Veridian owes to its other customers and would create competitive intelligence risks that are fundamentally inconsistent with Veridian's obligations as a multi-customer platform provider.
(c) Administrative Unworkability. The retroactive adjustment mechanism proposed by Halcyon would require Veridian to continuously monitor its entire portfolio of customer agreements, compare each new deal to the Halcyon agreement on an "effective per-user fee" basis, and retroactively adjust Halcyon's fees if a lower rate is identified. This is administratively unworkable, would create perpetual fee uncertainty, and would complicate Veridian's revenue recognition and financial reporting.
(d) Precedent Risk. Veridian has never agreed to a most-favored-customer clause in any customer agreement. Accepting an MFC in the Halcyon agreement would set an untenable precedent that other enterprise customers would inevitably seek to replicate, resulting in a cascading obligation across Veridian's customer base that would undermine Veridian's pricing flexibility and commercial autonomy.
