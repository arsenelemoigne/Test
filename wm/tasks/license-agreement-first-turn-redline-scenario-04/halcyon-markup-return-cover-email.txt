**Summary of Key Categories of Changes**

Set forth below is a summary of the principal areas in which we have proposed revisions. This summary is intended to be illustrative, not exhaustive; please refer to the redline and accompanying marginal comments for the full scope of Halcyon's proposed changes.

*1. Licensing Model and Scope of Use*

The template contemplates a Named User licensing model with a cap of 1,500 users. We have revised the license grant to transition to an unlimited Authorized User model encompassing Licensee and its Affiliates. Given Halcyon's multi-entity corporate structure across North America, we have also added Affiliate sublicensing rights at no additional fee, as well as assignment and transfer rights upon a change of control (whether by merger, acquisition, or reorganization). Halcyon considers operational flexibility in the license grant to be a threshold priority for this transaction.

*2. Commercial and Payment Terms*

We have proposed adjusting the annual fee escalator by tying it to CPI-U (All Urban Consumers, U.S. City Average) with a hard cap of 2%, in place of the fixed 4% compounding escalator in the template. We have also proposed shifting from quarterly-in-advance invoicing to semi-annual invoicing in arrears, net 60 days, which better aligns with Halcyon's internal procurement and accounts-payable cycle. Given the move to an unlimited-user model that removes the per-seat variable, Halcyon expects the Year 1 annual fee to remain at $2,640,000.

*3. Service Levels and Availability*

We have inserted a new Schedule C formalizing a 99.95% monthly uptime commitment, together with tiered service-level credits and a chronic-failure termination right. Halcyon views robust, enforceable SLA protections as essential given the mission-critical nature of an enterprise-wide ERP deployment of this scope.

*4. Data Ownership, Security, and Residency*

This is among Halcyon's highest-priority areas. Our revisions include: (a) deletion of Licensor's right to use de-identified, aggregated usage data derived from Halcyon's environment; (b) addition of a continental-U.S.-only data residency requirement, which is driven by Halcyon's internal information-security policies and is not subject to waiver; (c) a revised post-termination data return and destruction obligation requiring completion within thirty (30) days following termination or expiration, accompanied by a certified destruction letter executed by an authorized officer of Veridian. We ask that your team give particular attention to the data-governance provisions in the markup, as Halcyon regards them as foundational to the agreement.

*5. Intellectual Property Indemnification*

We have expanded the IP indemnity to cover trade-secret misappropriation claims in addition to patent and copyright infringement. We have also deleted the sole-remedy and mitigation clause set forth in Sections 10.2(a)–(d) of the template, which in Halcyon's view impermissibly constrains the indemnified party's rights. Additionally, we have added a post-termination defense obligation to ensure that Halcyon is not left unprotected in the event the MLA terminates while an IP claim remains pending. The scope and durability of the IP indemnity is a core concern for Halcyon.

*6. Limitation of Liability*

We have increased the aggregate liability cap to two times (2x) the total fees paid and payable over the full initial Term. We have also proposed carve-outs from the consequential-damages waiver for: (i) breaches of the confidentiality obligations; (ii) IP indemnity obligations; (iii) data-security obligations; and (iv) Licensee's lost profits attributable to downtime exceeding the SLA commitment. The IP indemnity and data-security obligations are also proposed as exceptions to the aggregate cap.

*7. Warranties*

The Platform warranty period has been extended from ninety (90) days to eighteen (18) months per release. We have also added a legal-compliance warranty pursuant to which Veridian would warrant ongoing compliance with applicable data-protection, privacy, export-control, and accessibility laws.

*8. Termination Rights*

We have added a reciprocal Licensee termination-for-convenience right, exercisable on ninety (90) days' prior written notice, with a pro-rata refund of prepaid fees and no early-termination fee. Halcyon views flexibility to exit the arrangement as important given the five-year initial term.

*9. Governing Law and Dispute Resolution*

We have changed the governing law from Delaware to Texas and replaced the exclusive Delaware court jurisdiction provision with mandatory arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association ("AAA") under its Commercial Arbitration Rules, with the seat of arbitration in Houston, Texas.

*10. Audit Rights*

We have deleted Article 12 (audit rights) in its entirety. Under the proposed unlimited-user licensing model, the compliance-audit mechanism in the template is no longer necessary or appropriate.

*11. New Provisions*

We have proposed three new provisions:

- **Source-Code Escrow (proposed Section 14.12):** Halcyon proposes that the parties enter into a source-code escrow arrangement with Iron Mountain Intellectual Property Management, Inc. as escrow agent. Release conditions would include Veridian's insolvency, a material uncured breach by Veridian, and discontinuation of the OnePlatform. This is framed as a business-continuity safeguard and is standard in engagements of this scale.

- **Mutual Non-Solicitation (proposed Section 14.13):** A mutual two-year non-solicitation covenant covering personnel directly involved in the performance of the MLA.

- **Most-Favored-Customer Pricing (proposed Section 14.14):** A most-favored-customer clause to ensure that Halcyon receives pricing parity with similarly situated Veridian customers for comparable deployments.

**Halcyon's Stated Priorities**

While Halcyon has proposed positions across all provisions of the MLA, I want to be transparent about the areas that Halcyon views as its highest priorities. They cluster into three groups:

1. **Licensing flexibility** — unlimited Authorized Users, Affiliate sublicensing rights, and change-of-control transfer rights;
2. **Data governance and security** — U.S. data residency, prohibition on secondary use of Halcyon data, and rigorous post-termination data handling; and
3. **Meaningful IP and liability protections** — commensurate with the scale, cost, and operational criticality of an enterprise-wide deployment.

I would also note that while Halcyon has put forward specific proposals on commercial terms — including the fee escalator and payment cadence — it is prepared to engage constructively with Veridian's positions on those points.
