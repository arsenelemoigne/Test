**Summary of Principal Changes**

We provide the following high-level summary of the substantive revisions reflected in the redline. The margin comments offer more granular, section-specific explanations.

**1. Definitions (Section 1)**

We have revised the definition of "Discovery" (Section 1.8) to require confirmation of a Security Incident through reasonable investigation, including preliminary forensic validation, replacing the original "first reasonably suspects or identifies" standard. In addition, we have inserted three new defined terms—"Derivative Works" (Section 1.17), "Pre-Approved Subprocessors" (Section 1.18), and "Residuals" (Section 1.19)—each of which supports substantive changes in the operative sections described below.

**2. Data Use Limitation and Intellectual Property (Section 4)**

The Authorized Purpose in Section 4.1 has been broadened to encompass not only aggregated benchmarking reports but also the development of derivative analytical models and machine-learning training for Luminos's internal and commercial purposes. New Section 4.1A clarifies that Luminos owns all Derivative Works, while Carden retains ownership of the Shared Data Set. New Section 4.5 establishes a Residuals clause permitting Luminos personnel to use general knowledge, ideas, concepts, and techniques retained in unaided memory without restriction, subject to the continued prohibition on re-identification under Section 7.

**3. Data Retention and Deletion (Section 6)**

The retention period in Section 6.1 has been extended from twelve (12) months to thirty-six (36) months from receipt, to support longitudinal benchmarking and model-training use cases. The mandatory-deletion obligation in Section 6.2 has been replaced with a dual-option framework: secure deletion/destruction or, at Luminos's election, Anonymization-in-Place rendering the data permanently and irreversibly anonymous. Conforming edits have been made to the deletion certification language in Section 6.3, and former Section 6.4 (which prohibited Anonymization-in-Place) has been deleted.

**4. Re-Identification Restrictions (Section 7)**

The absolute prohibition on re-identification in Section 7.1 has been retained. Former Section 7.2 ("No Exceptions") has been replaced with a narrow good-faith research exception permitting internal data-quality testing to verify pseudonymization integrity, subject to documented protocols, immediate reversal of any incidental re-linkage, and summary reporting to Carden on request. A conforming edit to the material-breach provision in Section 7.3 excludes activity permitted under the new Section 7.2.

**5. Security Incident Notification (Section 8)**

The notification window in Section 8.1 has been extended from twenty-four (24) hours to seventy-two (72) hours, running from the revised definition of Discovery (i.e., a confirmed breach rather than mere suspicion). Former Section 8.2 has been replaced with language confirming that the seventy-two-hour clock commences only upon such confirmation.

**6. Subprocessors (Section 9)**

The prior-written-consent requirement (withheld in sole discretion) has been replaced with a notice-and-objection mechanism: Luminos will provide thirty (30) days' prior written notice to Carden of any proposed subprocessor engagement; Carden may object within twenty (20) business days; the engagement is deemed approved if no timely objection is received. Three Pre-Approved Subprocessors—Arcanet Cloud Services, PvtShield Ltd., and DataForge Processing Co.—are deemed approved upon execution without a Vendor-Security Assessment, as detailed in new Schedule 1. A twelve-month stability period has been added during which Carden may not revoke consent for Pre-Approved Subprocessors absent a demonstrated Security Incident.

**7. Audit Rights (Section 10)**

Audits have been limited to remote-only assessments, removing the right to conduct on-site inspections. Audit frequency has been reduced from once per calendar year to once per eighteen-month period. The unrestricted regulatory-disclosure right has been replaced with a confidentiality framework requiring Luminos's prior written consent before audit findings may be shared with regulators, subject to a reasonableness standard and an exception for legally compelled disclosure.

**8. Cross-Border Data Transfers (Section 11)**

A specific carve-out has been inserted permitting transfer of the Shared Data Set to Luminos Insights UK Ltd., registered in England & Wales, for processing in the United Kingdom in support of the Authorized Purpose, subject solely to execution of EU/UK Standard Contractual Clauses. Former Sections 11.2 and 11.3 (which required a Transfer Impact Assessment and individual case-by-case approval for every cross-border transfer) have been deleted as inconsistent with the pre-approved UK transfer right.

**9. Term and Renewal (Section 12)**

The initial term has been extended from two (2) years to three (3) years. The automatic renewal period has been extended from one-year increments to two-year increments. The non-renewal notice window has been extended from ninety (90) days to one hundred eighty (180) days.

**10. Indemnification (Section 13)**

Luminos's existing indemnification obligations in Section 13.1 have been retained without change. New Section 13.1A establishes reciprocal indemnification by Carden for third-party claims arising from defects in pseudonymization or unlawful upstream collection of the consumer transaction data.

**11. Limitation of Liability (Section 14)**

The fixed $2,000,000 aggregate cap in Section 14.1 has been replaced with a fee-based cap equal to six (6) months of Data-Access Fees actually paid by Luminos as of the date the claim accrues. The exclusion of indirect damages in Section 14.2 has been broadened to expressly include consequential, incidental, and punitive damages. The carve-outs in Section 14.3 have been narrowed: the separate carve-out for breach of Section 7 (Re-Identification) has been removed, and gross negligence has been removed as a carve-out trigger. Retained carve-outs are limited to indemnification obligations, breach of confidentiality, and fraud or willful misconduct.

**12. Governing Law and Venue (Section 15)**

Governing law has been changed from the State of Delaware to the State of Maryland. Exclusive venue has been changed from the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware to the United States District Court for the District of Maryland.

**Rationale and Guiding Principles**

The proposed changes are driven by three overarching objectives: (a) balanced and proportionate risk allocation between the parties; (b) operational workability for Luminos's analytics platform and global workforce; and (c) alignment with industry-standard data-sharing and privacy practices, including GDPR Article 33 notification timelines and recognized anonymization frameworks. As noted above, the margin comments embedded in the redline provide section-specific explanations, and we are happy to walk through any of the changes in further detail.
