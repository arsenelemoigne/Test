1. Purpose and Scope of This Memorandum
This memorandum defines the specific authority boundaries and walk-away limits authorized for the Carden DSA Negotiation Team (the "Team") in connection with the ongoing negotiation of the Data Sharing Agreement (the "DSA" or "Agreement") with Luminos Insights Group, LLC ("Luminos"). The Team should treat this memorandum as the definitive statement of authorized settlement parameters for all material commercial and privacy terms currently under discussion. Its purpose is to ensure that the Team operates within clearly delineated guardrails during what is expected to be an intensive counter-turn exchange, while preserving Carden Analytics, Inc.'s ("Carden" or the "Company") core risk and privacy commitments.
This memorandum supplements, but does not replace, the standing Carden Third-Party Data Sharing Policy (the "Policy"). Where the Policy sets a non-negotiable floor or ceiling — for example, the absolute prohibition on Re-Identification (Policy § 4) or the maximum permissible data retention period (Policy § 3.1) — this memorandum does not authorize deviation from those thresholds. In the event of any inconsistency between the parameters articulated in this memorandum and the constraints imposed by the Policy, the Policy controls. The Team is expected to be familiar with the Policy in its entirety and to consult it directly when questions arise during negotiations.
This memorandum covers deal-specific parameters that require articulation beyond what the general Policy addresses, including but not limited to: the permissible range for the aggregate liability cap and the prohibition on certain cap formulations; the authorized range for data retention; the scope of acceptable carve-outs from the limitation of liability; concessions available on breach notification timing; subprocessor engagement protocols; audit and regulatory disclosure rights; cross-border transfer prerequisites; the Company's position on residuals clauses; indemnification structure; term and renewal mechanics; data use limitations and derivative works; and governing law and venue. Each of these topics is addressed in a dedicated section below, with explicit identification of the Team's authority ceiling, walk-away points, and escalation triggers.
Any position outside the limits set forth in this memorandum requires escalation to Priya Chandrasekaran, VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy, before it may be communicated to Luminos or its counsel at Whitfield & Sato LLP. This includes oral statements, conditional or hypothetical proposals, and "if/then" frameworks that would commit Carden to a position beyond the stated parameters. The Team should not characterize any position as "tentative" or "subject to approval" as a mechanism for testing positions beyond the authority granted herein; doing so risks creating implied commitments that complicate subsequent escalation discussions.
For ease of reference, each section below identifies: (a) Carden's original draft position; (b) the relevant Luminos counter-position (where applicable); (c) the Team's authorized range of movement; and (d) the walk-away point or escalation trigger. The Team should read this memorandum in conjunction with the annotated comparison chart circulated separately by this office.
2. Liability Cap Parameters
2.1 Original Draft Position
Carden's Initial Draft (Section 14.1) established the aggregate liability cap for claims arising under or in connection with the Agreement at the lesser of (i) actual direct damages incurred by the non-breaching party and (ii) Two Million Dollars ($2,000,000). This position was selected to align with the Company's standard risk allocation for data-sharing arrangements involving de-identified consumer data sets of comparable volume and sensitivity, and to ensure that Carden's maximum exposure under the Agreement remains within the pre-approved thresholds established during the annual enterprise-risk review completed in January 2025.
2.2 Authorized Ceiling
The negotiation team is authorized to increase the aggregate liability cap up to a maximum of Three Million Five Hundred Thousand Dollars ($3,500,000). This figure represents the highest aggregate cap amount that the Team may propose, counter-propose, or accept at the negotiation table without further escalation. The Team has discretion to move incrementally within the range of $2,000,000 to $3,500,000, and should exercise commercial judgment regarding the pace and magnitude of any upward movement. As a general matter, the Team should not move to the $3,500,000 ceiling until and unless movement on the liability cap is necessary to secure resolution of other material open items on terms favorable to Carden. The $3,500,000 figure has been reviewed by the Finance and Risk groups and is within the Company's insurable-loss tolerance for this engagement.
2.3 Prohibition on Fee-Multiple Formulations
The negotiation team may not accept any liability cap expressed as a multiple, fraction, or function of fees paid or payable under the Agreement. This prohibition applies regardless of the mathematical result such a formulation might produce. By way of illustration and not limitation, the following formulations are all outside the Team's authority:
•  "6 months of fees actually paid"
•  "1x annual fees"
•  "2x total fees paid in the preceding 12-month period"
•  "The greater of $X and Y months of fees paid"
•  Any formulation in which the cap fluctuates based on payment history, fee adjustments, or invoicing periods
The cap must remain a fixed monetary amount, stated as a dollar figure, consistent with Section 10.1 of the Third-Party Data Sharing Policy. This requirement exists to ensure certainty, auditability, and alignment with the Company's insurance program, which is underwritten on the basis of fixed-dollar exposure limits.
2.4 Rationale for Fixed-Amount Requirement
The prohibition on fee-multiple formulations is not a matter of mere preference; it reflects a structural risk concern that the Team should be prepared to articulate to Luminos's counsel if pressed. Fee-multiple formulations introduce variability in Carden's available remedies that is tied to payment timing, potential fee adjustments, and the specific fee components included in the calculation base. In the context of this particular transaction, the risk is especially acute:
Given Luminos's estimated annual Data-Access Fee of $960,000 (calculated as the quarterly Data-Access Fee of $240,000 multiplied by four quarters), a "6 months of fees actually paid" formulation — as proposed in Luminos's Markup at revised Section 14.1 — could yield a cap as low as $480,000 in the first year of the Agreement, depending on the payment schedule and the point at which a claim accrues. Even at steady state, such a formulation would cap Carden's aggregate recovery at approximately $480,000, which is materially below acceptable risk thresholds for a data set encompassing approximately 38 million records per calendar quarter.
To place this in context, the per-record remediation cost for a re-identification incident or unauthorized disclosure event — inclusive of notification, credit monitoring, forensic investigation, and regulatory response — has been estimated by the Company's Information Security and Privacy teams at between $3.50 and $7.00 per affected record, depending on the nature and severity of the incident. Even a partial exposure event affecting a fraction of the quarterly data set would generate potential direct damages well in excess of a $480,000 cap. The fixed-amount requirement ensures that Carden's contractual remedies remain proportionate to the actual risk profile of the data being shared.
2.5 Walk-Away.  If Luminos insists on a fee-multiple formulation in any form, or if Luminos demands a fixed monetary cap exceeding $3,500,000, the Team must escalate to Priya Chandrasekaran before proceeding further on this issue. No authority exists to concede either point at the table, and the Team should not signal willingness to seek internal approval for either position without first completing the escalation process. In the event of escalation, the Team should request a pause on the liability-cap discussion and indicate that further internal review is required without characterizing the nature or timeline of that review.
3. Liability Cap Carve-Outs and Exclusion of Damages
3.1 Existing Carve-Outs
Carden's Initial Draft (Section 14.3) establishes a critical set of carve-outs from the aggregate liability cap. These carve-outs ensure that Carden's most significant risk exposures under the Agreement are not subject to the dollar-amount ceiling that governs general contractual liability. The existing carve-outs are as follows:
(a) Breach of the Re-Identification prohibition set forth in Section 7 of the DSA;
(b) Luminos's indemnification obligations under Section 13 of the DSA;
(c) Breach of the confidentiality obligations set forth in Section 3 of the DSA; and
(d) Liability arising from willful misconduct or gross negligence by either party.
Each of these carve-outs must be retained. The Team has no authority to agree to the removal, narrowing, or conditioning of any of the foregoing carve-outs. These carve-outs reflect the Company's fundamental risk allocation for data-sharing arrangements and are consistent with the minimum carve-out requirements set forth in Policy Section 10.2. Luminos's Markup proposes to eliminate the carve-out for confidentiality breaches and to subject the Re-Identification carve-out to a separate sub-cap of $5,000,000; neither proposal is within the Team's authority to accept.
3.2 Consequential, Incidental, and Punitive Damages
Carden's Initial Draft (Section 14.2) contains a mutual waiver of indirect, special, incidental, consequential, and exemplary (punitive) damages, subject to the carve-outs enumerated in Section 14.3. This is a standard commercial provision and the Team should be prepared to maintain it.
The Team may accept a reformulated mutual exclusion of consequential and incidental damages — for example, if Luminos's counsel proposes to restructure the waiver language or clarify the categories of excluded damages — only if the following conditions are both satisfied:
(a) The existing carve-outs in Section 14.3 (as described in Section 3.1 above) remain intact and are not narrowed, conditioned, or subject to a sub-cap; and
(b) Luminos's obligations with respect to Re-Identification (Section 7 of the DSA) and Security Incident notification and remediation (Section 8 of the DSA) are not shielded from liability by the consequential-damages exclusion. The purpose of these carve-outs is to ensure that Carden retains the ability to recover the full scope of damages — including downstream notification costs, regulatory penalties, and reputational losses — that typically arise from data-security and re-identification events and that might otherwise be characterized as "consequential" under applicable law.
3.3 Walk-Away.  Any formulation that would cap or eliminate liability for Re-Identification attempts or breaches, breach notification failures, or confidentiality breaches is outside the Team's authority and constitutes a walk-away issue. This includes indirect mechanisms — such as reclassifying these obligations as general contractual duties subject to the aggregate cap, imposing a separate sub-cap on any of the carve-out categories, or defining "direct damages" in a manner that would exclude notification costs, forensic investigation expenses, or regulatory penalties. If Luminos proposes any such formulation, the Team must escalate before responding.
4. Data Retention Period
4.1 Policy Ceiling
Section 3.1 of the Third-Party Data Sharing Policy establishes an absolute maximum permissible retention period for any third-party Recipient of Shared Data: eighteen (18) months from receipt of each data delivery. This threshold is designated as non-negotiable under Section 3.4 of the Policy, which provides that "[n]o deviation from the maximum retention period may be authorized by transactional counsel or the negotiation team; any request for a retention period exceeding eighteen (18) months requires approval by the Chief Privacy Officer and must be documented in a written exception memorandum filed with the Privacy Office." The Team does not have authority to seek or grant such an exception through the negotiation process.
4.2 Authorized Range
The Team may move from the Initial Draft position of twelve (12) months (as set forth in Section 6.1 of the DSA) up to the Policy maximum of eighteen (18) months. Within this range, the Team has full discretion and should treat additional retention time as a bargaining chip to be deployed strategically — ideally in exchange for Luminos's concessions on other material terms (e.g., liability cap structure, breach notification timing, or audit rights). The Team should not volunteer movement beyond twelve months absent a specific Luminos request, and should not move to eighteen months as an opening counter-position.
No authority exists to agree to any retention period exceeding eighteen (18) months, regardless of the justification offered by Luminos or its counsel. The 36-month retention period proposed in the Luminos Markup at revised Section 6.1 is substantially beyond the Policy ceiling and cannot be entertained.
4.3 Anonymization-in-Place.  Luminos's Markup (revised Section 6.4) proposes an alternative to mandatory deletion or return of the Shared Data Set at the end of the retention period: "anonymization-in-place," under which Luminos would apply an irreversible anonymization process to the Shared Data Set and retain the anonymized residual data indefinitely.
Anonymization-in-place does not satisfy the deletion obligation under Carden's Policy (Section 3.3) or the Initial Draft (Section 6.4 of the DSA). Section 3.3 of the Policy provides that "[u]pon expiration of the applicable retention period, the Recipient must either (a) certify destruction of all copies of the Shared Data Set, including backup and archival copies, or (b) return all copies to Carden in the format specified in the applicable data sharing agreement. Anonymization, de-identification, pseudonymization, or other transformation of the Shared Data Set does not constitute destruction and does not satisfy the deletion obligation."
The rationale for this position is well established: anonymization techniques are not infallible, and the risk of re-identification from ostensibly anonymized data increases over time as new data sources and computational methods become available. Allowing anonymization-in-place would effectively grant Luminos an indefinite retention right, circumventing the time-bounded retention framework that is central to Carden's data governance model.
The Team may not accept anonymization-in-place as an alternative to mandatory deletion or return, and should communicate this position clearly and without qualification in the counter-turn.
4.4 Walk-Away.  Both Luminos's proposed 36-month retention period and the anonymization-in-place option are outside permissible parameters and represent walk-away positions. If Luminos is unwilling to accept a retention period of 18 months or less with mandatory deletion or return at expiration, the Team must escalate.
5. Re-Identification Prohibition
5.1 Zero-Tolerance Policy
The absolute prohibition on Re-Identification is the single most important privacy protection in the Agreement and the cornerstone of Carden's data-sharing program. Section 4 of the Third-Party Data Sharing Policy states:
"No Recipient shall, directly or indirectly, attempt to re-identify, de-anonymize, or link any element of a Shared Data Set to an identifiable natural person, whether through the use of auxiliary data, probabilistic matching, machine-learning inference, or any other technique. This prohibition is absolute and admits of no exception."
This prohibition was adopted by the Carden Board of Directors on September 14, 2023, as part of the Company's comprehensive privacy governance overhaul, and was reaffirmed without modification at the Board's annual policy review on February 6, 2025. The prohibition may only be modified by Board resolution. No exception, carve-out, qualification, or safe harbor is within the Team's authority to accept, propose, or discuss as a hypothetical without prior Board action. The Team should not engage in exploratory or "what if" discussions regarding potential modifications to the Re-Identification prohibition, as doing so could be interpreted by Luminos as signaling flexibility on a point where none exists.
5.2 "Good-Faith Research Exception"
Luminos's Markup (revised Section 7.2) proposes the addition of a carve-out to the Re-Identification prohibition for "good-faith internal data-quality testing," defined as "processes undertaken solely for the purpose of verifying the accuracy, completeness, and consistency of the Shared Data Set and not for the purpose of identifying any natural person." Although framed as a narrow and benign exception, this carve-out must be rejected without qualification.
Policy Section 4.3 specifically addresses this type of proposal:
"Proposed exceptions to the Re-Identification prohibition based on internal quality assurance, data validation, or research purposes are not permitted. The risk that testing or validation processes could be used — intentionally or inadvertently — to re-identify data subjects is not acceptable, regardless of the stated purpose. No transactional team is authorized to accept such exceptions."
The Team has no discretion on this point and should communicate Carden's position in unambiguous terms. If Luminos presses the issue, the Team should acknowledge the concern underlying the request (data quality assurance) and offer to discuss alternative mechanisms — such as Carden-administered data quality audits or pre-delivery validation protocols — that do not involve Luminos performing any re-identification-adjacent activity.
5.3 Walk-Away.  Any language introducing any exception, qualification, safe harbor, or carve-out to the Re-Identification prohibition is a walk-away issue absent Board action. This includes not only the "good-faith research exception" proposed by Luminos but also any reformulation that conditions the prohibition on intent (e.g., "shall not intentionally re-identify"), imposes a materiality threshold (e.g., "shall not re-identify a material number of records"), or limits the prohibition to specific techniques (e.g., "shall not use probabilistic matching to re-identify"). The prohibition must remain absolute, technique-agnostic, and intent-independent.
6. Breach Notification Timeline
6.1 Policy Maximum
Section 5.2 of the Third-Party Data Sharing Policy establishes the maximum acceptable notification window for Security Incidents affecting Shared Data: forty-eight (48) hours after Discovery, as defined in the applicable data sharing agreement. This represents the outer boundary of what the Company considers an adequate notification period for the purpose of enabling timely investigation, containment, and regulatory notification on Carden's end.
6.2 Authorized Range
The Initial Draft (Section 8.1) sets the notification obligation at twenty-four (24) hours after Discovery, which was selected as the Company's preferred position and is consistent with Carden's standard template for high-sensitivity data-sharing arrangements. The Team may move from 24 hours up to the Policy maximum of 48 hours if this concession is necessary to advance negotiations on other terms.
Critically, the notification trigger must remain "Discovery" as that term is defined in Section 1.8 of the Initial Draft DSA. Section 1.8 currently defines "Discovery" as "the earlier of (a) the date on which Luminos first becomes aware, or reasonably should have become aware, of a Security Incident, or (b) the date on which any employee, officer, agent, or Subprocessor of Luminos first becomes aware, or reasonably should have become aware, of facts or circumstances that would lead a reasonable person to conclude that a Security Incident has occurred or is reasonably likely to have occurred."
The Team may not accept any modification to the notification trigger that would replace "Discovery" with "confirmation," "validated breach," "determination that notification is warranted," or any similar formulation that introduces an intermediate investigative step before the notification clock begins to run. The purpose of the Discovery trigger is to ensure that Carden receives prompt notice at the earliest indication of a potential incident, enabling parallel investigation and containment efforts. A "confirmed breach" trigger would permit Luminos to delay notification indefinitely while conducting its own internal investigation, depriving Carden of critical response time.
6.3 Walk-Away.  Luminos's Markup (revised Section 8.1) proposes a 72-hour notification window tied to a "confirmed breach" trigger. This proposal is outside the Team's authority on both dimensions — the timeline exceeds the Policy maximum by 24 hours, and the trigger formulation is inconsistent with the Company's Discovery-based notification framework. The Team must reject both elements and may not accept either one independently. A 48-hour window with a "confirmed breach" trigger is not an acceptable compromise; nor is a 72-hour window with a "Discovery" trigger. Both the timeline and the trigger must fall within the parameters specified above.
7. Subprocessor Engagement
7.1 Vendor-Security Assessment Requirement
Section 6 of the Third-Party Data Sharing Policy requires that every Subprocessor engaged by a Recipient to process, store, access, or transmit any portion of a Shared Data Set must undergo and satisfactorily complete Carden's Vendor Security Assessment ("VSA") before being granted access to the Shared Data Set. The VSA encompasses a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed Subprocessor's information security controls, data protection practices, incident response capabilities, and compliance posture, and is administered by Carden's Information Security team in coordination with the Privacy Office.
Section 6.2 of the Policy specifically addresses the use of pre-approved lists:
"Pre-approved lists, blanket approvals, or deemed-consent mechanisms that would permit a Subprocessor to access Shared Data without first completing and satisfactorily passing Carden's Vendor Security Assessment are not acceptable and may not be included in any data sharing agreement."
The Initial Draft (Section 9.1) implements this requirement by providing that Luminos may not engage any Subprocessor to process, store, access, or transmit any portion of the Shared Data Set unless Luminos has (a) provided Carden with prior written notice of the proposed Subprocessor, (b) the proposed Subprocessor has satisfactorily completed the VSA, and (c) Carden has provided written approval of the Subprocessor engagement.
7.2 Authorized Concession
The Team recognizes that the VSA process can be time-consuming and that Luminos has a legitimate interest in onboarding Subprocessors within a commercially reasonable timeframe. Accordingly, the Team may agree to a streamlined or expedited VSA timeline for proposed Subprocessors. For example, the Team may commit Carden to completing the VSA within a stated number of business days — the Team has discretion to offer timelines in the range of fifteen (15) to twenty-five (25) business days from receipt of a complete submission package — subject to the proposed Subprocessor's timely and complete cooperation with Carden's assessment process.
However, the Team may not waive, reduce, or modify the substantive scope of the VSA requirement itself. The VSA must remain a prerequisite to Subprocessor access, and no Subprocessor may be granted access to any portion of the Shared Data Set until the VSA is satisfactorily completed and Carden has provided written approval.
7.3 Named Subprocessors (Arcanet Cloud Services, PvtShield Ltd., DataForge Processing Co.)
Luminos's Markup (revised Section 9.2 and Exhibit C) identifies three named entities as proposed initial Subprocessors: Arcanet Cloud Services ("Arcanet"), PvtShield Ltd. ("PvtShield"), and DataForge Processing Co. ("DataForge"). The Luminos Markup further proposes that these three entities be "deemed approved upon execution" of the Agreement, without undergoing the VSA process.
The Team may acknowledge Luminos's proposed initial list and may commit to prioritizing VSA review of the three named entities — for example, by agreeing to initiate the VSA process within five (5) business days of Agreement execution and to use commercially reasonable efforts to complete the VSA for each named entity on an expedited basis. However, the Team may not deem Arcanet, PvtShield, or DataForge approved upon execution of the Agreement without completed and satisfactorily passed VSAs. The VSA requirement applies uniformly to all proposed Subprocessors, including those identified prior to execution.
7.4 Walk-Away.  Any mechanism that would grant Subprocessor access to any portion of the Shared Data Set before VSA completion and written Carden approval is outside the Team's authority. This includes "deemed approval" upon execution, "interim access" pending VSA completion, "conditional approval" subject to post-access remediation, or any similar construct. The VSA must be completed and passed before access is granted — there is no permissible exception to this sequence.
8. Audit Rights and Regulatory Disclosure
8.1 Regulatory Cooperation
Section 7.2 of the Third-Party Data Sharing Policy provides that Carden shall retain the unilateral right to disclose audit findings, compliance reports, and related documentation to applicable regulatory authorities, including without limitation data protection authorities, consumer protection agencies, and financial regulators, without obtaining the consent, approval, or advance notice of the Recipient. This right is essential to the Company's ability to comply with its own regulatory obligations and to cooperate with regulatory inquiries and examinations in a timely and complete manner.
Section 7.3 of the Policy further provides:
"Any contractual provision that conditions Carden's right to disclose audit findings or compliance information to regulators on the prior consent, approval, or review of the Recipient is prohibited and may not be included in any data sharing agreement."
Luminos's Markup (revised Section 10.5) proposes to require Luminos's prior written consent before Carden may disclose any audit findings to a regulatory authority, except where such disclosure is compelled by law or legal process. This provision must be rejected in its entirety. The Team has no authority to accept any consent requirement, advance notice requirement, or review right that would condition or delay Carden's ability to disclose audit findings to regulators. The Team may, as a courtesy, agree to provide Luminos with prompt notice after any voluntary regulatory disclosure (i.e., a notification obligation that does not create a gatekeeping mechanism), but this concession must not be structured in a way that could be construed as a condition precedent to disclosure.
8.2 On-Site Audit Modality
The Initial Draft (Section 10.1) provides Carden with the right to conduct on-site audits of Luminos's facilities, systems, and records to verify compliance with the Agreement, including compliance with data security, data retention, Re-Identification, and Subprocessor management obligations. Section 7.1 of the Policy requires that on-site audit rights be preserved in all data sharing agreements; remote-only audit provisions are not acceptable.
Luminos's Markup (revised Section 10.1) proposes to limit Carden's audit rights to remote assessments conducted through a third-party auditor selected by Luminos. The Team must reject this limitation. While the Team may agree to permit remote audits as a supplementary mechanism — for example, for routine annual compliance checks — the Team must retain the right to conduct on-site audits at Luminos's principal processing facilities. The Team may negotiate reasonable logistical parameters for on-site audits (e.g., business-hours-only access, designated Luminos escort, reasonable limitations on access to non-relevant areas), but the right to conduct on-site audits must be preserved as a Carden option, exercisable at Carden's discretion.
8.3 Frequency.  The Team must preserve Carden's ability to audit Luminos at least once per calendar year, consistent with Policy Section 7.4. The Initial Draft (Section 10.2) provides for audit rights "not more than twice per calendar year, and not more than once per calendar quarter." The Team has discretion to negotiate audit frequency within reasonable commercial bounds — for example, the Team could agree to reduce the maximum frequency from twice per year to once per year if necessary — but the minimum of one audit per calendar year is non-negotiable.
Luminos's Markup (revised Section 10.2) proposes an audit cycle of not more than once every eighteen (18) months. This exceeds the Policy maximum interval and is outside the Team's authority. An 18-month audit cycle would create a gap during which Carden would lack visibility into Luminos's compliance posture, which is unacceptable given the volume and sensitivity of the data involved.
8.4 Notice Period
The 15-business-day advance notice period set forth in Section 10.3 of the Initial Draft is negotiable within reasonable commercial bounds. The Team has discretion to adjust this period and may agree to notice periods in the range of ten (10) to twenty (20) business days, depending on the commercial context and Luminos's stated operational concerns. The Team should resist any notice period exceeding twenty (20) business days, as longer periods could afford Luminos an opportunity to remediate compliance deficiencies before the audit, undermining the audit's effectiveness.
The Team should also ensure that the notice period does not apply to "for cause" audits triggered by a suspected Security Incident, a credible allegation of Re-Identification, or a regulatory inquiry. For-cause audits should be exercisable on shortened notice (e.g., 48 to 72 hours), and the Team should propose language to this effect in the counter-turn if not already addressed.
9. Cross-Border Data Transfers
9.1 Three-Part Prerequisite
Section 9 of the Third-Party Data Sharing Policy and Section 11.1 of the Initial Draft DSA establish a three-part prerequisite framework for any cross-border transfer of the Shared Data Set (or any portion thereof) outside the United States. All three of the following conditions must be satisfied before any such transfer may occur:
(a) Prior Written Approval: Carden must provide prior written approval for the specific transfer, identifying the receiving entity, the destination jurisdiction, and the categories of Shared Data to be transferred;
(b) Completed Transfer Impact Assessment ("TIA"): A Transfer Impact Assessment must be completed, evaluating the legal framework, government access practices, and data protection regime of the destination jurisdiction, and concluding that the transfer does not expose the Shared Data Set to material risk of compelled disclosure or inadequate legal protection; and
(c) Executed Standard Contractual Clauses ("SCCs"): The parties must execute Standard Contractual Clauses (or, where applicable, an equivalent transfer mechanism approved by the relevant supervisory authority) that are acceptable to Carden's Privacy Office.
All three prerequisites must be satisfied. The Team may not agree to any formulation in which SCCs alone constitute a sufficient transfer mechanism. SCCs address contractual obligations between the transferring and receiving parties but do not, standing alone, address the broader legal and regulatory environment of the destination jurisdiction. The TIA and prior written approval requirements serve distinct and complementary risk-mitigation functions and cannot be dispensed with.
9.2 Luminos UK Transfer
Luminos's Markup (revised Section 11.2 and Exhibit D) requests authorization to transfer the Shared Data Set to Luminos Insights UK Ltd., an affiliate organized under the laws of England and Wales, for processing in connection with Luminos's European institutional client base. The Markup proposes that this transfer be pre-authorized upon execution of the Agreement, subject only to execution of the UK International Data Transfer Agreement (UK IDTA) as the applicable transfer mechanism.
Transfer to Luminos Insights UK Ltd. is not pre-authorized. The Team may acknowledge Luminos's interest in effectuating this transfer and may agree to an expedited review process — for example, by committing to initiate the TIA within ten (10) business days of a formal transfer request and to use commercially reasonable efforts to complete the review process within forty-five (45) calendar days — but the full three-part prerequisite applies. Specifically:
(a) Carden must conduct and complete a TIA for the United Kingdom as the destination jurisdiction;
(b) The TIA must conclude that the transfer is permissible under Carden's risk framework;
(c) The parties must execute appropriate transfer mechanism documentation (whether SCCs, the UK IDTA, or another mechanism approved by the Privacy Office); and
(d) Carden must provide written approval for the specific transfer.
The Team may discuss the substance of the transfer mechanism (SCCs vs. UK IDTA) and may agree to use the UK IDTA if the Privacy Office determines it is appropriate following completion of the TIA, but this determination cannot be made at the negotiation table and must not be pre-committed.
9.3 Walk-Away.  Any provision that authorizes a cross-border transfer upon execution of SCCs alone (or any other single transfer mechanism alone), without prior written Carden approval and a completed TIA, is outside the Team's authority and constitutes a walk-away issue. Similarly, any provision that deems a specific transfer pre-authorized upon execution of the Agreement — whether to Luminos Insights UK Ltd. or any other entity — without completion of the full three-part prerequisite is outside authority. If Luminos is unwilling to accept the three-part prerequisite framework, the Team must escalate.
10. Residuals Clause
10.1 Policy Position
A "Residuals Clause" is a contractual provision that permits a Recipient to retain and use information retained in the unaided memories of its personnel who had access to the Shared Data Set, notwithstanding the confidentiality, use limitation, and deletion obligations that otherwise apply under the Agreement. Residuals clauses are disfavored and presumptively unacceptable under Section 8 of the Third-Party Data Sharing Policy. The Policy reflects the Company's long-standing view that residuals clauses create an uncontrollable and unauditable exception to the data-protection framework, effectively permitting Luminos's personnel to use insights derived from the Shared Data Set in perpetuity without any mechanism for Carden to verify compliance or prevent misuse.
Luminos's Markup (new Section 3.5) proposes a broadly worded Residuals Clause that would permit Luminos's employees and contractors to "use, in the course of their normal duties, any information retained in their unaided memories, without restriction," and further provides that "such use shall not constitute a breach of any confidentiality, use limitation, or data protection obligation under this Agreement." The breadth of this formulation is particularly concerning, as it would effectively neutralize the confidentiality and use limitation provisions of the Agreement with respect to any information that a Luminos employee or contractor claims to have memorized.
10.2 Escalation Requirement
If Luminos insists on inclusion of a Residuals Clause — whether in the form proposed in the Markup or in any narrowed or modified form — the Team must escalate to the Chief Privacy Officer for written sign-off before any acceptance. The negotiation team has no independent authority to accept a Residuals Clause in any form. This escalation requirement applies regardless of any narrowing language or limitations that Luminos may propose (e.g., limiting the clause to non-personal information, excluding information subject to the Re-Identification prohibition, or imposing a temporal limitation).
10.3 Recommended Position
Consistent with historical practice under Policy Section 8.3, the Team should reject the Residuals Clause in full in the first counter-turn. The Team should not engage in drafting negotiations regarding the scope or limitations of a potential Residuals Clause, as doing so may signal willingness to accept the concept and complicate the escalation process if Luminos insists. The recommended response is a clean deletion of proposed Section 3.5 in the counter-markup, accompanied by a brief oral explanation that Carden's policy does not permit residuals provisions in data-sharing agreements.
If Luminos pushes back, the Team should acknowledge the request, note that any reconsideration would require escalation to senior privacy leadership, and move the discussion to other open terms. The Team should not characterize the escalation as likely to result in approval.
11. Indemnification Scope
11.1 Mutual Indemnification
The Initial Draft (Section 13) establishes a unilateral indemnification framework in which Luminos indemnifies Carden for specified categories of claims. Luminos's Markup (revised Section 13) proposes to restructure the indemnification as a mutual obligation, with each party indemnifying the other for claims arising from the indemnifying party's breach of its obligations under the Agreement.
The Team may accept a mutual indemnification structure, subject to the following limitation: Carden's indemnification obligation must be limited to third-party claims arising from Carden's demonstrated breach of its representations regarding lawful collection and authority to share the Shared Data Set. Specifically, Carden's indemnification obligation should cover claims by third parties (e.g., data subjects or regulators) alleging that Carden lacked the legal authority to collect the data comprising the Shared Data Set or lacked the legal authority to share such data with Luminos under the Agreement — but only to the extent that such claims result from Carden's actual breach of its representations under the Agreement, rather than from Luminos's processing activities or other downstream conduct.
The Team should ensure that Carden's indemnification obligation does not extend to claims arising from Luminos's use, processing, storage, or disclosure of the Shared Data Set, even where the underlying claim implicates the lawfulness of the original collection. The distinction between "authority to share" and "downstream use" must be clearly drawn in the indemnification language.
11.2 Luminos's Indemnification
Luminos's indemnification obligations as set forth in Section 13.1 of the Initial Draft must be retained in full and without material modification. These obligations require Luminos to indemnify, defend, and hold harmless Carden and its affiliates, officers, directors, employees, and agents from and against all claims, losses, damages, liabilities, costs, and expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees) arising from or related to:
(a) Any breach by Luminos of the Re-Identification prohibition (Section 7);
(b) Any Security Incident affecting the Shared Data Set in Luminos's possession, custody, or control, including failure to comply with the notification obligations set forth in Section 8;
(c) Any unauthorized use, disclosure, or processing of the Shared Data Set by Luminos, its personnel, or its Subprocessors in violation of the use limitations and data protection obligations of the Agreement; and
(d) Any failure by Luminos to comply with applicable data protection laws in connection with its processing of the Shared Data Set.
The Team may negotiate the procedural mechanics of the indemnification (e.g., notice requirements, cooperation obligations, settlement approval rights) within reasonable commercial bounds, but the substantive scope of Luminos's indemnification obligation as described above must be preserved.
11.3 Walk-Away.  Any formulation that would reduce Luminos's indemnification obligations for Re-Identification breaches or Security Incidents, or that would subject such indemnification obligations to the aggregate liability cap established in Section 14 of the Agreement, is outside the Team's authority and constitutes a walk-away issue. The carve-out of Luminos's indemnification obligations from the aggregate liability cap (as discussed in Section 3.1 of this memorandum) is inextricably linked to the indemnification framework, and the Team must resist any attempt to collapse the two provisions. If Luminos proposes to cap its indemnification exposure or to subject the indemnification to the aggregate liability cap, the Team must escalate.
12. Term, Renewal, and Non-Renewal Notice
12.1 Initial Term
The Initial Draft (Section 12.1) sets the initial term of the Agreement at two (2) years from the Effective Date. The Team may extend the initial term to a maximum of three (3) years if doing so is necessary to achieve resolution of other material open terms on terms favorable to Carden. The initial term should not be extended gratuitously; it should be treated as a concession item to be traded against Luminos's movement on higher-priority issues such as the liability cap structure, retention period, or Re-Identification prohibition. An extended initial term benefits Luminos by providing greater certainty and continuity of data access, and the Team should ensure that Carden captures commensurate value in exchange.
12.2 Renewal Periods
The Initial Draft (Section 12.2) provides for automatic renewal in successive one (1)-year periods unless either party provides written notice of non-renewal within the applicable notice period. This remains the Team's preferred position. One-year automatic renewal periods provide Carden with regular decision points at which the Agreement can be evaluated against current business needs, privacy risk assessments, and regulatory developments.
Luminos's Markup (revised Section 12.2) proposes automatic renewal in successive two (2)-year periods. The Team may accept two-year automatic renewal periods, but only if this concession is balanced by reasonable termination-for-convenience rights that ensure Carden is not locked into a multi-year renewal period without an exit mechanism. Specifically, any acceptance of two-year renewal periods should be conditioned on the inclusion of a termination-for-convenience right exercisable by either party upon not less than one hundred twenty (120) days' prior written notice, with the termination effective at the end of the notice period (rather than at the end of the then-current renewal term).
12.3 Non-Renewal Notice
The Initial Draft (Section 12.3) requires ninety (90) days' prior written notice of non-renewal. The Team may extend this notice period up to a maximum of one hundred twenty (120) days if this concession is necessary to resolve the non-renewal notice issue.
Luminos's Markup (revised Section 12.3) proposes a non-renewal notice period of one hundred eighty (180) days. This exceeds the Team's preferred range. However, the Team may accept a 180-day non-renewal notice period if, and only if, this acceptance is offset by Carden retaining a termination-for-convenience right exercisable with one hundred twenty (120) days' prior written notice. The termination-for-convenience right provides Carden with a meaningful exit mechanism that mitigates the practical impact of a longer non-renewal notice period. The Team should clearly link these two provisions in negotiation — a 180-day non-renewal notice is acceptable only as part of a package that includes termination-for-convenience.
13. Data Use Limitation and Derivative Works / Intellectual Property
13.1 Authorized Purpose
The core use limitation set forth in Section 4.1 of the Initial Draft DSA — permitting Luminos to use the Shared Data Set solely for the purpose of producing aggregated, non-personal Benchmarking Reports for institutional clients — must be retained. This use limitation defines the fundamental scope of the data-sharing relationship and is the basis on which Carden's internal approvals, privacy impact assessment, and data protection impact assessment were conducted. Any expansion of the authorized purpose beyond the production of aggregated, non-personal Benchmarking Reports for institutional clients would require a new or supplemental privacy impact assessment and is outside the Team's authority.
The Team should resist any attempt by Luminos to broaden the authorized purpose to include general analytics services, consumer-facing products, direct marketing, advertising-related use cases, or any purpose that involves the disclosure of record-level or disaggregated data to Luminos's clients. If Luminos proposes additional use cases, the Team should note that any expansion of the authorized purpose would require formal review by the Privacy Office and cannot be addressed within the current negotiation.
13.2 Machine-Learning and Derivative Analytical Models
Luminos's Markup (new Section 4.3) proposes to expand the authorized purpose to include the development of "derivative analytical models and machine-learning tools" trained on the Shared Data Set. The Team recognizes that derivative models represent a potential area of value creation for Luminos and that a categorical prohibition may not be commercially sustainable. Accordingly, the Team may discuss the inclusion of derivative analytical models within the authorized purpose, but only if all three of the following conditions are satisfied:
(a) Aggregation Requirement: All derivative models must be trained exclusively on aggregated, non-personal data derived from the Shared Data Set. No model may be trained on record-level data, and no model may incorporate data elements that could, individually or in combination, be used to identify a natural person;
(b) No Extraction or Reconstruction: No derivative model may be designed, trained, or deployed in a manner that permits the extraction, inference, or reconstruction of record-level Shared Data from model outputs, model parameters, or model architecture. The Team should require Luminos to represent and warrant that appropriate technical safeguards (e.g., differential privacy, model inversion testing) have been implemented to prevent such extraction or reconstruction; and
(c) Audit Rights Over Training Pipelines: Carden must retain the right to audit the derivative model training pipelines, including the input data, training methodology, model architecture, and output validation processes, to verify compliance with conditions (a) and (b) above. This audit right should be in addition to, and not a limitation of, the general audit rights discussed in Section 8 of this memorandum.
If all three conditions cannot be incorporated to Carden's satisfaction, the Team should reject the derivative-model provision in its entirety and maintain the authorized purpose as limited to aggregated, non-personal Benchmarking Reports.
13.3 Intellectual Property Ownership
Luminos's Markup (new Section 4.4) asserts that Luminos shall own "all intellectual property rights in and to all derivative works, analytical models, reports, and outputs created by or on behalf of Luminos using or derived from the Shared Data Set." This blanket IP claim is overbroad and must be resisted.
The Team's negotiating position should preserve the following principles:
(a) Carden's Ownership of the Shared Data Set: Carden retains all right, title, and interest in and to the Shared Data Set, including all intellectual property rights therein. Nothing in the Agreement operates as a transfer or assignment of ownership of the Shared Data Set or any component thereof;
(b) Restrictions on Derivative Works: To the extent Luminos creates derivative works from the Shared Data Set (subject to the conditions in Section 13.2 above), any IP ownership concession must be accompanied by binding restrictions preventing such derivative works from being used, directly or indirectly, to circumvent the Re-Identification prohibition. This includes restrictions on combining derivative works with third-party data sets in a manner that could facilitate re-identification; and
(c) No Survival of IP Rights Post-Termination to Circumvent Deletion: Luminos's intellectual property rights in derivative works must not operate to excuse or circumvent the deletion or return obligations set forth in Section 6 of the DSA. Upon termination or expiration of the Agreement, Luminos must delete or return the Shared Data Set in accordance with Section 6, and any derivative works that incorporate or embed record-level Shared Data (as opposed to purely aggregated, non-personal outputs) must be treated as Shared Data for purposes of the deletion obligation.
The Team may agree that Luminos owns intellectual property rights in the specific analytical models and methodologies it develops, provided such models are trained exclusively on aggregated, non-personal data and the restrictions described above are incorporated. The Team should not concede ownership of any output that constitutes or contains identifiable elements of the Shared Data Set.
14. Governing Law and Venue
14.1 Preferred Position
Carden's Initial Draft (Section 15) provides that the Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflicts-of-law principles, and that the exclusive venue for any action arising under or in connection with the Agreement shall be the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware (or, if the Court of Chancery declines jurisdiction, the Superior Court of the State of Delaware). This is Carden's preferred position and is consistent with the Company's standard approach for commercial agreements.
14.2 Authorized Fallback
If Luminos objects to the Court of Chancery as the designated venue — which is anticipated, given that Whitfield & Sato LLP's Markup (revised Section 15) proposes Maryland governing law and venue in the Circuit Court for Baltimore City — the Team may offer the following fallback position as a compromise: Delaware governing law (unchanged) with exclusive venue in the United States District Court for the District of Delaware. This fallback preserves Delaware law, which is the Company's primary objective, while offering a federal forum that may be more familiar and acceptable to Luminos's counsel.
The Team should present the fallback only after Luminos has clearly rejected the Court of Chancery and should not volunteer the federal venue option preemptively.
14.3 Walk-Away.  Maryland governing law and venue (as proposed in Luminos's Markup) is not within the Team's authority to accept without escalation to Priya Chandrasekaran. This applies to both the governing law component and the venue component — the Team may not accept Maryland governing law even if Delaware venue is preserved, and may not accept Maryland venue even if Delaware governing law is preserved. The Team must maintain Delaware governing law as a non-negotiable element and should treat any departure from Delaware law as requiring escalation.
If Luminos proposes a third-state alternative (e.g., New York governing law and venue), this is similarly outside the Team's authority and must be escalated. The authorized options are limited to (a) Delaware law with Court of Chancery venue, or (b) Delaware law with U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware venue.
15. Escalation Protocol
The following escalation protocol applies to all aspects of the negotiation and supplements the issue-specific escalation triggers identified in Sections 2 through 14 of this memorandum:
15.1 General Escalation. Any request, counter-proposal, or negotiating position advanced by Luminos or its counsel that falls outside the parameters set forth in Sections 2 through 14 of this memorandum must be escalated to Priya Chandrasekaran, VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy, before any response — whether substantive, procedural, or conditional — is communicated to Luminos or Whitfield & Sato LLP. Escalation should be initiated by email to p.chandrasekaran@cardenanalytics.com, with a copy to the Carden DSA Negotiation Team shared mailbox, and should include a concise summary of the issue, the relevant Luminos position, and the negotiation context. In time-sensitive situations, the Team may initiate escalation by telephone, but must follow up with a written summary within four (4) business hours.
15.2 Re-Identification and Residuals Clause Escalation. Matters involving the Re-Identification prohibition (Section 5 of this memorandum) or the Residuals Clause (Section 10 of this memorandum) require escalation not only to Priya Chandrasekaran but also to the Chief Privacy Officer. These matters implicate Board-level policy commitments and organizational privacy principles that are beyond the authority of transactional counsel. The Team should not represent to Luminos that internal review of these issues is likely to result in any change to Carden's stated position.
15.3 No Oral Commitments. No oral commitments, conditional acceptances, or "in principle" agreements on terms that fall outside the parameters of this memorandum are authorized. The Team should be particularly cautious in live negotiation sessions, where the pace of discussion may create pressure to respond to Luminos proposals in real time. If a Luminos proposal is outside the Team's authority, the appropriate response is to note that internal review is required and to reserve Carden's position pending completion of the escalation process. Under no circumstances should the Team characterize a position as "likely to be approved" or "subject to final sign-off" unless and until the relevant escalation has been completed and approval has been received.
15.4 Documentation. All escalation decisions and the resulting instructions should be documented in writing and maintained in the negotiation file. The Team should keep a running log of escalation events, including the date, the issue escalated, the individuals involved, the resolution, and any conditions or limitations attached to the resolution.
