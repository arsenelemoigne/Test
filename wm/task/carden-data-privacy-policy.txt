Section 1 — Purpose and Scope
1.1 This Third-Party Data Sharing Policy (this "Policy") governs all arrangements under which Carden Analytics, Inc. ("Carden" or the "Company") discloses, licenses, transfers, or otherwise makes available consumer data—including, without limitation, pseudonymized, de-identified, tokenized, hashed, or aggregated data—to any third-party recipient. The scope of this Policy extends to every form of data-sharing arrangement, whether structured as a licensing agreement, a data partnership, a joint venture data contribution, a service-provider engagement in which Carden's data is made accessible for downstream use, or any other contractual mechanism through which consumer-derived information leaves Carden's direct custody or control.
1.2 This Policy applies uniformly and without exception to all business units, product lines, functional groups, and data sets originating from or derived from Carden's merchant network, consumer-facing products, analytics platforms, and internal data warehouses. No business unit, product team, or revenue organization may establish its own data-sharing standards that diverge from or fall below the requirements stated herein. For the avoidance of doubt, data sets that have been subjected to pseudonymization, aggregation, or other privacy-enhancing transformations remain within the scope of this Policy unless and until they have been irreversibly anonymized in a manner reviewed and confirmed in writing by the Chief Privacy Officer.
1.3 This Policy establishes the mandatory minimum standards that must be reflected in every third-party data-sharing agreement to which Carden is a party. Individual agreements negotiated with specific Recipients may impose protections, requirements, or restrictions that are more stringent than those set forth in this Policy, and business teams and legal counsel are encouraged to pursue heightened protections where the nature of the data, the identity of the Recipient, the proposed use case, or applicable law warrants additional safeguards. However, no individual agreement may relax, reduce, waive, or otherwise derogate from the thresholds, prohibitions, mandatory requirements, or approval processes stated in this Policy, except where this Policy expressly provides a mechanism for Board-level or officer-level exception.
1.4 This Policy has been reviewed and approved by the Board of Directors of Carden Analytics, Inc. It is effective across all jurisdictions in which Carden operates, including the United States and any international jurisdiction in which Carden maintains offices, engages personnel, processes data, or contracts with third parties. Where local law imposes requirements that exceed the standards in this Policy, the more protective standard shall apply. Where local law is less protective than the standards set forth herein, this Policy shall nonetheless be observed in full.
1.5 All Carden employees, contractors, temporary personnel, and outside advisors involved in the negotiation, review, approval, or management of third-party data-sharing arrangements are expected to familiarize themselves with this Policy, adhere to its requirements, and escalate questions or ambiguities to the Privacy Legal team as further described in Section 11.
Section 2 — Definitions
The following terms, when used in this Policy (including in section headings, operational guidance, and cross-references), shall have the meanings set forth below. To the extent a defined term is also defined in a specific data-sharing agreement, the definition in such agreement shall apply to the interpretation of that agreement, but the definitions below shall control for purposes of determining compliance with this Policy.
2.1 "Recipient" means any third-party entity—whether a corporation, limited liability company, partnership, sole proprietorship, governmental body, academic institution, or other legal person—that receives, accesses, or is granted the right to receive or access data from Carden under a data-sharing agreement, licensing agreement, partnership agreement, or any analogous contractual arrangement. The term "Recipient" includes the Recipient's affiliates and subsidiaries to the extent such affiliates or subsidiaries are granted access to Shared Data under or in connection with the governing agreement.
2.2 "Shared Data" means any data set, data feed, data file, API response, database extract, report, or other compilation of data disclosed, transmitted, licensed, or otherwise made available to a Recipient by or on behalf of Carden, regardless of the format in which the data is delivered (including structured, semi-structured, or unstructured formats), the volume of records contained therein, the degree of pseudonymization, de-identification, aggregation, or other transformation applied to the data, or the medium of delivery (including electronic transmission, secure file transfer, API access, physical media, or cloud-hosted data environments). Shared Data includes any derivative data sets created by Carden specifically for delivery to a Recipient.
2.3 "Subprocessor" means any entity engaged by a Recipient—whether as a subcontractor, hosting provider, managed-service provider, cloud-infrastructure provider, analytics vendor, or in any other capacity—to store, process, transmit, access, or otherwise handle Shared Data on the Recipient's behalf. The term "Subprocessor" includes any entity further engaged by an initial Subprocessor (i.e., sub-sub-processors) in a chain of processing.
2.4 "Discovery" means the point at which a Recipient, any of the Recipient's personnel (including employees, contractors, and temporary workers), or any of the Recipient's Subprocessors first reasonably suspects or identifies a Security Incident involving Shared Data, irrespective of whether the incident has been confirmed, forensically validated, scoped as to the volume of affected records, attributed to a specific threat actor, or otherwise fully investigated. Discovery occurs at the moment of reasonable suspicion and is not deferred by the pendency of an internal investigation, the engagement of forensic consultants, the involvement of law enforcement, or the Recipient's desire to gather additional facts before reporting.
2.5 "Security Incident" means any event constituting or reasonably believed to constitute the unauthorized access, acquisition, disclosure, exfiltration, loss, destruction, alteration, or use of Shared Data, whether resulting from a malicious attack, an insider threat, a system misconfiguration, accidental exposure, a vendor failure, or any other cause. The term "Security Incident" is to be construed broadly and includes, without limitation, ransomware events affecting systems on which Shared Data resides, credential-compromise events where compromised credentials could provide access to Shared Data, and inadvertent disclosures to unauthorized persons.
2.6 "Transfer Impact Assessment" or "TIA" means Carden's standardized assessment, administered by the Privacy Legal team in coordination with the Information Security team, evaluating the legal regime, enforcement landscape, government-access framework, judicial-redress mechanisms, and technical safeguards applicable to a proposed cross-border transfer of Shared Data to a jurisdiction outside the United States. The TIA is designed to identify risks to data subjects that may arise from the legal environment of the destination jurisdiction and to determine whether supplementary technical or contractual measures are necessary to ensure an adequate level of protection.
2.7 "Vendor-Security Assessment" or "VSA" means Carden's proprietary security-evaluation process administered by the Information Security team, encompassing an evaluation of the technical controls (e.g., encryption standards, access management, network segmentation, vulnerability management), organizational controls (e.g., security governance, personnel training, incident-response capabilities), and contractual controls (e.g., confidentiality obligations, security commitments, audit provisions) maintained by a proposed Subprocessor. The VSA methodology and scoring criteria are maintained by the Information Security team and are updated periodically to reflect evolving threats and industry best practices.
2.8 "Residuals Clause" means any contractual provision—however denominated—that purports to allow a Recipient, its personnel, or its Subprocessors to retain and freely use knowledge, ideas, concepts, techniques, know-how, methodologies, patterns, correlations, or insights derived from or developed through exposure to Shared Data, whether retained in the unaided memory of the Recipient's personnel or otherwise, after the termination or expiration of the governing agreement. A Residuals Clause may appear under various labels, including "residual knowledge," "residual information," "general knowledge," or similar formulations.
2.9 "Chief Privacy Officer" or "CPO" means the most senior officer within Carden's organizational structure with enterprise-wide accountability for the Company's privacy program, including the establishment of privacy policies, oversight of privacy compliance, management of privacy risk, and engagement with privacy regulators. As of the effective date of this Policy, the CPO reports to the General Counsel and has a dotted-line reporting relationship to the Board's Audit & Risk Committee.
2.10 "VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy" means the Vice President and Associate General Counsel responsible for leading the Privacy Legal team, overseeing the legal aspects of Carden's privacy and data-sharing programs, and serving as the primary legal advisor on the negotiation, interpretation, and enforcement of third-party data-sharing agreements. As of the effective date of this Policy, this role is held by Priya Chandrasekaran.
Section 3 — Data Retention Limits
3.1 Maximum Recipient Retention Period. No Recipient shall retain Shared Data for longer than eighteen (18) months from the date on which the Recipient first receives or is first granted access to the applicable Shared Data (the "Retention Period"). This eighteen-month maximum applies regardless of whether the governing data-sharing agreement remains in effect, regardless of the Recipient's asserted business justification for continued retention, and regardless of the degree of pseudonymization, de-identification, or aggregation applied to the Shared Data. Every data-sharing agreement must incorporate the eighteen-month Retention Period as a binding contractual obligation of the Recipient.
3.2 Destruction or Return Upon Expiration. Upon expiration of the eighteen-month Retention Period—or upon earlier termination or expiration of the governing data-sharing agreement, whichever occurs first—the Recipient must securely delete or return to Carden all copies of Shared Data in the Recipient's possession or control, including copies residing in production environments, development and testing environments, backup systems, disaster-recovery archives, email systems, collaboration platforms, and any Subprocessor environments. Within thirty (30) calendar days following the expiration of the Retention Period or the termination of the agreement, the Recipient must deliver to Carden a written certification of destruction, executed by an authorized officer of the Recipient, confirming that all copies of Shared Data have been securely destroyed or returned in accordance with the agreement and this Policy. The certification must describe the methods of destruction used and confirm that destruction was performed in a manner consistent with NIST Special Publication 800-88 (Guidelines for Media Sanitization) or an equivalent standard.
3.3 Anonymization Does Not Satisfy Deletion Obligation. For the avoidance of doubt, the application of anonymization, further de-identification, further aggregation, or other privacy-enhancing techniques to Shared Data does not satisfy the Recipient's obligation to delete or return Shared Data upon expiration of the Retention Period. Carden's position is that anonymization-in-place introduces unacceptable residual risk, given the evolving state of re-identification techniques and the difficulty of verifying the irreversibility of a Recipient's anonymization process. Only complete and irreversible destruction of all copies of Shared Data, or full return of Shared Data to Carden, will be accepted as compliance with the deletion obligation. In extraordinary circumstances, the CPO and the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy may jointly approve, in writing, an alternative disposition method (such as verified anonymization under specified technical standards), but such approval is expected to be exceedingly rare and will require the Recipient to demonstrate, to Carden's satisfaction, that the proposed method renders re-identification infeasible under any reasonably foreseeable circumstances.
3.4 Non-Negotiable Maximum. The eighteen-month maximum Retention Period is a non-negotiable policy threshold. Contractual retention periods proposed by a Recipient that exceed eighteen months must be rejected by the negotiating team. Business-unit leads, deal teams, product managers, and account executives do not have the authority to agree to, approve, or represent that Carden will accept a retention period longer than eighteen months. This threshold may only be extended or amended by a formal amendment to this Policy approved at the Board level, in accordance with the policy-amendment procedures set forth in Section 11. Negotiating teams confronted with pressure from a Recipient to extend the Retention Period beyond eighteen months should escalate the matter to the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy for guidance and, if warranted, further escalation to the CPO.
Section 4 — Prohibition on Re-Identification
4.1 Board-Approved Zero-Tolerance Standard. Carden maintains an absolute, unqualified prohibition on any attempt—whether direct or indirect, successful or unsuccessful, automated or manual—by a Recipient, any of the Recipient's personnel, any of the Recipient's affiliates, or any downstream party (including Subprocessors, clients, partners, or other third parties to whom the Recipient provides services or with whom the Recipient collaborates) to re-identify, reverse-pseudonymize, de-anonymize, or otherwise link Shared Data back to identifiable consumers, households, devices, or any other unit of identification associated with natural persons. This prohibition applies with equal force regardless of the technique employed, the stated purpose of the re-identification attempt, or the scale at which it is conducted. The re-identification prohibition must be stated expressly and prominently in every data-sharing agreement to which Carden is a party.
4.2 No Exceptions Permitted. No exceptions of any kind are permitted to the re-identification prohibition set forth in Section 4.1. This includes, without limitation, exceptions or carve-outs characterized as:
(a) good-faith research or scientific inquiry;
(b) data-quality testing, data-validation exercises, or accuracy benchmarking;
(c) model-training, model-calibration, or algorithm-development activities;
(d) fraud-detection or anti-money-laundering investigations conducted by or on behalf of the Recipient;
(e) compliance with legal process, regulatory demands, or law-enforcement requests directed to the Recipient;
(f) academic, philanthropic, or public-interest research; or
(g) any other purpose, however meritorious or narrowly defined.
Where a Recipient asserts that applicable law compels an activity that may constitute re-identification, the matter must be escalated immediately to the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy and the CPO for evaluation and response. Under no circumstances may the negotiating team independently agree to a legal-compulsion carve-out to the re-identification prohibition.
4.3 Rejection of Contractual Carve-Outs. Any contractual provision proposed by a Recipient that introduces a carve-out, safe harbor, qualified exception, reasonableness qualifier, materiality threshold, or constructive-knowledge limitation with respect to the re-identification prohibition must be rejected outright by Carden's negotiating team. Negotiating teams do not have the authority to accept, conditionally accept, or agree to "park" such language for later resolution. Any such proposal should be communicated promptly to the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy for documentation and response.
4.4 Material Breach and Immediate Termination. A Recipient's attempt—whether successful or unsuccessful—to re-identify Shared Data shall constitute a material breach of the governing data-sharing agreement. Upon becoming aware of any such attempt, Carden shall be entitled to:
(a) immediately terminate the governing agreement without cure period or further notice;
(b) require the immediate return or destruction of all Shared Data in the Recipient's possession or control, including data held by any Subprocessor;
(c) pursue all remedies available under the governing agreement, at law, and in equity, including damages, injunctive relief, and indemnification; and
(d) report the incident to applicable regulatory authorities as Carden determines appropriate.
The foregoing remedies are cumulative and not exclusive of any other rights or remedies available to Carden.
4.5 Board Authority. The zero-tolerance re-identification prohibition set forth in this Section 4 was adopted by the Board of Directors of Carden Analytics, Inc. and reflects the Board's determination that re-identification of consumer data by third-party Recipients represents an unacceptable risk to consumer trust, regulatory standing, and the Company's brand and reputation. This prohibition may only be modified, relaxed, or rescinded by a subsequent resolution of the Board of Directors. No officer, employee, or agent of Carden—regardless of seniority—has the authority to waive, modify, or grant exceptions to this Section 4 absent a duly adopted Board resolution.
Section 5 — Security Incident and Breach Notification
5.1 Notification Trigger. Every data-sharing agreement must require the Recipient to notify Carden promptly upon Discovery of any Security Incident involving or reasonably believed to involve Shared Data. The contractual obligation to notify Carden must be triggered by Discovery of a Security Incident, as defined in Section 2.4 of this Policy—that is, the point at which the Recipient, its personnel, or its Subprocessor first reasonably suspects or identifies the occurrence of a Security Incident. The notification obligation is expressly not conditioned upon confirmation of the incident, completion of a forensic investigation, determination of the scope or severity of the incident, attribution of the incident to a particular threat actor, or any other post-Discovery event. Negotiating teams must ensure that the contractual language defining the notification trigger mirrors the definition of "Discovery" set forth in Section 2.4 of this Policy and does not introduce qualifiers—such as "confirmed," "validated," "verified," "substantiated," or "material"—that would defer or narrow the trigger.
5.2 Maximum Notification Window. The Recipient must notify Carden of any Security Incident no later than forty-eight (48) hours after Discovery. The forty-eight-hour notification window represents the maximum permissible period; negotiating teams may and should negotiate shorter notification windows where the sensitivity of the Shared Data, the volume of records involved, or applicable regulatory requirements warrant more rapid notification. The forty-eight-hour window runs from the moment of Discovery as defined in Section 2.4, without tolling for weekends, holidays, or business-hour limitations.
5.3 Rejection of Extended or Conditional Notification Periods. Any proposed notification period exceeding forty-eight hours must be rejected by the negotiating team. Similarly, any formulation that conditions the start of the notification window on a "confirmed" breach, a "validated" incident, an "assessed" event, a determination of "materiality," or any other post-Discovery milestone must be rejected. These formulations are inconsistent with this Policy because they permit delay during the critical early hours following a potential compromise of Shared Data—a period in which Carden's own regulatory obligations, incident-response protocols, and consumer-notification timelines may be running concurrently.
5.4 Notification Recipients. The Recipient's initial notification of a Security Incident must be directed simultaneously to both of the following:
(a) Carden's Privacy Incident Response team, at the designated incident-reporting address or telephone number provided to the Recipient at the outset of the data-sharing relationship and updated from time to time by Carden; and
(b) the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy (or such other individual as Carden may designate from time to time in writing).
Notification to only one of these parties does not satisfy the Recipient's contractual obligation. Data-sharing agreements must specify the current contact information for both notification channels, and the Privacy Legal team is responsible for ensuring that such contact information is kept current in all active agreements.
5.5 Form and Content of Notification. Initial notification may be made orally (by telephone) or by electronic communication (by email to the designated incident-reporting address), provided that the Recipient communicates the essential facts known at the time of notification, including the date and time of Discovery, a preliminary description of the nature of the Security Incident, and the categories of Shared Data believed to be affected. The initial oral or electronic notification must be followed by a detailed written incident report delivered to Carden no later than five (5) business days after the initial notification. The written incident report must include, at a minimum:
(a) a description of the nature, scope, and known or suspected cause of the Security Incident;
(b) the categories and approximate volume of Shared Data records affected or reasonably believed to be affected;
(c) the date and time of Discovery and, if different, the estimated date and time of the Security Incident's occurrence;
(d) the identity of any Subprocessors involved in or affected by the Security Incident;
(e) a description of the measures taken or proposed to be taken to contain, remediate, and mitigate the Security Incident; and
(f) the name, title, and contact information of the Recipient's designated point of contact for ongoing communications regarding the Security Incident.
Carden reserves the right to request supplemental written reports at reasonable intervals as the Recipient's investigation proceeds.
Section 6 — Subprocessor Engagement and Vendor-Security Assessment
6.1 Vendor-Security Assessment Prerequisite. No Recipient may engage any Subprocessor to access, store, process, transmit, or otherwise handle Shared Data unless that Subprocessor has first undergone and satisfactorily completed Carden's Vendor-Security Assessment. This requirement applies to all proposed Subprocessors, regardless of the Subprocessor's size, reputation, industry certifications, geographic location, or the nature of the processing activities to be performed. The obligation to complete the VSA process rests with the Recipient, which must cooperate with Carden's Information Security team in facilitating the assessment, including by providing contact information for the proposed Subprocessor and ensuring the Subprocessor's timely responses to VSA questionnaires, documentation requests, and technical inquiries.
6.2 Timing of VSA Completion. The Vendor-Security Assessment must be completed, and the results reviewed and approved by Carden's Information Security team, before the proposed Subprocessor is granted any access to Shared Data. This requirement is absolute and does not permit provisional or interim access pending completion of the VSA. Pre-approved lists, blanket certifications, or industry-standard security badges (such as SOC 2 Type II reports or ISO 27001 certifications) maintained by the Recipient do not substitute for or bypass the VSA process, although such certifications may be considered as inputs to the VSA evaluation. The purpose of this requirement is to ensure that Carden has independently evaluated each Subprocessor's security posture against Carden's own standards, rather than relying on the Recipient's assessment or third-party audit frameworks alone.
6.3 Carden's Discretion to Reject or Revoke Approval. Carden retains sole and absolute discretion to reject a proposed Subprocessor based on VSA findings, including findings related to inadequate technical controls, insufficient organizational maturity, unfavorable jurisdictional risk, unacceptable contractual terms between the Recipient and the Subprocessor, or any other factor that Carden's Information Security team determines poses a material risk to the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of Shared Data. Furthermore, Carden may revoke a previously granted Subprocessor approval at any time upon identification of material deficiencies, including deficiencies identified through periodic reassessment, audit findings, threat intelligence, publicly reported security incidents affecting the Subprocessor, or changes in the Subprocessor's ownership, management, or operating environment. Upon revocation of approval, the Recipient must cease providing the affected Subprocessor with access to Shared Data within the timeframe specified by Carden and must ensure the secure deletion or return of all Shared Data in the Subprocessor's possession.
6.4 Prior Written Consent for New Subprocessors. Data-sharing agreements must require the Recipient to obtain Carden's prior written consent before engaging any new Subprocessor in connection with Shared Data. Blanket consents, general authorizations, deemed-approval mechanisms (including mechanisms under which a proposed Subprocessor is deemed approved if Carden does not object within a specified period), and unilateral notification processes do not satisfy this requirement. Each proposed new Subprocessor must be individually submitted to Carden for evaluation through the VSA process described in this Section 6, and Carden's express written approval must be obtained before the engagement commences. Negotiating teams should ensure that the agreement clearly distinguishes between Carden's consent to the engagement of a specific Subprocessor and Carden's approval of the Subprocessor's security posture through the VSA process; both are required.
6.5 Recipient Liability for Subprocessor Conduct. The Recipient remains fully and unconditionally liable for the acts, omissions, errors, and failures of all approved Subprocessors with respect to Shared Data. The engagement of a Subprocessor does not relieve the Recipient of any obligation under the governing data-sharing agreement, and a breach, Security Incident, or other non-compliance event attributable to a Subprocessor shall be treated as the Recipient's breach, Security Incident, or non-compliance for all purposes under the agreement. Data-sharing agreements must include explicit language to this effect, and negotiating teams must reject any proposed limitation-of-liability or force-majeure provision that would reduce or excuse the Recipient's responsibility for Subprocessor conduct.
Section 7 — Audit Rights and Regulatory Cooperation
7.1 Mandatory Audit Rights. Every third-party data-sharing agreement to which Carden is a party must include the right for Carden—or its designated auditors, which may include internal audit personnel, external audit firms, or specialized privacy or security consultants engaged by Carden—to audit the Recipient's handling of Shared Data. The audit right must encompass both on-site audits (conducted at the Recipient's facilities, data centers, or other locations where Shared Data is stored or processed) and remote audits (conducted through review of documentation, system configurations, access logs, and other records provided by the Recipient). The audit right must extend to the Recipient's Subprocessors to the extent such Subprocessors handle Shared Data. The scope of audits may include, without limitation, verification of compliance with retention limits, re-identification prohibitions, security controls, access restrictions, Subprocessor management, and all other obligations set forth in the governing agreement and this Policy.
7.2 Regulatory Audit Cooperation. Carden's ability to share audit findings, compliance reports, investigation results, and related documentation with applicable regulatory authorities—including, without limitation, the Federal Trade Commission, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, state attorneys general, state privacy regulators, and any other governmental body with jurisdiction over Carden's data practices—is a non-negotiable element of every data-sharing agreement. No agreement may restrict, condition, delay, or require the Recipient's prior consent before Carden discloses audit results, compliance assessments, or related materials to a regulator. Carden must retain full and unfettered discretion to share such information with regulators as Carden determines necessary or appropriate in connection with regulatory examinations, inquiries, investigations, enforcement proceedings, or voluntary disclosures.
7.3 Rejection of Restrictive Audit Provisions. Any proposed clause in a data-sharing agreement that limits audit findings to confidential treatment between the parties, subjects Carden's sharing of audit results with regulators to the Recipient's prior review, consent, or approval, requires Carden to redact or anonymize audit findings before sharing with a regulator, or otherwise impedes Carden's ability to cooperate fully and transparently with regulatory authorities must be rejected outright by the negotiating team. Such provisions are fundamentally incompatible with Carden's regulatory obligations and its commitment to maintaining a cooperative and transparent relationship with the regulators that oversee its business. If a Recipient insists on such provisions, the matter should be escalated to the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy and, if necessary, to the CPO.
7.4 Audit Frequency and Notice. Audit frequency and notice periods are subject to negotiation within reasonable commercial bounds, taking into account the nature and volume of Shared Data, the sensitivity of the use cases, and the Recipient's overall compliance posture. However, audit provisions must not be structured in a manner that impedes Carden's practical ability to verify the Recipient's compliance at least once per calendar year. Common commercial accommodations—such as requirements for reasonable advance notice (typically fifteen to thirty business days for routine audits), limitations on the number of routine audits per year, and provisions permitting the Recipient to satisfy certain audit requests through the delivery of recent third-party audit reports (e.g., SOC 2 Type II)—are generally acceptable, provided that such accommodations do not eliminate or materially diminish Carden's substantive audit right or its ability to conduct supplemental audits in response to specific compliance concerns or Security Incidents. In all cases, Carden must retain the right to conduct audits on an expedited basis, with shortened notice periods, in the event of a Security Incident, a credible allegation of non-compliance, or a regulatory inquiry affecting the Recipient's handling of Shared Data.
Section 8 — Residuals Clauses
8.1 Presumptive Rejection. Residuals Clauses, as defined in Section 2.8 of this Policy, are disfavored by Carden and are presumptively unacceptable in any agreement governing Shared Data. Carden's strong institutional preference is that no data-sharing agreement include a Residuals Clause of any kind, because such provisions create a pathway through which insights, patterns, correlations, and derivative knowledge drawn from Shared Data may be retained and exploited by the Recipient and its personnel indefinitely and without accountability, effectively undermining the retention limits, use restrictions, and deletion obligations that are central to Carden's data-protection framework.
8.2 Mandatory Escalation and CPO Approval. If a Recipient proposes a Residuals Clause—whether as a standalone provision or embedded within a broader intellectual-property, confidentiality, or knowledge-retention clause—the negotiating team must immediately escalate the request to the Chief Privacy Officer for review. The negotiating team does not have the authority to accept, conditionally accept, or agree to include a Residuals Clause without the CPO's express written sign-off. The escalation must include the full text of the proposed Residuals Clause, the Recipient's stated rationale for requiring the clause, and the negotiating team's assessment of the commercial importance of the clause to the overall transaction.
8.3 Historical Practice. Historically, proposed Residuals Clauses have been rejected following CPO review, reflecting the significant and well-documented risk that such provisions effectively permit uncontrolled retention and exploitation of insights derived from Shared Data. The CPO's analysis has consistently concluded that Residuals Clauses, even when narrowly drafted, present enforcement challenges (because the boundaries of "unaided memory" are inherently subjective and unverifiable) and create regulatory risk (because regulators may view such provisions as inconsistent with Carden's representations regarding data deletion and use limitations). Negotiating teams should communicate this institutional history to Recipients early in the negotiation process to set appropriate expectations and avoid protracted negotiations over provisions that are unlikely to be approved.
8.4 Narrow Tailoring Requirement for Approved Clauses. In the rare and exceptional circumstance that the CPO determines, following a thorough review of the specific facts and circumstances, that a Residuals Clause is warranted and approved in writing, the clause must be narrowly tailored to exclude, at a minimum, any information that could, alone or in combination with other information reasonably available to the Recipient, enable re-identification of consumers, households, or devices. The approved Residuals Clause must also be subject to the re-identification prohibition set forth in Section 4 of this Policy, and the agreement must include express language confirming that the Residuals Clause does not create any exception to, or limitation on, the re-identification prohibition. The CPO's written approval must be documented in the contract-negotiation file maintained by the Privacy Legal team.
Section 9 — Cross-Border Data Transfers
9.1 Prerequisites for Cross-Border Transfers. No Shared Data may be transferred, transmitted, made accessible, or otherwise disclosed outside the United States to any Recipient affiliate, Subprocessor, or other third party unless all three of the following prerequisites have been satisfied in advance:
(a) Prior Written Approval. The VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy (or a designee expressly authorized by the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy for this purpose) has provided express written authorization for the specific proposed transfer. The written authorization must identify the Shared Data to be transferred, the identity and jurisdiction of the importing entity, the purpose of the transfer, and any conditions or supplementary measures required as a condition of approval. General or standing authorizations that do not specify the particular transfer, importing entity, and destination jurisdiction are insufficient to satisfy this prerequisite.
(b) Transfer Impact Assessment. Carden's Privacy Legal team, in coordination with the Information Security team, has completed a Transfer Impact Assessment evaluating the legal and regulatory environment of the destination jurisdiction. The TIA must address, at a minimum, the following factors: (i) the destination jurisdiction's data-protection and privacy laws and regulations; (ii) the existence, scope, and practical application of government surveillance, intelligence-collection, or law-enforcement access authorities in the destination jurisdiction; (iii) the availability of effective judicial redress and independent oversight mechanisms for data subjects in the destination jurisdiction; (iv) the enforceability of contractual commitments, including Standard Contractual Clauses, in the courts and administrative bodies of the destination jurisdiction; and (v) the technical and organizational safeguards that will be applied to the Shared Data during and after the transfer. The TIA must be documented in writing, reviewed by the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy, and retained in the transaction file.
(c) Applicable Standard Contractual Clauses. The parties to the data-sharing agreement and, where relevant, the importing entity in the destination jurisdiction have executed the applicable European Union Standard Contractual Clauses (as adopted by the European Commission), the United Kingdom International Data Transfer Agreement or Addendum (as adopted by the UK Information Commissioner's Office), or such other transfer mechanism as Carden, in its sole discretion, deems adequate to provide an appropriate level of protection for the Shared Data in the destination jurisdiction. The selection of the appropriate transfer mechanism is the responsibility of the Privacy Legal team and must be documented in the transaction file.
9.2 SCCs Alone Are Insufficient. For the avoidance of doubt, the execution of Standard Contractual Clauses or other transfer mechanisms, standing alone, is not sufficient to authorize a cross-border transfer of Shared Data. Execution of SCCs without the completion of a Transfer Impact Assessment and the grant of prior written approval by the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy does not satisfy this Policy. All three prerequisites set forth in Section 9.1 must be independently satisfied before any cross-border transfer may proceed.
9.3 Individual Assessment Required. Each proposed transfer destination and each proposed importing entity must be individually assessed under the requirements of Section 9.1. Blanket approvals covering multiple jurisdictions, multiple affiliates of a Recipient, or multiple Subprocessors in different countries are not permitted. Where a Recipient intends to transfer Shared Data to entities in multiple jurisdictions, a separate TIA and a separate written approval must be completed for each destination jurisdiction and each importing entity. This requirement reflects Carden's view that the legal risks, government-access risks, and enforcement landscapes vary materially across jurisdictions, and that a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction analysis is essential to responsible cross-border data governance.
Section 10 — Liability and Risk Allocation Principles
10.1 Preferred Cap Structure. Data-sharing agreements should include an aggregate liability cap expressed as a fixed monetary amount (e.g., a dollar-denominated cap), rather than as a multiple or fraction of fees payable under the agreement. Fee-multiple formulations—such as caps expressed as "two times (2×) the fees paid or payable in the twelve-month period preceding the claim"—introduce unpredictability into Carden's risk exposure, particularly in agreements where fee volumes may fluctuate significantly over the term. Fixed-amount caps provide greater certainty and are strongly preferred. Where a Recipient insists on a fee-based formulation, the negotiating team should consult with the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy to determine whether an acceptable hybrid structure (e.g., the greater of a fixed-dollar floor and a fee multiple) can be achieved.
10.2 Authorized Cap Ranges. Negotiating teams should consult with the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy for current authorized liability-cap ranges before extending or accepting any liability proposal from a Recipient. Authorized cap ranges may vary based on the sensitivity of the Shared Data, the volume of records, the Recipient's financial strength and creditworthiness, the Recipient's insurance coverage, the nature of the permitted use cases, and other transaction-specific factors. The Privacy Legal team maintains a current schedule of authorized cap ranges, which is updated periodically and distributed to negotiating personnel on a need-to-know basis.
10.3 Carve-Outs from Liability Caps. Carve-outs from aggregate liability caps—that is, categories of liability that are not subject to, or are subject to a higher, cap—should be carefully evaluated in each transaction. As a general matter, obligations related to the following subject areas should not be subject to reduced caps and should, where commercially achievable, be carved out from the general aggregate cap or subject to a super-cap at a higher dollar amount:
(a) violations of the re-identification prohibition set forth in Section 4 of this Policy;
(b) breaches of confidentiality obligations applicable to Shared Data;
(c) failures to comply with the Security Incident notification requirements set forth in Section 5 of this Policy;
(d) indemnification obligations arising from the Recipient's unauthorized use or disclosure of Shared Data; and
(e) breaches of applicable data-protection laws attributable to the Recipient's handling of Shared Data.
Negotiating teams should work with the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy to determine the appropriate cap structure for each transaction, balancing Carden's risk-mitigation objectives with the commercial realities of the specific deal.
Section 11 — Policy Administration
11.1 Policy Ownership. This Policy is owned and maintained by the Privacy Legal team under the direction of the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy, currently Priya Chandrasekaran. The Privacy Legal team is responsible for interpreting this Policy, providing guidance to negotiating teams and business units regarding its application, monitoring compliance with its requirements, and recommending amendments or updates as necessary to reflect changes in law, regulation, industry practice, or the Company's risk profile. Questions regarding the interpretation or application of this Policy should be directed to the Privacy Legal team in the first instance.
11.2 Amendment Authority. Amendments to the following sections of this Policy, which govern mandatory thresholds and non-negotiable requirements, require the written approval of the Chief Privacy Officer:
•  Section 3 (Data Retention Limits)
•  Section 5 (Security Incident and Breach Notification)
•  Section 6 (Subprocessor Engagement and Vendor-Security Assessment)
•  Section 7 (Audit Rights and Regulatory Cooperation)
•  Section 9 (Cross-Border Data Transfers)
Amendments to Section 4 (Prohibition on Re-Identification) require both the written approval of the Chief Privacy Officer and a resolution of the Board of Directors, reflecting the Board-level origin and significance of the zero-tolerance re-identification standard. Amendments to other sections of this Policy (including Sections 1, 2, 8, 10, and 11) may be approved by the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy, in consultation with the CPO, without Board action.
11.3 Annual Review Cycle. This Policy shall be reviewed at least once per calendar year by the Privacy Legal team, in consultation with the Information Security team, the CPO, and relevant business stakeholders. The annual review shall assess whether the Policy's requirements remain aligned with applicable laws and regulations, industry best practices, regulatory expectations, and the Company's evolving business operations and risk profile. The next scheduled review date shall be set by the VP & Associate General Counsel – Privacy upon adoption of this Policy and communicated to the CPO and the Board's Audit & Risk Committee.
11.4 Distribution to Negotiating Personnel. All Carden employees, contractors, and outside counsel acting on Carden's behalf who are involved in negotiating, drafting, reviewing, or approving third-party data-sharing agreements must be provided a copy of this Policy before commencing or responding to any data-sharing negotiation. The Privacy Legal team is responsible for maintaining a distribution list of all such personnel and for ensuring that updated versions of this Policy are promptly distributed following any amendment. Receipt and acknowledgment of this Policy shall be documented by the Privacy Legal team, and such documentation shall be retained in accordance with the Company's records-retention schedule. Outside counsel engaged by Carden to negotiate or advise on data-sharing agreements must acknowledge receipt of this Policy in writing and confirm that the terms of the applicable engagement are consistent with adherence to this Policy's requirements.
