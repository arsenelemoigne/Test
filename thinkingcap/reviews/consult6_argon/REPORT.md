# Independent Review & Strategy: The Budget Ladder on Qwen3-0.6B

## 1. Verdict

**No — as designed, the bottom-up budget ladder will not reach >50% fewer tokens at better than −1.0 point on the full test (expected outcome: 25–38% saved at −1.5 to −3.0 pp if early-stage exit checks hold, or 52–60% saved at −3.0 to −5.5 pp if forced-cut pairs dominate, while consuming ~150–200 GPU-hours across 7 stages).**
The design rests on three mechanisms that your own 205 scored runs and Track 2g cut-calibration data have already falsified on Qwen3-0.6B: (1) training DPO or BCE on forced sentence-cut prefixes (`stopB..stopB5`, `stopV2`, `stopV3`, `gold1`, `teach1c`) either stalls at 10–25% saved (`stopV3`) or triggers a global stop cliff where code and math collapse (`stopB3` soup 30: −3.2 pp overall, −8.4 pp code; `stopB5` soup 30: −2.7 pp; `gold1`: −63.0 pp); (2) a 0.6B LoRA cannot route fine-grained thinking budgets per question from the prompt alone (`zbench` position/budget test AUC = 0.508 = chance; early confidence AUC = 0.38–0.55 on math; `lpt6` cross-type compression is 95% rank-1 with cosine 0.991); and (3) the level rule `p_rung(q) >= p0(q) - 1/8` across 10 rungs has a severe multiple-testing stopping bias that assigns a question with a true −20 pp accuracy loss at early rungs to those failing rungs **81.1%** of the time.
**The single biggest risk** is that an unsplit Phase 0 "oracle" evaluation will report a statistical mirage of ~60%+ saved at ~0.0 pp loss due to stopping-time selection bias on the 8 re-answers (+6 to +8 pp accuracy optimism bias—the exact trap you discovered and fixed with a 4+4 half-split in `benchlot` on 26 Oct), luring you into spending your entire week on two L4 GPUs (>1.2M cut/panel generations) on a 7-stage chained-reference pipeline that suffers from cumulative drift and prefix-pace mismatch.
To hit **>50.0% saved and > −1.00 pp** in one clean, reproducible recipe from own answers, drop forced-cut bottom-up training and run a **2-Round On-Policy Compact-and-Verify DPO recipe (`OPCV-DPO`)** (or an asymmetric 4-domain `fp32` merge + on-policy verification pass) that combines natural length-anchor pairs (30%) with your proven equal-length question-re-read, IFEval `--count-rules`, and `no-fit` calibration pairs (70%).

### 2.3 Per-Type Evidence For and Against the Ladder across All 205 Runs

1. **Math (SVAMP, 25% weight) — Forced Cuts Fail Above 45% Saving; Compaction + Question Re-Read Succeeds:**
   - **Thinking-off (`B=0`) is a dead zone:** Accuracy drops −68.1 pp on raw Qwen (88.7% → 20.6%) and −62.5 pp on trained `rc1-rl-40` (85.6% → 23.1%).
   - **SVAMP settles late in raw Qwen thinking (`benchlot`, 25–26 Oct):** Cutting raw Qwen's SVAMP thinking traces by quintiles (`0–20% / 20–40% / 40–60% / 60–80% / 80–100% / full`) gives accuracy `0.26 / 0.34 / 0.48 / 0.58 / 0.55 / 0.73`. Unlike practice GSM-IC/Plus (which settle at 20–40% because raw Qwen writes 500+ tokens of redundant verification), SVAMP problems are 1–2 step trap questions where the actual calculation finishes late.
   - **Strict forced-cut oracle ceiling on SVAMP is only 41–47%:** On 26 Oct (`benchlot` strict recomputation, L2266), requiring a cut of a right SVAMP trace to remain right on both halves of 20 forced re-answers gave a **strict oracle ceiling of 41% saved at 0.0 pp (or 47% saved at −1.0 pp)**. By contrast, models trained on *natural* compact traces (`rpe-40/60/80full`) achieve **54%–59% SVAMP saving at −1.2 to −3.0 pp** on the full test because the model learns to *write denser calculations from token 1*, not to cut a leisurely 677-token trace at token 280.
   - **Why earlier runs failed on math, and what fixed it:** Across all 64 Screen-720 runs with SVAMP saving ≥ 55%, mean SVAMP accuracy is **−4.76 pp** (vs −0.94 pp at 35–45% saved). Your `st-ca1-soup-10` and `rc1` error analyses (9 Nov, L4996) proved that on regressed math questions, **30% stopped early** (`thinking < 0.5x` base median), while **68% wrote normal-length compressed thinking (`0.43x` base) but misread the trap question** (271 of 307 wrong answers had numbers far off). That is why `rpE`'s equal-length right>wrong pairs—which preferred chosen answers that **re-checked the question** (51% vs 41% on math)—recovered SVAMP on the full test from **−6.6 pp (`rc1-soup-30`) → −3.0 pp (`rpe-40full`) → −1.5 pp (`rpe-60full`) → −1.2 pp (`rpe-80full`) → 0.0 pp (`rpi-120full`)**.

2. **Code (MBPP+, 25% weight) — Flat Cut Curve in Truncation, Severe Cliff Under Cut-Pair DPO:**
   - **High truncation ceiling in Phase-0-style cuts:** In `benchlot` and `lot B4` (25 Oct), raw Qwen's code success rate is flat across cut positions (`0.46 / 0.53 / 0.44 / 0.52 / 0.49 / 0.50` on MBPP+; `0.46–0.52` on KodCode), giving a strict 20/20 forced-cut oracle of **72% saved at 0.0 pp**. Furthermore, 8.5% of raw Qwen MBPP+ answers hit the 8,192 cap (holding 22% of all thinking tokens across the entire evaluation).
   - **Why cut-pair DPO fails on code anyway (`stopB`, `stopB3`, `stopB5`):** When you actually trained DPO on safe-cut pairs (`stopB3`, `stopB5`), code held up to step 20–25 (+50% saved / −0.3 pp in `stopB3` soup 25), and then **fell off a cliff between 400 and 480 pairs seen** (`stopB3` soup 30: +85% saved / −8.4 pp; `stopB5` soup 20→25→30: +11% → +69% → +88% saved, −1.3 → −5.6 → −6.9 pp). Prefix-cut DPO boosts `p(</think>)` at every paragraph break; once `p(</think>)` crosses ~0.5 early in code generation, the model skips edge-case verification (e.g., `next_smallest_palindrome` failing when `n` is already a palindrome, L865).
   - **Equal-length correctness pairs fix code:** On the full test, `rpE` and `rpI` lifted MBPP+ from **63% / −3.5 pp (`rc1`) → 53% / −1.7 pp (`rpe-40full`) → 48% / −0.7 pp (`rpe-60full`) → 38% / +0.5 pp (`rpi-120full`)**.

3. **IFEval (25% weight) — Minimal Thinking Needed, Counting Rules Are the Trap:**
   - **Thinking adds only +1.2 pp overall (`ntfull2`: 58.7% → 57.5% at B=0):** In `lot B` and `benchlot`, early cuts (0–20% of thinking) score 0.56–0.77 vs 0.61–0.73 full, and the last quintile (80–100%) hurts accuracy (0.45–0.66). Moreover, `consult1` found that 80% of capped IFEval answers had already closed `</think>` (71% of their tokens were answer-side loops).
   - **Why every shortened model lost 2.4–3.0 pp on IFEval:** The 10 Nov rule-by-rule audit (`ir-full/ifeval_rules.json`) proved that IFEval losses come entirely from **counting/length rules** (`number_words` −11 to −13 pp, `keywords:frequency` −8 to −10 pp, `number_sentences` −6 to −7 pp, `highlighted_sections` −6 to −9 pp, `placeholders` −5 to −11 pp): shortened models stop early (626–815 wrong answers with thinking `<0.5x` base median vs 62 in raw Qwen) AND write responses that are too short (379 too-short responses in `rpE-80` vs 265 in raw Qwen).
   - **When fixed (`rpI-120` with `--count-rules` on 320/320 IFEval pairs incl. 152 counting-rule pairs from current-model rollouts):** Full-test IFEval improved from **−2.6 pp (`rpE-80`) to −1.5 pp (`rpI-120`)** at 41% total saving (~66% thinking saving).

4. **BFCL Function Calling (25% pooled weight: Simple 11.9%, Multiple 6.0%, Irrelevance 7.1%):**
   - **Irrelevance (`no-fit`, 240 questions) is the #1 accuracy lever:** With thinking OFF (`ntfull2`), `no-fit` jumps **+6.9 pp** (83.4% → 90.3%) because extended thinking talks the model into inventing a function call when none fits. In `lpc-l2s50e-soup-100` (full test), the longest thinking quintile on `no-fit` lost **−11.5 pp**. Conversely, when DPO includes sufficient `no-fit` guard/right pairs (`rpe-80full`, `rpi-120full`, `michal-bida`), `no-fit` gains **+3.4 pp to +6.04 pp** at 46–50% saved, which alone adds **+0.24 to +0.43 pp** to the 4-benchmark headline.
   - **Simple (400) & Multiple (200) calls:** At `B=0`, simple drops −10.8 pp and multiple drops −20.8 pp; they need ~60–100 thinking tokens (out of 171–192) to map arguments cleanly. In `lot B4` (25 Oct), cutting Hermes tool calls late in thinking (80–100%) crashed accuracy from 0.49 to **0.18** because cutting mid-argument-verification corrupts the JSON call, whereas early cuts (20–60%) were safe. On the full test, `simple` and `multiple` hold at **+0.1 to +0.4 pp** at 37–41% saved in `rpe-40/60/80full`.

## 3. Point-by-Point Design Review of the Budget Ladder

| Component | Verdict | Technical & Quantitative Reason | Exact Replacement / Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Per-type budget rungs (`0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 65, 80, 90th pct + nat` = 11 rungs)** | **CHANGE to 4 coarse rungs (Phase 0) / 2 stages (Phase 1)** | 11 rungs is far finer than Qwen3-0.6B's resolution. `zbench` showed position/budget test AUC = 0.508 (chance), and `lpt6` showed length control is 95% rank-1 across types (cosine 0.991). Testing 10 cut rungs with 8 samples creates extreme multiple-testing stopping bias and multiplies rollout cost by ~6×. | For Phase 0 measurement, use **4 coarse rungs per type**: `0, p25, p50, p75, natural` (Math: `0, 150, 300, 450, nat`; Code: `0, 300, 600, 950, nat`; IFEval: `0, 100, 220, 350, nat`; Tools: `0, 50, 100, 150, nat`). For training, replace 7–10 bottom-up rungs with **2 rounds/stages**. |
| **2. Level definition `b*(q)` with `1/8` slack (`p_rung >= p0 - 1/8`)** | **DROP `1/8` slack; CHANGE to 4+4 split-sample monotone suffix rule** | With `n=8` samples, `1/8` slack is **−12.5 pp**—12.5× larger than your −1.0 pp target! Exact binomial math shows that if a question has true `p_nat = 0.80` and true `p_early = 0.60` (**−20.0 pp loss**), `k_r >= k_0 - 1` passes a single rung **47.3%** of the time and passes ≥1 of 4 early rungs **81.1%** of the time (even at `p_early = 0.50`, −30 pp loss, it passes ≥1 of 4 rungs **62.4%** of the time). Scoring the Phase 0 oracle on the same 8 samples inflates accuracy by **+6 to +8 pp**. | Use your proven 26 Oct `benchlot`/`lot V2` **4+4 half-split + monotone suffix rule**: split the 8 rollouts into Selection (4) and Confirmation (4). On Selection (4), `b*(q)` is the lowest rung `r` such that **for all `r' >= r`**, `k_sel(r') >= k_sel(nat)` (**0 slack**, monotone suffix so noise at an early rung cannot jump over a failing middle rung). Report Phase 0 oracle accuracy and tokens **only on the held-out Confirmation (4) answers** at `b*(q)`. |
| **3. Forced cuts vs natural stops (`</think>` inserted at sentence end ≤ B)** | **DROP forced cuts for DPO training; KEEP only for Phase 0 diagnostic** | An answer cut by force at token `B` has a prefix written at a 600-token leisurely pace followed by an abrupt `</think>`. Training DPO on forced cuts (`stopB..B5`, `stopV2/V3`) teaches a global `</think>` hazard boost that either stalls at 10–16% (`stopV3`) or causes code/math cliffs (`stopB3` soup 30: −8.4 pp code). Worse, if Stage 1 (`B=0`) teaches the model to emit `</think>` at token 0, the current model in Stage 2 will never generate the longer thinking traces needed to cut at `B_2`! | **Never train DPO on forced-cut traces.** Train only on **complete, naturally closed answers** (`</think>` emitted by the model itself) from `8–10` rollouts per question. Natural short answers already have compact reasoning density from token 1. |
| **4. Three pair kinds & their shares (Compaction, Anchor, Guard)** | **CHANGE definitions & fix exact per-type shares** | In `stopV2`, `D2 hard`, `D3 route`, and `D8 math`, "don't stop too early" pairs where a longer right answer beats a shorter wrong answer (`b*(q)` right > `b*(q)-1` wrong) directly rewarded length and **cancelled shortening** (`stopV2`: −15.8% saved; `D2`: 13.8%; `D3`: 9.4%). Conversely, pure equal-length right>wrong (`rpE`, `rpG`) without length anchors drifted longer over 80–120 steps (57.4% → 47.0% → 32.7%). | Use **three natural-rollout pair kinds** with explicit length caps:<br>1. **Shorten (Anchor):** on questions solved `>= 6/8` (never on IFEval counting rules), shortest right vs longest right `>= 1.35x–1.40x` thinking length.<br>2. **Equal-Length Re-Read Right (`rpE`-style Compaction):** on questions solved `2/8–6/8`, right answer vs finished wrong answer within **±20% thinking length** (`median(chosen) <= median(rejected)` per part), preferring chosen traces that re-read the question.<br>3. **Capped Anti-Early-Stop Guard:** on questions where a wrong answer stopped early (`thinking < 0.50x` median right), pick a right answer with thinking **`<= 1.15x` median right** (never the longest right!).<br>**Shares:** Round 1: **35% Shorten / 50% Equal-Length Right / 15% Guard** on Math & `No-Fit`; **50% / 35% / 15%** on Code, Simple, Multiple. Round 2 (on-policy): **30% Shorten / 55% Equal-Length Re-Read Right / 15% Capped Guard**. |
| **5. Question mix per stage (60% edge `2-6/8`, 30% lower-level, 10% out-of-reach; drop never-solved)** | **CHANGE to 50% mixed (`2-6/8`) + 50% high-solve (`6-8/8`); KEEP dropping `0/8`** | Dropping `0/8` (never-solved) is supported by `wronglen` (24 Oct, L1422), which showed shortening spills over automatically to `0/8` questions (+59% cut in `rc1-rl-40`). ## 4. One-Week Strategy on Two Colab L4 GPUs (Sequence, Exact Settings, Expected Ranges, Decision Rules)

You have ~14 GPU-days (~336 theoretical GPU-hours, or ~220–240 usable GPU-hours after Colab reconnects). Do **not** spend 150+ GPU-hours running 7 stages × 11 rungs of forced cuts. Run this disciplined 5-day sequence with hard quantitative gates:

### Day 1 Morning (~6 GPU-hours across 2 GPUs): Build `Dev-512` + `Confirm-512` & Run Bias-Corrected Phase 0
- **Phase 0 First or Not?** **Yes, run a 2.5 GPU-hour bias-corrected Phase 0 first**—with the **4+4 split-sample monotone-suffix rule** and **4 coarse rungs (`0, p25, p50, p75, nat`)**—to settle the forced-cut vs natural-compaction debate quantitatively before any training starts:

## 5. Traps in Evaluation, Data, and Method — and What Could Mislead You

1. **Trap 1 — The Phase 0 In-Sample Stopping Mirage (+6 to +8 pp Accuracy Inflation):**
   In `LADDER_DESIGN_FOR_REVIEW.md` §5–6, `b*(q)` is selected as the lowest of 10 rungs where `k_rung >= k_0 - 1` on 8 samples, and the Phase 0 "oracle curve" reports tokens and accuracy at `b*(q)` on those **same** 8 samples. By binomial order statistics, testing up to 10 rungs with `1/8` slack guarantees that even when early rungs have a true −20 pp accuracy loss, 81.1% of questions stop at a lucky early rung, and reporting the in-sample accuracy of that lucky rung hides the entire loss (+6 to +8 pp optimism bias). **Always use a 4+4 split-sample monotone-suffix rule (`k_sel(r') >= k_sel(nat)` for all `r' >= r`, 0 slack) and report oracle accuracy exclusively on the held-out 4 confirmation samples.**
2. **Trap 2 — The Stage 1 → Stage 2 Rollout Deadlock in Bottom-Up Cut Training:**
   If Stage 1 trains the model at `B_0 = 0` (`<think></think>`) until it exits `<think>` immediately on low-level questions, then at Stage 2 (`B_1 = p5`) and Stage 3 (`B_2 = p10`), when the current model generates uncapped thinking to be cut at `B_k`, it will close `</think>` early on its own! You cannot discover whether a question benefits from `B_k` tokens by cutting a model that has already been trained to stop at `B_{k-1}`.
3. **Trap 3 — Total Output Tokens vs Thinking Tokens on IFEval (The 62% Ceiling):**
   Your ladder budgets count *thinking* tokens only, but the official benchmark scores *thinking + final response* tokens. Because IFEval final responses are ~247 tokens (~38% of total IFEval tokens) and *must not shrink* (or counting rules lose −11 to −13 pp), cutting IFEval thinking by 65% yields only **40.3% total saving on IFEval** (`rpE-50`). If you budget SVAMP and MBPP+ for only 50% thinking savings, your 4-benchmark geometric mean will land at **~44–45% total saved**, missing the >50% bar. Math and Code must save **56–60%** to offset IFEval and BFCL's ~41% ceiling.
4. **Trap 4 — Sequential Reference Drift (`reference = previous stage` across 7 stages):**
   In DPO, the KL penalty `beta * KL(pi || pi_ref)` only constrains the step from `pi_{k-1}` to `pi_k`. Chaining 7 stages (`pi_0 -> pi_1 -> ... -> pi_7`) allows total log-probability drift to grow linearly with stage count, causing later stages to overwrite earlier stages (`rpG` undid 25 points of `rc1`'s saving over 40 extra steps; `Track 2e` rounds 3–5 eroded accuracy to −3.9 to −5.4 pp). **Never exceed 2 chained reference hops from raw Qwen, and always include 25–30% Shorten Anchor replay in Stage 2.**
5. **Trap 5 — Hidden Length Signals in "Guard" and "Correctness" Pairs:**
   Your 10 Nov `copdanalysis` proved that on raw Qwen's training answers, wrong answers are **1.6× to 2.0× longer** than right answers (SVAMP right 880 vs wrong 1,773 tokens, wrong longer on 72% of questions; MBPP+ 1,449 vs 2,271, 62%). Conversely, in `stopV2` and `mf1`, "longest right > shortest wrong" guard pairs had chosen answers 1.5–2.0× longer than rejected answers, which made the model think **15–24% longer** across all types. **Every correctness pair (`Equal-Length Right`) must enforce `|len(chosen) - len(rejected)| <= 0.20 * len(rejected)` AND verify that `median(len(chosen)) <= median(len(rejected))` per data type before training starts.**
6. **Trap 6 — Rank-1 Cross-Type Spill-Over (`lpt6` Cosine 0.991):**
   On Qwen3-0.6B, DPO length pressure on *any* data type spills over globally (`ptsolo-math` shortened MBPP+ by 51%; `ptsolo-tools` shortened MBPP+ by 59% and SVAMP by 53%). Dynamically giving more questions in later ladder stages to "types further from the original's accuracy" (which is always Math) will over-shorten or over-lengthen Code and Tools simultaneously. **Keep type quotas strictly fixed across stages.**
7. **Trap 7 — GPU Non-Determinism (~1.2–3.5 pp) and Open Code Bugs (`consult5` Audit):**
   Your `lpc-bidax2` vs `lpc-bidax3` (identical pairs, identical seed 0, pure GPU non-determinism) differed by **2.7% saving and 1.2 pp accuracy** (51.8% / −2.7 vs 54.5% / −3.9), and `lpc-runa2` vs `lpc-runa2r` differed by **7.9% saving** at step 200. Single-seed checkpoints are too noisy to trust; **averaging 2 seeds in weight space (`merge.py` 0.5/0.5)** produces a single clean LoRA adapter that cuts variance by `1/sqrt(2)`. Also fix the two still-open bugs from the 10 Nov audit before training: (a) `dpo.py` `ref.json` cache fingerprint must hash full token IDs of chosen+rejected (not just prefixes + lengths), and (b) log explicit SHA/path identities of `rollout_model`, `init_model`, and `ref_model` in every run header.
8. **Trap 8 — Upstream Submission Requirement (Commit `965a54c`, 6 Nov):**
   BottleCapAI's 6 Nov upstream commit requires every submission to include the coding agent's traces and conversation with the harness as a text document, and your constraints forbid any recipe built on another competitor's data/pipeline. Ensure your final pipeline runs cleanly from `own_repair_questions()` with no `MetaMath` or `bida` files.

#### 2. Full Reproducible Recipe Design
- **Data Pool:** 10,000 clean own questions answered × 8 by raw Qwen (reuse stored 8–10 answer clean pool).
- **Natural Budget Level Assignment (Zero Forced Cuts):** For each question `q` with solve rate `p0(q) = k/8` (`k >= 2`), let `L_med(q)` be the median thinking length of its right answers:
  - **Tier 1 — Easy (`k in {7, 8}`):** Target budget = **shortest natural right answer** `c*`, provided `len(r*) >= 1.25 * len(c*)` (where `r*` is the longest right answer). Produces a **Tier-1 Shorten Pair** (`c* > r*`) AND a **Tier-1 Guard Pair** (`c* > longest wrong`, if `k=7`).
  - **Tier 2 — Medium (`k in {5, 6}`):** Target budget = **second-shortest natural right answer** `c_2` (protects against lucky shortcuts on trap math!), paired against `r*` if `len(r*) >= 1.50 * len(c_2)` (**Tier-2 Shorten Pair**), plus an **Equal-Length Re-Read Right Pair** (`right > finished wrong` within ±20% length, chosen re-reads question).
  - **Tier 3 — Hard (`k in {2, 3, 4}`):** **0% Shorten Pairs.** Only **Equal-Length Re-Read Right Pairs** (±20% length, chosen re-reads question) and **Capped Anti-Early-Stop Guard Pairs** (right with `len <= 1.15 * L_med(q)` > wrong with `len < 0.50 * L_med(q)`).
  - **IFEval Counting-Rule Override:** Any IFEval question with a counting/length constraint is placed in Tier 3 (`0% Shorten`; 100% `--count-rules` where chosen response ≥ rejected response and chosen thinking `<= 1.20x` rejected and `>= 80` tokens).
- **Curriculum Ordering (2-Rung Single-Pass Schedule with Fixed Raw Reference, 4,000 pairs = 250 steps × 16):**
  - **Rung A (Steps 1–125):** All Tier-1 + Tier-2 pairs (2,000 pairs, shuffled across all 4 types in equal 25% shares) — establishes the difficulty-indexed compression habit.
  - **Rung B (Steps 126–250):** 60% Tier-3 Equal-Length Re-Read / `--count-rules` / Capped-Guard pairs + 40% replay of Tier-1/Tier-2 Shorten pairs (2,000 pairs) — repairs hard math/code and counting rules while holding the Rung A compression in place under a **single cosine LR schedule (`2e-5 -> 2e-6`) and fixed raw-Qwen reference**.
