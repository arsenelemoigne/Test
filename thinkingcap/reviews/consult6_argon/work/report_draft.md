# Independent Review & Strategy: The Budget Ladder on Qwen3-0.6B

## 1. Verdict

**No — as designed, the bottom-up budget ladder will not reach >50% fewer tokens at better than −1.0 point on the full test (estimated outcome: 25–38% saved at −1.5 to −3.0 pp if early-stage exit checks hold, or 52–60% saved at −3.0 to −5.5 pp if forced-cut pairs dominate, while consuming ~150–200 GPU-hours across 7 stages).**
The design rests on three mechanisms that your own 205 scored runs and Track 2g cut-calibration data have already falsified on Qwen3-0.6B: (1) training on forced sentence-cut prefixes (`stopB..stopB5`, `stopV2`, `stopV3`, `gold1`, `teach1c`) either stalls at 10–25% saved (`stopV3`) or triggers a global stop cliff where code and math collapse (`stopB3` soup 30: −3.2 pp; `stopB5` soup 30: −2.7 pp; `gold1`: −63.0 pp); (2) a 0.6B LoRA cannot route thinking budgets per question from the prompt alone (`zbench` position/budget test AUC = 0.508 = chance; early confidence AUC = 0.38–0.55 on math; `lpt6` cross-type compression is 95% rank-1 with cosine 0.991); and (3) the level rule `p_rung(q) >= p0(q) - 1/8` across 10 rungs has a massive multiple-testing stopping bias that assigns a question with a true −20 pp accuracy loss at early rungs to those failing rungs **81.1%** of the time.
**The single biggest risk** is that an unsplit Phase 0 "oracle" evaluation will report a mirage of ~60%+ saved at ~0.0 pp loss due to stopping-time selection bias on the 8 re-answers (+6 to +8 pp optimism bias, the exact trap you uncovered and fixed with a 4+4 half-split in `benchlot` on 26 Oct), luring you into spending your entire week of two L4 GPUs (~160+ GPU-hours for >1.2M cut/panel generations) on a 7-stage chained DPO pipeline that suffers from stage-to-stage reference drift and prefix-pace mismatch.
To hit **>50.0% saved and > −1.00 pp** in one clean, reproducible recipe from own answers, you must drop the 10-rung forced-cut bottom-up curriculum and instead run a **2-stage (or 2-round) On-Policy Compact-and-Calibrate DPO recipe** (or a 3-rung coarse natural-rollout ladder) anchored to the raw model, combining natural length-compaction pairs with your proven equal-length re-read/counting-rule correctness pairs (`rpE`/`rpI`).

## 2. Quantitative Analysis of the Team's Evidence (Recomputed from `EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` & `all_runs.json`)

### 2.1 Official Headline Mechanics & The Non-Thinking Token Floor
The official ThinkingCap-Test headline weights the four benchmarks equally (25% each: SVAMP 1,000, MBPP+ 378, IFEval 541, BFCL 840 pooled as `400/840` simple + `200/840` multiple + `240/840` irrelevance), with `saved = 1 - (r_v * r_c * r_f * r_bfcl)**(1/4)` over **all output tokens (thinking + final response)**.
Recomputing across `full-base` and `ntfull2` (full test 2,759 × 8, thinking OFF vs ON) reveals a severe arithmetic constraint that the ladder design overlooks:

- **The IFEval Response-Token Bottleneck:** On IFEval, the final response is ~247–265 tokens (~38% of total tokens). Even at `B = 0` (`ntfull2`), IFEval saves only **62%** of total tokens. Furthermore, your 10 Nov diagnostic (`ir-full/ifeval_rules.json`) proved that shrinking IFEval responses from median 209 to 145–161 tokens on counting/length rules causes a −11 to −13 pp crash on word-count rules and costs −0.65 pp on the overall 4-benchmark headline. When `rpE @ 50` preserved IFEval response length (247 → 245 tokens) while cutting thinking by **63.6%** (420 → 153 tokens), total IFEval saving was only **40.3%**.
- **Consequence for the 50% Goal:** Because a safe IFEval model (response untouched, thinking cut 60–65%) saves only **37.2–40.3%** total tokens on IFEval, and BFCL stabilizes at **40–43%** total saved across every accurate run (`rpe-80full`: 41.2%, `rpi-120full`: 41.0%, `rpe-40full`: 42.6%), the geometric mean formula requires **SVAMP and MBPP+ to average ≥ 57.5%–58.5% total savings** (e.g., SVAMP ≥ 58%, MBPP+ ≥ 57%) to push the 4-benchmark headline above 50.0%.

### 2.2 Per-Type Evidence: Forced Cuts vs Natural Compaction across 205 Runs

1. **Math (SVAMP, 25% weight) — Why Forced Cuts Fail and Compaction + Re-Read Works:**
   - **Thinking-off (`B=0`) is catastrophic:** Raw Qwen drops −68.1 pp (88.7% → 20.6%); even after 4 rounds of training (`rc1-rl-40`), thinking-off on fresh-240 drops −62.5 pp (85.6% → 23.1%). Only ~20% of SVAMP questions can be answered at `B=0`.
   - **SVAMP settles late in raw thinking traces (`benchlot`, 25–26 Oct):** When cutting raw Qwen's SVAMP thinking traces by quintiles (`0–20% / 20–40% / 40–60% / 60–80% / 80–100% / full`), accuracy is `0.26 / 0.34 / 0.48 / 0.58 / 0.55 / 0.73`. Unlike practice GSM-IC/Plus (which settle at 20–40% of thinking because raw Qwen writes 500+ tokens of redundant verification), SVAMP problems are 1–2 step trap problems where the calculation finishes late.
   - **Strict forced-cut oracle ceiling on SVAMP is below 50%:** On 26 Oct (`benchlot` strict recomputation, L2266), requiring a cut of a right SVAMP answer to remain right on both halves of 20 forced re-answers gave a **strict oracle ceiling of only 41% saved at 0.0 pp (or 47% saved at −1.0 pp)**. By contrast, models trained on *natural* shorter completions (`rc1`, `rpe-40full`, `rpe-60full`, `rpe-80full`) achieve **54%–59% SVAMP saving at −1.2 to −3.0 pp** on the full test because the model learns to *write denser calculations from token 1*, not to truncate a leisurely 677-token trace at token 280.
   - **Why math loses accuracy at >58% saving:** Across all 64 Screen-720 runs with SVAMP saving ≥ 55%, mean SVAMP accuracy change is **−4.76 pp** (vs −0.94 pp at 35–45% saved). Your `st-ca1-soup-10` and `rc1` error analyses (9 Nov, L4996) showed two distinct failure modes on regressed math questions: **30% stopped early** (thinking < 0.5× base median), and **68% wrote normal-length compressed thinking (0.43× base) but misread the trap question** (271 of 307 wrong answers had numbers far off). That is why `rpE`'s equal-length right>wrong pairs—which preferred chosen answers that **re-checked the question** (51% vs 41% on math)—recovered SVAMP on the full test from **−6.6 pp (`rc1-soup-30`) → −3.0 pp (`rpe-40full`) → −1.5 pp (`rpe-60full`) → −1.2 pp (`rpe-80full`) → 0.0 pp (`rpi-120full`)**.

2. **Code (MBPP+, 25% weight) — High Cut Ceiling, Severe Cliff Under Cut-Pair DPO:**
   - **Flat cut curve in Phase-0-style truncation:** In `benchlot` and `lot B4` (25 Oct), raw Qwen's code success rate is flat across cut positions (`0.46 / 0.53 / 0.44 / 0.52 / 0.49 / 0.50` on MBPP+; `0.46–0.52` on KodCode), giving a strict 20/20 forced-cut oracle of **72% saved at 0.0 pp**. Moreover, 8.5% of raw Qwen MBPP+ answers hit the 8,192 cap (holding 22% of all thinking tokens across the entire benchmark!).
   - **Why cut-pair DPO fails on code anyway (`stopB`, `stopB3`, `stopB5`):** When you actually trained DPO on safe-cut pairs (`stopB3`, `stopB5`), code held up to step 20–25 (+50% saved / −0.3 pp in `stopB3` soup 25), and then **fell off a cliff between 400 and 480 pairs seen** (`stopB3` soup 30: +85% saved / −8.4 pp; `stopB5` soup 25→30: +11% → +69% → +88% saved, −1.3 → −5.6 → −6.9 pp). Why? Because prefix-cut DPO boosts `p(</think>)` at every paragraph break; on code, once `p(</think>)` crosses ~0.5 early in the trace, the model skips edge-case verification (e.g., `next_smallest_palindrome` failing when `n` is already a palindrome, L865).
   - **Equal-length correctness pairs fix code:** On the full test, `rpE` lifted MBPP+ from **63% / −3.5 pp (`rc1`) → 53% / −1.7 pp (`rpe-40full`) → 48% / −0.7 pp (`rpe-60full`) → 46% / −1.0 pp (`rpe-80full`)**.

3. **IFEval (25% weight) — Minimal Thinking Needed, Counting Rules Are the Trap:**
   - **Thinking adds only +1.2 pp overall (`ntfull2`: 58.7% → 57.5% at B=0):** In `lot B` and `benchlot`, early cuts (0–20% of thinking) score 0.56–0.77 vs 0.61–0.73 full, and the last quintile (80–100%) actually *hurts* (0.45–0.66). Furthermore, `consult1` found that 80% of capped IFEval answers had already closed `</think>` (71% of their tokens were answer-side loops).
   - **Where the −2.5 to −3.0 pp full-test loss comes from:** Every full-test run (`rc1` −2.5, `ca1` −2.4, `rpe-40` −2.8, `rpe-60` −3.0, `rpe-80` −2.6) lost 2.4–3.0 pp on IFEval. The 10 Nov rule-by-rule audit (`ir-full/ifeval_rules.json`) proved this loss comes entirely from **counting/length rules** (`number_words` −11 to −13 pp, `keywords:frequency` −8 to −10 pp, `number_sentences` −6 to −7 pp, `highlighted_sections` −6 to −9 pp, `placeholders` −5 to −11 pp): shortened models stop early (626–815 wrong answers with thinking <0.5× base median vs 62 in raw Qwen) AND write responses that are too short (379 too-short responses in `rpE-80` vs 265 in raw Qwen).
   - **When fixed (`rpI-120` with `--count-rules` on 320/320 IFEval pairs incl. 152 counting-rule pairs from current model rollouts):** Full-test IFEval improved from **−2.6 pp (`rpE-80`) to −1.5 pp (`rpI-120`)** at 41% total saving (~66% thinking saving!).

4. **BFCL Function Calling (25% pooled weight: Simple 11.9%, Multiple 6.0%, Irrelevance 7.1%):**
   - **Irrelevance (`no-fit`, 240 questions) is the #1 accuracy lever:** With thinking OFF (`ntfull2`), `no-fit` jumps **+6.9 pp** (83.4% → 90.3%) because extended thinking talks the model into inventing a function call when none fits. In `lpc-l2s50e-soup-100` (full test), the longest thinking quintile on `no-fit` lost **−11.5 pp**! Conversely, when DPO includes sufficient `no-fit` guard/right pairs (`rpe-80full`, `rpi-120full`, `michal-bida`), `no-fit` gains **+3.4 pp to +6.04 pp** at 46–50% saved, which alone contributes **+0.24 to +0.43 pp** to the 4-benchmark headline!
   - **Simple (400) & Multiple (200) calls:** At `B=0`, simple drops −10.8 pp and multiple drops −20.8 pp; they need ~60–100 thinking tokens (out of 171–192) to map arguments cleanly. In `lot B4` (25 Oct), cutting Hermes tool calls late in thinking (80–100%) crashed accuracy from 0.49 to **0.18** because cutting mid-argument-verification corrupts the JSON call, whereas early cuts (20–60%) were safe. On the full test, `simple` and `multiple` hold at **+0.1 to +0.4 pp** at 37–41% saved in `rpe-40/60/80full`.

## 3. Point-by-Point Design Review of the Budget Ladder

| Component | Verdict | Technical & Quantitative Reason | Exact Replacement / Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Per-type budget rungs (0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 65, 80, 90th pct + nat = 11 rungs)** | **CHANGE to 4 coarse rungs (Phase 0) / 2 stages (Phase 1)** | 11 rungs is far finer than Qwen3-0.6B's resolution. `zbench` showed position/budget test AUC = 0.508, and `lpt6` showed length control is 95% rank-1 across types. Worse, testing 10 cut rungs with 8 samples creates catastrophic multiple-testing stopping bias and multiplies rollout cost by ~6×. | For measurement, use **4 coarse rungs per type**: `0, p25, p50, p75, natural` (e.g., Math: `0, 150, 300, 450, nat`; Code: `0, 300, 600, 950, nat`; IFEval: `0, 100, 220, 350, nat`; Tools: `0, 50, 100, 150, nat`). For training, replace 7–10 bottom-up rungs with **2 stages** (Stage 1: Compact+Guard; Stage 2: On-Policy Calibrate+Repair). |
| **2. Level definition `b*(q)` with `1/8` slack (`p_rung >= p0 - 1/8`)** | **DROP `1/8` slack; CHANGE to split-sample monotone suffix rule** | With `n=8` samples, `1/8` slack is **−12.5 pp**—12.5× larger than your −1.0 pp target! Exact binomial calculation shows that if a question has true `p_nat = 0.80` and true `p_early = 0.60` (**−20.0 pp loss**), `k_r >= k_0 - 1` passes a single rung **47.3%** of the time and passes ≥1 of 4 early rungs **81.1%** of the time! In Phase 0, scoring the oracle on the same 8 samples inflates accuracy by **+6 to +8 pp**. | Use your proven 26 Oct `benchlot`/`lot V2` **half-split + monotone suffix rule**: split the 8 rollouts into Selection (4) and Confirmation (4). On Selection (4), `b*(q)` is the lowest rung `r` such that **for all `r' >= r`**, `k_sel(r') >= k_sel(nat)` (**0 slack**, monotone suffix so noise at an early rung cannot jump over a failing middle rung). Report Phase 0 oracle accuracy and tokens **only on the held-out Confirmation (4) answers** at `b*(q)`. |
| **3. Forced cuts vs natural stops (`</think>` inserted at sentence end ≤ B)** | **DROP forced cuts for DPO training; KEEP only for Phase 0 diagnostic** | An answer cut by force at token `B` has a prefix written at a 600-token leisurely pace followed by an abrupt `</think>`. Training DPO on forced cuts (`stopB..B5`, `stopV2/V3`) teaches a global `</think>` hazard boost that either stalls at 10–16% (`stopV3`) or causes code/math cliffs (`stopB3` soup 30: −8.4 pp code). Furthermore, if Stage 1 (`B=0`) teaches the model to emit `</think>` at token 0, the current model in Stage 2 will never generate the longer thinking traces needed to cut at `B_2`! | **Never train DPO on forced-cut traces.** Train only on **complete, naturally closed answers** (`</think>` emitted by the model itself) from `8–10` rollouts per question. Natural short answers already have compact reasoning density from token 1. |
| **4. Three pair kinds & their shares (Compaction, Anchor, Guard)** | **CHANGE definitions & fix exact per-type shares** | In `stopV2`, `D2 hard`, `D3 route`, and `D8 math`, "don't stop too early" pairs where a longer right answer beats a shorter wrong answer (`b*(q)` right > `b*(q)-1` wrong) directly rewarded length and **cancelled shortening** (`stopV2`: −15.8% saved; `D2`: 13.8%; `D3`: 9.4%). Conversely, pure equal-length right>wrong (`rpE`, `rpG`) without length anchors drifted longer over 80–120 steps (57.4% → 47.0% → 32.7%). | Use **three natural-rollout pair kinds** with explicit length constraints: <br>1. **Shorten (Anchor):** on questions solved `>= 6/8` (never on IFEval counting rules), shortest right vs longest right `>= 1.35x` thinking length.<br>2. **Equal-Length Re-Read Right (`rpE`-style Compaction):** on questions with mixed outcomes (`2/8` to `7/8`), right answer vs finished wrong answer within **±20% thinking length** (median chosen ≤ median rejected length per part!), preferring chosen traces that re-read/verify the question.<br>3. **Anti-Early-Stop Guard (Capped Length):** on questions where a wrong answer stopped early (`thinking < 0.5x` base right median), pick a right answer whose thinking is **≤ 1.15× the question's median right thinking** (never the longest right!).<br>**Shares:** Stage 1 (from raw): **45% Shorten / 40% Equal-Length Right / 15% Guard** (for `no-fit` and `math`: **30% Shorten / 55% Equal-Length Right / 15% Guard**). Stage 2 (on-policy calibration): **25% Shorten / 60% Equal-Length Re-Read Right / 15% Capped Guard**. |
| **5. Question mix per stage (60% edge `2-6/8`, 30% lower-level, 10% out-of-reach; drop never-solved)** | **CHANGE to 50% mixed (`2-6/8`) + 50% high-solve (`7-8/8`); KEEP dropping `0/8`** | Dropping `0/8` (never-solved) is supported by `wronglen` (24 Oct, L1422), which showed shortening spills over automatically to `0/8` questions (+59% cut in `rc1-rl-40`). However, `10% out-of-reach (0-1/8 at B_k)` yields noisy/lucky right answers, and adaptive re-weighting between stages by accuracy gap over-allocates math pairs, which `lpt6` and `mf1` proved spills over globally and lengthens all types. | Keep **type shares fixed** to representative pool proportions (25% math, 25% code, 25% IFEval incl. ≥50% counting rules, 25% BFCL with **40% `no-fit` / 35% `simple` / 25% `multiple`**). Within each type, draw **50% sometimes-solved (`2/8` to `6/8`)** for Equal-Length Right + Guard pairs and **50% reliably-solved (`7/8` to `8/8`)** for Shorten pairs. |
| **6. Hyperparameters: steps, lr, LoRA rank, beta, `reference = previous stage`** | **CHANGE reference to fixed base (or ≤2 stages max); KEEP `fp32+fp16`, `beta=0.1`, `NLL=0`** | Chaining `reference = previous stage` across 7 stages allows unbounded cumulative drift (`sum KL`), which destroyed accuracy in rounds 3–5 (`Track 2e/2f`) and caused severe length regression in `rpG`/`rpH`. Also, 20–40 steps at `lr=1e-5` is too weak to move raw Qwen (`rpA` at 40 steps moved DPO loss 0.693 → 0.667 and changed saving by 0%). Meanwhile, `bidax6p` proved `fp32 weights + fp16 autocast` with `NLL=0` is the single biggest stabilizer (+3 pp accuracy over `bf16`). | Limit training to **at most 2 stages** (Stage 1: ~160–200 steps of batch 16 = 2,560–3,200 pairs from raw Qwen, `lr = 1.5e-5` cosine to `5e-6`, `LoRA r16 alpha 32` or `r32 alpha 64`, `beta = 0.1`, `NLL = 0`, `fp32+fp16`; Stage 2: 60–80 steps on fresh on-policy rollouts from Stage 1 merged model, `lr = 1e-5` constant, `reference = Stage 1 merged`). Never chain 7 sequential references. |
| **7. Tracking panel (300 q × 8 × 11 rungs every stage) & Exit check / Rollback** | **CHANGE panel to 1-pass natural generation on 512-q dev set; DROP retry loops** | Re-answering 300 questions × 8 samples × 11 rungs = **26,400 generations after every stage** (~2.5–3 GPU-hours per stage just for the panel!). Worse, with 50 questions per type × 8 samples, the per-type 95% CI half-width is **±4.5 to ±6.5 pp**, so requiring "accuracy at B_k rose AND lower-level thinking within +10%" will fail by sampling noise on ~50% of stages, triggering endless 2-batch retry loops and adaptive cherry-picking. | Use a **single 512-question held-out dev set** (from own clean data: 140 SVAMP-like math, 100 KodCode, 132 IFEval-matched, 140 ToolACE/xLAM tools), evaluated with **standard uncapped test-time generation × 8 samples** (4,096 generations = ~25 min on vLLM). Use **paired seed-matched comparisons** against the raw model on this 512-q dev set. Fix the stage count in advance (no retry loops). |
| **8. Online (current model) vs Offline (original model) answers** | **KEEP Online for Stage 2; Offline for Stage 1** | Stage 1 *starts* from the original model, so online and offline are identical in Stage 1 (saving ~15 GPU-hours by reusing your existing 8–10 answer clean pool!). For Stage 2, your 10 Nov controlled comparison (`rpI` vs `rpG`/`rpH` at steps 80→120) proved decisively that **online answers from the current model prevent length drift** (`rpI` stayed at 43.0% saved / +0.90 pp on 720, +1.64 vs curve on full test, whereas `rpG` and `rpH` on old answers drifted back to 32.7% and 37.0% saved). | Reuse stored raw-Qwen rollouts for Stage 1; generate fresh x8 rollouts from the Stage 1 model for Stage 2. Run the offline-vs-online control only on Stage 2 (where they actually diverge). |

IFEval handling (`--count-rules`)** | **KEEP and strengthen `--count-rules`** | On 10 Nov (`ir-full/ifeval_rules.json`), counting rules (`number_words`, `keywords:frequency`, `number_sentences`, `highlighted_sections`, `placeholders`) caused 100% of IFEval's −2.6 pp full-test loss in `rpE-80`. | Keep `--count-rules` (chosen response length ≥ rejected response length AND chosen thinking ≤ 1.2× rejected thinking; zero shorten pairs on counting-rule prompts) AND add a hard floor: for any IFEval pair, **never pick a chosen answer whose thinking is `< 80` tokens** if the prompt has ≥2 constraints or a counting rule (to prevent the 626–815 early-stop errors seen in `ir-full`). |

## 4. One-Week Strategy on Two Colab L4 GPUs (Sequence, Exact Settings, Expected Ranges, Decision Rules)

You have ~14 GPU-days (~336 raw GPU-hours, or ~220–250 usable GPU-hours after Colab reconnects/Drive syncs). Do **not** spend 150+ GPU-hours running 7 stages × 11 rungs of forced cuts. Run the following 4-step sequence, where every step has a hard quantitative gate.

### Step 0: Build the Locked Non-Benchmark Dev Set (512 q) + Confirmation Set (512 q) & Run Bias-Corrected Phase 0 (~6 GPU-hours total; Day 1 morning)
- **Should you run Phase 0 first?** **Yes, but ONLY with the 4+4 split-sample fix and 4 coarse rungs (`0, p25, p50, p75, nat`)**—because it takes only ~2.5 GPU-hours and will immediately prove to the whole team that (a) the unsplit `1/8`-slack oracle is inflated by +6 to +8 pp, and (b) forced cuts on raw Qwen SVAMP cannot reach the ~58% math saving needed for a >50% headline without compacting the reasoning itself.
- **Exact Setup:**
  1. **Dev-512 & Confirm-512 (never touched by training, zero overlap with the 2,759 test):** Draw from `own_repair_questions()` (`pool-dp13` minus MetaMath + `gsm8k_std` + `nofit1/2` + `tools1` + `code1`, 8-gram & 13-word decontaminated against all 2,759 test questions): per set, **140 SVAMP-worded GSM8K/GSM-IC/Plus, 100 KodCode (MBPP-like slice), 132 IFEval-default (matched instruction distribution, ≥50% counting rules), 140 BFCL-clean tools (60 simple, 35 multiple, 45 verified `no-fit`)**. Score `full-base` (raw Qwen) × 8 on both (~50 min total across 2 GPUs).
  2. **Bias-Corrected Phase 0 (1,500 own training questions × 8):** On the 8 uncapped rollouts, split into **Selection (4)** and **Confirmation (4)**. Test 4 coarse cut rungs (`0, p25, p50, p75`) + `natural`. Compute both the *unsplit `1/8`-slack oracle* and the *split-sample `0`-slack monotone-suffix oracle*, plus the Spearman rank correlation `rho` between `b*(q)` and prompt length, and compare against the **natural shortest-of-8 oracle** on the same questions.
- **Expected Result (Range):**
  - Unsplit `1/8`-slack forced-cut oracle: **58–66% saved / −0.5 to +3.0 pp** (artifact of stopping-time bias).
  - **Split-sample (4+4) `0`-slack forced-cut oracle:** **44–50% total saved / −0.5 to −1.5 pp** (SVAMP 38–45% saved, MBPP+ 65–72%, IFEval 28–33% total saved, BFCL 40–45% saved), and prompt-length rank correlation **`|rho| < 0.20`**.
  - **Split-sample natural shortest-right oracle (on questions with ≥2 right):** **32–42% saved in 1 round at 0.0 pp loss**, confirming that multi-round natural compaction is needed rather than forced truncation.
- **Decision Rule:**
  - If the **split-sample forced-cut oracle** saves `< 55%` total tokens at `>= -0.5 pp` OR `|rho| < 0.30` (expected: both fail), **drop forced-cut ladder training immediately** and proceed to **Experiment 1 (Coarse 2-Stage Natural Budget Ladder vs 2-Round On-Policy Compaction)** below.

### Step 1 (Main Bet — GPU 1 & GPU 2 in Parallel): 2-Stage Natural Compaction + On-Policy Calibration from Raw Qwen (~45 GPU-hours; Day 1 afternoon – Day 3)
To satisfy **Constraint 1 (one clean, reproducible single training line from raw Qwen)**, **Constraint 2 (no competitor data or recipe)**, and **Constraint 3 (own answers only)** while combining the two things that actually worked in your log—on-policy multi-round shortening (`cleanA` / `odpo`) and equal-length re-read + `--count-rules` + `no-fit` calibration (`rpE` / `rpI`)—run two clean arms from raw Qwen (2 seeds each, `s0` on GPU 1, `s1` on GPU 2):

- **Shared Clean Own Data Pool (~14,000 own questions, zero MetaMath/bida files):** `gsm8k_std` + `gsm_ic` + `gsm_plus` (math), `kodcode` (code), `ifeval_default` matched (instructions, ≥1,500 prompts), `toolace_clean` + `nofit1/2` (tools, with 35–40% `no-fit`).
- **Stage 1 (Base Compaction + Budget Guard, from Raw Qwen, 8,000 questions × 8 stored own answers):**
  - **Pair rules (all natural complete answers, `</think>` emitted naturally; 3,200 pairs = 200 steps × 16):**
    - **Quotas by type:** SVAMP-math **800** (25%), KodCode **800** (25%), IFEval **800** (25%, of which ≥350 counting-rule prompts), Tools **800** (25%: 280 simple, 200 multiple, **320 `no-fit`**).
    - **Pair mix within Math & `No-Fit`:** **35% Shorten Anchor** (on questions solved `>= 6/8`: shortest right > longest right `>= 1.4x` thinking) + **50% Equal-Length Re-Read Right** (on questions solved `2/8–6/8`: right > finished wrong within **±20% thinking length**, chosen prefers question re-read) + **15% Capped Anti-Early-Stop Guard** (right with thinking `<= 1.15x` median right > wrong that stopped early `< 0.5x` median right).

## 5. Traps in Evaluation, Data, and Method — and the Better Route You Are Missing

### 5.1 Eight Concrete Traps Identified in Your Log & Codebase
1. **Trap 1 — The Phase 0 In-Sample Stopping Mirage (+6 to +8 pp Accuracy Inflation):**
   In `LADDER_DESIGN_FOR_REVIEW.md` §5–6, `b*(q)` is defined as the lowest of 10 rungs where `k_rung >= k_0 - 1` on 8 samples, and the Phase 0 "oracle curve" reports tokens and accuracy at `b*(q)` on those **same** 8 samples. By binomial order statistics, testing up to 10 rungs with 1/8 slack guarantees that even if early rungs have a true −20 pp accuracy loss, 81.1% of questions stop at a lucky early rung, and reporting the in-sample accuracy of that lucky rung hides the entire loss (+6 to +8 pp optimism bias). **Fix:** Always use a 4+4 split-sample monotone-suffix rule (`k_sel(r') >= k_sel(nat)` for all `r' >= r`, 0 slack) and report oracle accuracy exclusively on the held-out 4 confirmation samples.
2. **Trap 2 — The Stage 1 → Stage 2 Rollout Deadlock in Bottom-Up Cut Training:**
   If Stage 1 trains the model at `B_0 = 0` (`<think></think>`) until it exits `<think>` immediately on low-level questions, then at Stage 2 (`B_1 = p5`) and Stage 3 (`B_2 = p10`), when the current model generates uncapped thinking to be cut at `B_k`, it will close `</think>` early on its own! You cannot discover whether a question benefits from `B_k` tokens by cutting a model that has already been trained to stop at `B_{k-1}`.
3. **Trap 3 — Total Output Tokens vs Thinking Tokens on IFEval (The 62% Ceiling):**
   Your ladder budgets count *thinking* tokens only, but the official benchmark scores *thinking + final response* tokens. Because IFEval final responses are ~247 tokens (38% of total IFEval tokens) and *must not shrink* (or counting rules lose −11 to −13 pp), cutting IFEval thinking by 65% yields only **40.3% total saving on IFEval** (`rpE-50`). If you budget SVAMP and MBPP+ for only 50% thinking savings, your 4-benchmark geometric mean will land at **~44–45% total saved**, missing the >50% bar. Math and Code must save **56–60%** to offset IFEval and BFCL's ~41% ceiling.
4. **Trap 4 — Sequential Reference Drift (`reference = previous stage` across 7 stages):**
   In DPO, the KL penalty `beta * KL(pi || pi_ref)` only constrains the step from `pi_{k-1}` to `pi_k`. Chaining 7 stages (`pi_0 -> pi_1 -> ... -> pi_7`) allows total log-probability drift to grow linearly with stage count, causing later stages to overwrite earlier stages (`stopV3` Block 2b overwriting Block 2a, or `rpG` undoing 25 points of `rc1`'s saving). **Fix:** Never exceed 2 chained reference hops from raw Qwen, and include 25–30% Shorten Anchor replay in Stage 2.
5. **Trap 5 — Hidden Length Signals in "Guard" and "Correctness" Pairs:**
   Your 10 Nov `copdanalysis` proved that on raw Qwen's training answers, wrong answers are **1.6× to 2.0× longer** than right answers (SVAMP right 880 vs wrong 1,773 tokens, wrong longer on 72% of questions; MBPP+ 1,449 vs 2,271, 62%). Conversely, in `stopV2` and `mf1`, "longest right > shortest wrong" guard pairs had chosen answers 1.5–2.0× longer than rejected answers, which made the model think **15–24% longer** across all types! **Fix:** Every correctness pair (`Equal-Length Right`) must enforce `|len(chosen) - len(rejected)| <= 0.20 * len(rejected)` AND verify that `median(len(chosen)) <= median(len(rejected))` per data type before training starts.
6. **Trap 6 — Rank-1 Cross-Type Spill-Over (`lpt6` Cosine 0.991):**
   On Qwen3-0.6B, DPO length pressure on *any* data type spills over globally (`ptsolo-math` shortened MBPP+ by 51%; `ptsolo-tools` shortened MBPP+ by 59% and SVAMP by 53%). Therefore, dynamically giving more questions in later ladder stages to "types further from the original's accuracy" (which is always Math) will either over-shorten or over-lengthen Code and Tools simultaneously. **Fix:** Keep type quotas strictly fixed across stages.
7. **Trap 7 — GPU Non-Determinism (~1.2–3.5 pp) and Open Code Bugs (`consult5` Audit):**
   Your `lpc-bidax2` vs `lpc-bidax3` (identical pairs, identical seed 0, pure GPU non-determinism) differed by **2.7% saving and 1.2 pp accuracy** (51.8% / −2.7 vs 54.5% / −3.9), and `lpc-runa2` vs `lpc-runa2r` differed by **7.9% saving** at step 200. Single-seed checkpoints cannot be trusted; **averaging 2 seeds in weight space (`merge.py` 0.5/0.5)** is a single clean LoRA adapter that cuts variance in half. Also fix the two still-open bugs from the 10 Nov audit before training: (a) `dpo.py` `ref.json` cache fingerprint must hash full token IDs of chosen+rejected (not just prefixes + lengths), and (b) log explicit SHA/path identities of `rollout_model`, `init_model`, and `ref_model` in every run header.
8. **Trap 8 — Upstream Submission Requirement (Commit `965a54c`, 6 Nov):**
   BottleCapAI's 6 Nov upstream commit requires every submission to include the coding agent's traces and conversation with the harness as a text document, and forbids any recipe built on another competitor's data/pipeline. Ensure your final 2-stage script runs end-to-end from clean own data (`own_repair_questions` pool) with a clean audit trail.

### RANK 3 (Best Realization of the Team's Budget-Allocation Intuition — Estimated Win Probability: 25–35%):
**Difficulty-Indexed Natural Budget DPO + Short On-Policy Guard (`Natural Budget Ladder`)**

#### 1. Why It Can Win (Quantitative Evidence from Your Log)
- **What `D4 lpd-budget` proved:** In your 12-way DPO pair screen on 5 Nov (L4628), **D4 `lpd-budget`**—which replaced the flat `1.5x` shorten threshold with a **solve-rate-indexed per-question length budget** (so `8/8` easy questions are pushed to very short natural answers, `5–6/8` medium questions to moderate natural answers, and `<4/8` hard questions get no shortening)—was the **#1 best single-pass run in the entire D-series**, scoring **49.0% saved / −1.42 pp (+1.29 vs curve)** with **MBPP+ at 43% saved / −0.2 pp** and **BFCL multiple at +1.7 pp**.
- **Why `D4` beat the bottom-up forced-cut ladder before you even built it:** `D4` achieves the exact mathematical objective of Section 4 (`spend tokens where the marginal accuracy gain is highest, indexed by p0(q)`) using **natural complete rollouts** in **one training pass** anchored to the raw model, completely avoiding forced sentence cuts, 10-rung stopping bias, and 7-stage reference drift!
- **Why `D4` missed by 1.0% saving and 0.42 pp accuracy:** `D4` used only 4 rollouts per question (so the shortest right answer of 4 on `8/8` questions was only at the ~20th percentile, not the ~10th percentile available in 8–10 rollouts), lacked the **question re-read filter** on math (`SVAMP −2.9 pp`), and lacked **`--count-rules`** on IFEval (`IFEval −1.9 pp`).

#### 2. Full Reproducible Recipe Design
- **Data Pool:** 10,000 clean own questions answered × 8 by raw Qwen (reuse stored 8–10 answer clean pool).
- **Natural Budget Level Assignment (Zero Forced Cuts):** For each question `q` with solve rate `p0(q) = k/8` (`k >= 2`), let `L_med(q)` be the median thinking length of its right answers:
  - **Tier 1 — Easy (`k in {7, 8}`):** Target budget = **shortest natural right answer** `c*`, provided `len(r*) >= 1.25 * len(c*)` (where `r*` is the longest right answer). Produces a **Tier-1 Shorten Pair** (`c* > r*`) AND a **Tier-1 Guard Pair** (`c* > longest wrong`, if `k=7`).
  - **Tier 2 — Medium (`k in {5, 6}`):** Target budget = **second-shortest natural right answer** `c_2` (protects against lucky shortcuts on trap math!), paired against `r*` if `len(r*) >= 1.50 * len(c_2)` (**Tier-2 Shorten Pair**), plus an **Equal-Length Re-Read Right Pair** (`right > finished wrong` within ±20% length, chosen re-reads question).
  - **Tier 3 — Hard (`k in {2, 3, 4}`):** **0% Shorten Pairs.** Only **Equal-Length Re-Read Right Pairs** (±20% length, chosen re-reads question) and **Capped Anti-Early-Stop Guard Pairs** (right with `len <= 1.15 * L_med(q)` > wrong with `len < 0.50 * L_med(q)`).
