

RESEARCH MODE: YOU MAY ALSO TRAIN. Besides editing weights, you may run preference training (DPO) with the project's own training code, choosing everything from a fixed menu, with a budget of {TRAIN_HOURS} GPU-hours of training in total (used: {TRAIN_USED}). Your goal is the objective above: the final model (a trained one, an edited one, or a trained one you then edit) must think more than 50% fewer tokens than the RAW model at less than 1 point of accuracy lost against the RAW model.

What you can read first, in {DATA}:
- test_card.md: the test you will be scored on (composition, protocol, scoring, rules), without its questions;
- history/history.jsonl: every training run the project already made, one line per scored checkpoint: the run's exact settings line, the training step, and its result per test part (tokens saved %, accuracy change in points) on a 720-question screen of the test. history/README.md explains the fields. Use it to learn what each setting does before spending your budget;
- v1_training/: how v1 (today's best) was made: its recipe, its pairs and its checkpoints.

The TRAIN tool. Reply with a ```forward block (it counts as one forward pass AND uses training hours):
 {"type": "train", "name": "<short new name>", "start": "raw" or "v1", "recipe": one of {RECIPES},
  "pairs": {pair options, any of: {PAIR_KEYS}},
  "dpo": {training options, any of: {DPO_KEYS}}}
It builds the pairs from the RAW model's own answers to the practice questions with the recipe and options you chose,
trains a LoRA adapter with DPO (michal-bida's exact settings unless you change them), and replies with the pair counts
by kind and part, the training log (loss and the share of pairs ordered right, every few steps) and the hours used. One
pass over the pairs takes "target_steps" steps (at most 339; about 3 GPU-hours at 339, about 1 hour at 100). The model
is then available as "trained:<name>" in every forward pass, and its checkpoints as "trained:<name>:step-N" (every 10
steps). To compare two models on the same practice problems with the same seeds, use a generation pass with
 {"type": "generate", "compare": ["<model a>", "<model b>"], "problems": [...], "n": 1}
where a model is "original", "edited", "checkpoint:step-N" (v1's training), "trained:<name>" or "trained:<name>:step-N";
it returns both models' accuracy, thinking and cut share by type and the paired difference b - a.
Keep in mind: the test counts the four benchmarks equally; screen on enough problems of every type (the standard error
of an accuracy difference is about 50/sqrt(n) points); a trained model that saves more but loses accuracy is a failure.
A training request that fails (bad option, budget exceeded) does not count.
